# Real-to-Sim and Proxy-Based Evaluation of Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies (as of 2026-10-01)

Scope of this note: works that evaluate policies trained on real data WITHOUT running the real robot, and that validate the proxy against paired real-world results (sim-vs-real correlation). Pure sim benchmarks with no real-world correlation study (LIBERO, CALVIN, RLBench, Meta-World, plain RoboCasa, etc.) are out of scope; they are mentioned only as contrast where a paper measured their (lack of) predictivity. Everything below was checked by opening the primary source unless explicitly flagged "unverified" or "secondary summary only".

Metric glossary used throughout:
- Pearson r: linear correlation between proxy and real success rates (across policies, checkpoints, or task-conditions).
- MMRV (Mean Maximum Rank Violation, introduced by SIMPLER): for each policy, the largest real-world performance margin by which the proxy mis-ranks it against another policy, averaged over policies; range [0,1], lower is better. Definition quoted from the SIMPLER paper: "the rank violation between two policies ... weighs the significance of the simulator incorrectly ranking the items by the corresponding margin in real-world performance" — [SIMPLER arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- Spearman ρ / Kendall τ / R²: rank correlation / coefficient of determination, used by some papers instead of or in addition to the above.

## Master catalog (요약표): which works exist, what category, what real setup, what agreement number

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 there are roughly 40 works that claim to evaluate real-robot policies by proxy; about 30 of them report a quantitative sim/proxy-vs-real agreement number. The field splits into three families — (A) physics-simulator digital twins (hand-built or reconstructed), (B) 3D-Gaussian-Splatting / video-to-asset reconstruction pipelines, and (C) action-conditioned video world models — with reported Pearson r typically 0.78–0.99 on the paper's own setup, but with recurring caveats (small policy sets, single embodiments, VLM/human judging, overestimation/underestimation biases).

### Inferences
- The highest reported correlations (r ≥ 0.95) come from either very small evaluation sets (DreamDojo: one policy, 20 scenes; R2S-Eval: 7 tasks on one robot) or from settings where real numbers are taken from an existing leaderboard (RoboWorld, MolmoSpaces vs RoboArena), so cross-paper comparison of r values is not apples-to-apples.
- Three near-simultaneous 2026 studies (Practical Recipe, SimFoundry, ReVeal) independently converge on the same two levers: (i) rendering/geometry fidelity matters more than task alignment, and (ii) a small amount of sim adaptation (≈10 demos/task or 10% sim co-training for hundreds of steps) raises agreement, while more adaptation overfits and lowers it.

### Gaps
- No single study yet evaluates the same policy set with simulator twins AND video world models AND real robots across multiple embodiments; cross-family comparisons exist only as baselines inside individual papers (PolaRiS vs Ctrl-World; SimFoundry vs PolaRiS; Practical Recipe across three simulators).

## Key Question 1: Simulator-based real-to-sim evaluation (SIMPLER and successors, REALM, RobotArena ∞, PolaRiS-class, Isaac-Lab suites, DROID sim evals, RoboTwin, BEHAVIOR, 2025–2026 additions)

### Takeaway
SIMPLER (CoRL 2024) established the methodology (visual matching + system identification + MMRV/Pearson on ~1,500 paired episodes), and the 2025–2026 wave moved to the DROID Franka embodiment (REALM, PolaRiS, RoboLab, sim-evals, MolmoSpaces, Practical Recipe) where RoboArena supplies ground-truth real rankings; reported Pearson r on DROID-class setups ranges from 0.68 (RoboLab) to 0.98 (PolaRiS vs RoboArena), while RobotArena ∞, RoboDojo and RoboTwin 2.0 do not report sim-real correlation.

### Cited Findings

#### SIMPLER / SimplerEnv — "Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation"
- Authors: Xuanlin Li, Kyle Hsu, Jiayuan Gu, Karl Pertsch, Oier Mees, Homer Rich Walke, Chuyuan Fu, Ishikaa Lunawat, Isabel Sieh, Sean Kirmani, Sergey Levine, Jiajun Wu, Chelsea Finn, Hao Su, Quan Vuong, Ted Xiao (UCSD, UC Berkeley, Stanford, Google DeepMind). arXiv 2405.05941 (May 2024); CoRL 2024 (PMLR v270). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05941); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/li25c.html); [project](https://simpler-env.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/simpler-env/SimplerEnv)
- Approach: hand-built SAPIEN environments for Google Robot and WidowX+Bridge; two protocols — Visual Matching (green-screen real background + projected textures) and Variant Aggregation (average over lighting/texture/distractor variants); system identification of controller dynamics from real trajectories. — [project page](https://simpler-env.github.io/)
- Policies: RT-1 (Converged / 15% / Begin checkpoints), RT-1-X, RT-2-X, Octo-Base, Octo-Small. ~1,500 paired sim-real episodes across two embodiments. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- Google Robot results (Table I): Visual Matching — Pick Coke Can MMRV 0.031 / r 0.976; Move Near MMRV 0.111 / r 0.855; Drawer MMRV 0.027 / r 0.942; average MMRV 0.056 / r 0.924. Variant Aggregation — average MMRV 0.143 / r 0.778 (Drawer r only 0.486). Baseline "validation MSE" proxy — MMRV 0.375 / r 0.308. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- Control gap: system identification reduced control loss from 0.267–0.432 to 0.131 and improved MMRV from 0.070–0.100 to 0.031; perturbing the identified parameters degraded correlation. Visual gap: green-screen + matched drawer/robot textures gave MMRV 0.050 vs 0.087 without. Arm-texture sensitivity: Octo-Base success varied 0%→29.3% with robot arm texture while RT-1-X was robust; a real "wrapped arm" test confirmed the sim-predicted sensitivity. Physical parameter robustness: mass/friction/joint variations changed success ≤15%. Isaac Sim gave comparable correlation to SAPIEN. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- Stated limitations (quoted): rigid-object tasks only; "green-screening approach is limited to fixed cameras"; "pipeline involves some manual effort in curating assets and assembling scenes." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- External criticism (AutoEval, arXiv 2503.24278): "Different policies may suffer differently from the remaining sim-to-real gap in SIMPLER evaluations. As a result, SIMPLER's effectiveness is policy dependent and it cannot provide a reliable policy evaluation." Example: Open-π0 on "put eggplant in sink" scored very poorly in SIMPLER but high in the real world; SIMPLER matched well on "open drawer". AutoEval itself reached r 0.942 / MMRV 0.015 vs human-run real evals. — [AutoEval arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278)
- External re-measurement (Practical Recipe, 2026, DROID embodiment, aligned tasks): SIMPLER ρ 0.400, r 0.402, MMRV 0.128; SIMPLER omits behavior perturbations; its language perturbations had r 0.086 with real. — [arXiv HTML 2606.10366](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10366)
- External observation (RobotArena ∞): all tested VLAs scored "substantially higher ... on SIMPLER than on BridgeSim", suggesting SIMPLER's small environment set may overestimate performance. — [arXiv HTML 2510.23571](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571)

#### ManiSkill3 digital twins (GPU port of SIMPLER)
- ManiSkill3 "ports over and GPU parallelizes some environments from SIMPLER"; implements 4 SIMPLER environments as evaluation digital twins; evaluates Octo at 60–100x real-time, "approximately 10x faster than the original digital twin implementations in SIMPLER"; Bridge V2 digital twins documented. No new sim-real correlation study. — [ManiSkill3 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00425); [ManiSkill docs: Digital Twins](https://maniskill.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tasks/digital_twins/)

#### DROID sim-evals (precursor to PolaRiS)
- GitHub `arhanjain/sim-evals`: Isaac Sim evaluation scenes for DROID; "tuned to work zero-shot with DROID policies trained on the real-world DROID dataset, so no separate simulation data is required"; supports π0-FAST-DROID joint-position policies (not joint-velocity); references `pi05_droid_jointpos_polaris` configs. No published correlation numbers. — [GitHub](https://github.com/arhanjain/sim-evals)

#### REALM — "A Real-to-Sim Validated Benchmark for Generalization in Robotic Manipulation"
- Authors: Martin Sedlacek, Pavlo Yefanov, Georgy Ponimatkin, Jai Bardhan, Simon Pilc, Mederic Fourmy, Evangelos Kazakos, Cees Snoek, Josef Sivic, Vladimir Petrik (CIIRC CTU Prague; FEE CTU; U. Amsterdam). arXiv 2512.19562 (Dec 2025); project page states IEEE RA-L with presentation at IROS 2026. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19562); [project](https://martin-sedlacek.com/realm/); [GitHub](https://github.com/martin-sedlacek/REALM)
- Setup: Isaac Sim; mirrors DROID Franka; 7 skills (pick, put, push, rotate, stack, open, close), 15 perturbation factors (6 visual, 8 semantic, 7 behavioral incl. combos), 3,500–4,000+ objects, 10 scenes. Control alignment: CMA-ES system identification of 14 joint friction/armature parameters minimizing L2 joint-position error on replayed trajectories. Visual check: attention-map cosine similarity 0.85/1.0 between real and sim frames (~280 frames). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19562v1); [project](https://martin-sedlacek.com/realm/)
- Validation: π0, π0-FAST, GR00T N1.5; "nearly 800 pairs of real and simulated rollouts"; "p-value of p<0.001 for the correlation between real and simulated rollouts for all settings"; "correlation is consistently high ... with a linear trend close to the identity line and largely consistent relative policy rankings"; validation done in "a digital cousin of a previously unseen scene." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19562v1)
- Findings: behavioral generalization (unseen objects) hardest; semantic perturbations surprisingly harmful; camera-viewpoint sensitivity across all models; absolute success rates low. Limitation: DROID embodiment only. — [project](https://martin-sedlacek.com/realm/)
- Independent re-measurement (Practical Recipe): REALM zero-shot ρ 0.700 / r 0.785 / MMRV 0.030; after 10-demo-per-task sim tuning ρ 0.875 / MMRV 0.015; REALM was the only simulator preserving the real-world perturbation hierarchy (behavior > layout > vision ≈ language). — [arXiv HTML 2606.10366](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10366)

#### PolaRiS — "Scalable Real-to-Sim Evaluations for Generalist Robot Policies"
- Authors: Arhan Jain*, Mingtong Zhang*, Kanav Arora, William Chen, Marcel Torne, Muhammad Zubair Irshad, Sergey Zakharov, Yue Wang, Sergey Levine, Chelsea Finn, Wei-Chiu Ma, Dhruv Shah, Abhishek Gupta, Karl Pertsch (UW, Princeton, UC Berkeley, Stanford, TRI, USC, Cornell, Physical Intelligence). arXiv 2512.16881 (v2 Dec 30 2025). Venue not stated. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881); [project](https://polaris-evals.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/arhanjain/PolaRiS/tree/main)
- Approach: 2–5 min phone video -> 2D Gaussian Splatting -> TSDF/mesh for collision + splats for rendering; Gaussian primitives anchored to robot links (enables wrist cameras, which "SIMPLER/prior real-to-sim methods cannot evaluate"); TRELLIS for object assets; GUI composition to USD; Isaac Sim physics. Lightweight sim co-training: 10% sim demos + 90% real pretraining data for ~1,000 steps. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- Scale: 6 paired real-to-sim environments across two institutions, 15 extra co-training environments, ~350 sim demos; 20 real rollouts and 50 sim rollouts per policy-task pair. Policies: π0, π0-FAST, PaliGemma-binning, π0.5. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881); [project](https://polaris-evals.github.io/)
- Results: average Pearson r 0.90 across unseen environments, worst-case 0.81; r 0.98 against RoboArena rankings. Baselines: validation MSE poorly correlated; LIBERO score ~90–95% for all policies, correlation with real near zero; video-model evaluation (Ctrl-World) hallucinated during object interaction, MMRV 0.22. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- Ablations: GS rendering > ray-traced > untextured (co-training partially compensates); out-of-distribution co-training data works as well as in-domain (zero-shot new environments); correlation strong after hundreds of steps, degrades beyond ~1k steps (overfitting). Limitations: no non-rigid/soft-body/complex contact; positioned as complement to real evaluation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- Counter-measurement: SimFoundry (NVIDIA, Jun 2026) reports PolaRiS at Pearson 0.32 when used WITHOUT policy adaptation on SimFoundry's task set, vs SimFoundry's 0.911 — note this is PolaRiS without its co-training step. — [SimFoundry arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28276v3)

#### RobotArena ∞ — "Scalable Robot Benchmarking via Real-to-Sim Translation"
- Authors: Yash Jangir, Yidi Zhang, Pang-Chi Lo, Kashu Yamazaki, Chenyu Zhang, Kuan-Hsun Tu, Tsung-Wei Ke, Lei Ke, Yonatan Bisk, Katerina Fragkiadaki (CMU, NTU). arXiv 2510.23571 (v3 Mar 2026); ICLR 2026. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571); [ICLR proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/2aa3da3c1463ee2cdaaee94be4f8ba3f-Paper-Conference.pdf); [project](https://robotarenainf.github.io); [GitHub](https://github.com/offjangir/RobotArena)
- Pipeline from a single static-camera RGB frame: DR-Robot differentiable robot rendering for camera calibration, Hunyuan-3D meshes, Gemini for scene understanding, MoGE depth, MINIMA correspondence, LaMa inpainting, Genesis simulator with simulated-annealing system ID. Arenas: BridgeSim (~70 envs), DROIDSim, RH20TSim; ~100 nominal environments + hundreds of perturbations (ΔBG, ΔColor, ΔObjPose). Scoring: Gemini 2.5 Pro task-progress on last 30% frames + 8,749 crowdsourced pairwise preferences -> Bradley-Terry/Elo. Policies: Octo, RoboVLM, SpatialVLA, CogAct, X-VLA-0.9B, π0. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571)
- Real validation: one task ("Put the carrot in the plate"), three policies: "RoboVLM and SpatialVLA succeeded in both real and simulated settings, while Octo failed in both." No Pearson/MMRV. Authors: "Estimation of correlations between policy rankings in RobotArena ∞ and in the real world would require multi-task evaluations ... a task very difficult to execute accurately, free of human biases." Limitations: no wrist-camera policies; physics struggles with fine contact (e.g., inserting chargers). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571)

#### RoboLab — "A High-Fidelity Simulation Benchmark for Analysis of Task Generalist Policies" (NVIDIA)
- Authors: Xuning Yang, Alex Zook, Hugo Hadfield, Ankit Goyal, Stan Birchfield, Jonathan Tremblay, Rishit Dagli, Fabio Ramos. arXiv 2604.09860 (v3 May 2026). IsaacLab; mirrors DROID (Franka, Robotiq, ZED). 120 hand-curated pick-and-place tasks (3 difficulty levels; visual/procedural/relational axes). Policies: π0.5, π0-FAST, π0, PaliGemma, GR00T N1.6 (DROID fine-tunes). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09860v3); [project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/srl/projects/robolab/); [GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/RoboLab/)
- Real validation against RoboArena: Spearman ρ 1.00, Pearson r 0.68 (4 policies). SOTA models achieve only 5–28% on RoboLab-120; strong dependence on wrist-camera calibration; residual visual shift acknowledged. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09860v3)

#### X2Real (X Square Robot)
- arXiv 2609.27449 (Sep 23 2026). Built on NVIDIA Isaac Lab-Arena ("Mana" ecosystem) with a physical DSL; 44 long-horizon tasks across 10 capability dimensions; embodiments Franka, ArtiXon Arm-6A dual-arm, ARX R5, Quanta X1 mobile. Hardware-aligned calibration with "millimeter-level" replay deviation; strict train/eval separation (green-booth setup adapted from ManipArena). Faithfulness evidence: linear correlation 0.84 between sim and real scores for real-data-only models; number of real episodes not specified in the report. Strongest policy fell 53.9% ID -> 34.3% OOD. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27449v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/X-Square-Robot/x2real)

#### VISER — "Toward Visually Realistic Simulation: A Benchmark for Evaluating Robot Manipulation in Simulation"
- Authors: Yixin Zhu, Zixiong Wang, Jian Yang, Jin Xie, Jingyi Yu, Jiayuan Gu, Beibei Wang (Nanjing U., Nankai U., ShanghaiTech). arXiv 2605.06311 (May 2026). SAPIEN with ray tracing (~20 FPS on RTX 4090), >1,000 PBR assets (319 categories); WidowX and Google Robot; 14 curated + 8 reconstructed real tasks + procedural tasks; policies Octo, OpenVLA, X-VLA. Paired rollouts in reconstructed Bridge V2 scenes: average Pearson r 0.92. Findings: specular highlights improved pot-geometry task 10%→90%; soft shadows improved spoon-on-towel 12%→49%; hard shadows drove success to near zero. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06311)

#### MolmoSpaces (AI2)
- arXiv 2602.11337 (Feb 2026); 230k environments, 130k assets, simulator-agnostic (MuJoCo, Isaac, ManiSkill). Sim-to-real: MolmoSpaces-Bench pick/open/close on Franka FR3 DROID rig; real numbers taken from RoboArena and CAP; pick task (752 RoboArena episodes): "Pearson R² ≈ 0.96" per HTML extraction (abstract phrasing: "Pearson R = 0.96"), Spearman ρ 0.98; open/close positively correlated but with large error bars. Policies: π0, π0-FAST, π0.5, CAP. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11337); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11337v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/allenai/molmospaces)

#### Genie Sim 3.0 (AgiBot)
- arXiv 2601.02078 (Jan 2026; launched CES 2026). Isaac Sim + 3DGS scene scanning (MetaCam LiDAR, gsplat, PGSR meshes); AgiBot G1/G2. Sim-vs-real: 8 tasks × 50 trials per configuration; policies π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.6; reports R² 0.94 with slope ≈1.025 between sim and real success; 2×2 design (sim/real training × sim/real eval): sim-to-real avg 0.83 vs real-to-real 0.75. Limits: contact dynamics, camera-calibration and end-effector changes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02078v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/AgibotTech/genie_sim); [press](https://www.therobotreport.com/agibot-introduces-genie-sim-3-0-simulation-platform-embodied-ai/)

#### THE COLOSSEUM (RSS 2024) and Colosseum V2 (2026)
- COLOSSEUM: 20 tasks, 14 perturbation axes; "simulation results are correlated (R̄² = 0.614) to similar perturbations in real-world experiments"; camera-pose perturbations R² 0.46–0.52, while background texture/distractor/table color/light color/object color-texture-size R² 0.74–0.94; 3D-printable real replicas released. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191); [RSS PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p133.pdf)
- Colosseum V2 (USC et al., arXiv 2605.27759 v4, accepted Aug 2026): ManiSkill GPU sim; Franka single/bimanual; ACT and π0.5; real: 5 tasks × 7 perturbations × 20 trials; R² 0.49 across 35 task-perturbation pairs; fit "SRsim = 0.86·SRreal + 15.1"; mean |Δ| 12.3 pp. Rigid-body only. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27759); [project](https://colosseum-v2.github.io/)

#### H2RBench (CoRL 2026)
- Xiao, Zhan, Krishna, Meng, Irshad, Zakharov, Held. arXiv 2609.24778. Real-to-sim benchmark for human-to-robot transfer methods (4 tasks from real human videos); "simulation performance is broadly predictive of real-world robot performance": Pearson r 0.89, Spearman ρ 0.85, MMRV 0.06. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24778v1)

#### R2S-Eval (Physical Intelligence, Sharpa, NJU, Tongji, Tsinghua)
- arXiv 2609.03276 (Sep 2026). Calibrated Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab-Arena twin of a SharpaNorth dual-arm humanoid (63 DoF, dexterous hands); 7 tabletop tasks; candidate policies adapted on sim data via action replay / planning / teleop before closed-loop eval; VLM evaluator produces pairwise preferences -> rankings. Six VLAs: π0, π0.5, OpenVLA, NORA-Long, SmolVLA, X-VLA. 3,525 real trajectories, 3,445 sim (97.8% action-replay success). Agreement: VLM–human 91.9% (real-to-sim), Spearman 0.957, Pearson 0.978, MMRV 0.005; mean real-sim success gap 2.13 pp. Residual gaps in contact-rich manipulation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03276); [project](https://r2s-eval.github.io)

#### Isaac Lab-Arena / GR00T sim evaluation (NVIDIA)
- NVIDIA blog states Isaac Lab-Arena integrates with GR00T N post-training/inference and that "NVIDIA is partnering with benchmark authors to build evaluations on Isaac Lab-Arena and publish sim-to-real validated evaluation methods, tasks, and datasets" — a stated plan, not a reported correlation. Isaac Lab-Arena was benchmarked against MuJoCo on RoboCasa tasks with GR00T N1.5 (speed, not real correlation). — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/simplify-generalist-robot-policy-evaluation-in-simulation-with-nvidia-isaac-lab-arena/)
- Works that DO use Isaac Lab-Arena with real validation: R2S-Eval, X2Real (above).

#### Works checked that do NOT report sim-real evaluation correlation
- RoboDojo (arXiv 2607.04434, Jul 2026; 42 sim + 18 real tasks, 30 policies, Isaac Sim/Isaac Lab via MagicSim; ARX X5 / Piper): "We do not construct one-to-one aligned task pairs between simulation and the real world. The real-world benchmark is not intended to measure direct sim-to-real transfer on matched tasks." No Pearson/Spearman/MMRV. Notable: π0.5 ranks 1st in real but 3rd in sim. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434); [site](https://robodojo-benchmark.com)
- RoboTwin 2.0 (arXiv 2506.18088): data generator + sim benchmark with domain randomization; real experiments show training gains (+13.5% to +33.0% with 1,000 synthetic trajectories on COBOT-Magic), not proxy-evaluation correlation. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18088); [project](https://robotwin-platform.github.io/)
- BEHAVIOR-1K (arXiv 2403.09227): built "a digital twin of a real-world mock apartment" and evaluated policies in sim and real with a mobile manipulator to analyze gap sources (grasping, perception noise, actuation inaccuracies); qualitative, no correlation statistic. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09227)
- TRI "A Careful Examination of Large Behavior Models" (arXiv 2507.05331): Drake-based `lbm_eval` modeled after a real station; ~47k sim and ~1,800 real rollouts; two "seen" tasks had real success exceeding sim; "Simulation and real-world evaluations under distribution shifts are not directly comparable." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1)

### Inferences
- The DROID Franka rig plus RoboArena has become the de-facto ground-truth source for 2026 sim-evaluation papers (PolaRiS, RoboLab, MolmoSpaces, RoboWorld), which makes those correlation numbers partially dependent on RoboArena's own sampling and on the same small set of π-family policies.
- Hand-built twins without co-training appear to plateau around r 0.7–0.8 on DROID-class tasks (RoboLab 0.68, REALM 0.785 zero-shot), whereas reconstruction+light adaptation or calibrated twins reach 0.9+ — consistent with the Practical Recipe's finding that fidelity and a little adaptation dominate.

### Gaps
- SIMPLER's WidowX/Bridge per-task MMRV/r values are in appendix tables (Tables IV–V) that I could not extract from the HTML/PDF; only Google Robot numbers are verified here.
- REALM's own numeric Pearson/MMRV values are rendered in figures (Fig. 6) and could not be extracted as text; only p<0.001 and the ~800-pair scale are quoted. The r 0.785 / MMRV 0.030 figures are from the independent Practical Recipe study, not REALM's paper.
- No published correlation numbers for the DROID `sim-evals` repo or for any official GR00T/Isaac-Lab evaluation suite.
- MolmoSpaces: ambiguity whether the 0.96 figure is Pearson R or R²; the abstract says "Pearson R = 0.96", the HTML extraction said "R² ≈ 0.96".

## Key Question 2: Reconstruction-based (3DGS / NeRF / photogrammetry / video-to-asset) evaluation pipelines — which report policy-evaluation correlation vs. training-only use

### Takeaway
Among Gaussian-splatting pipelines, PolaRiS, the Columbia soft-body real2sim-eval, SimFoundry, ReVeal, Genie Sim 3.0 and (qualitatively) Real-is-Sim and GSWorld report paired sim-vs-real policy evaluation, while SplatSim, RL-GSBridge, Re3Sim and RoboGSim are primarily training/transfer tools whose papers (as far as retrieved) do not report policy-evaluation correlation.

### Cited Findings

#### Real-to-Sim Robot Policy Evaluation with Gaussian Splatting Simulation of Soft-Body Interactions (ICRA 2026)
- Authors: Kaifeng Zhang, Shuo Sha, Hanxiao Jiang, Matthew Loper, Hyunjong Song, Guangyan Cai, Zhuo Xu, Xiaochen Hu, Changxi Zheng, Yunzhu Li (Columbia University, SceniX Inc., Google DeepMind). arXiv 2511.04665 (Nov 2025); venue ICRA 2026 per project page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04665); [project](https://real2sim-eval.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/kywind/real2sim-eval); HF collection `shashuo0104/real-to-sim-policy-eval`
- Approach: 3DGS rendering + PhysTwin soft-body digital twins (dense spring-mass systems optimized from interaction video) on NVIDIA Warp extended with robot-object collision; physics parameters optimized to match real behavior. Robot: UFactory xArm 7 with RealSense D405 (wrist) + D455 (fixed). Tasks: plush-toy packing, rope routing, T-block pushing. Policies: ACT, Diffusion Policy, π0, SmolVLA across multiple checkpoints; evaluation sets of 20 / 27 / 16 episodes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04665)
- Results: Pearson r > 0.9 for all tasks — toy packing 0.944 (w/o physics opt 0.694; w/o color alignment 0.805), rope routing 0.901 (0.832 / 0.714; IsaacLab 0.237), T-push 0.915 (0.905 / 0.529; IsaacLab 0.649); MMRV 0.076–0.174 for the full method. Policies were trained exclusively on real data (no co-training). Limitations: manual scene segmentation and periodic re-scanning; tabletop only; absolute success rates sometimes diverge despite high correlation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04665)

#### PolaRiS (2DGS) — see Key Question 1 for full details (r 0.90 avg; wrist-camera support via link-anchored splats). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)

#### SimFoundry — "Modular and Automated Scene Generation for Policy Learning and Evaluation" (NVIDIA, GaTech, Stanford, UT Austin, U. Toronto)
- Authors incl. Nadun Ranawaka, Josiah Wong, ..., Li Fei-Fei, Linxi Fan, Bowen Wen, Ajay Mandlekar, Yuke Zhu. arXiv 2606.28276 (v3 Jun 25 2026). Input: single real video -> extraction / generation (mesh, pose, articulation) / augmentation ("digital cousins") -> interactive sim-ready twin. Robots: DROID Franka and bimanual YAM. Policies: π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.6/N1.7, DreamZero. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28276v3); [project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/simfoundry/)
- Evaluation agreement: mean Pearson 0.911, MMRV 0.018 WITHOUT policy adaptation; sub-task-level scoring raised correlation 0.90→0.95; reports PolaRiS baseline at 0.32 under the same no-adaptation protocol. Limitations: inherits foundation-model failure modes; planar tabletop assumption; transparent objects. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28276v3)

#### ReVeal — "A Reconstruction-Aware Real-to-Sim Framework for VLA Policy Evaluation" (Purdue; Samsung SDS Research America)
- Authors: Xinyi Wang, Heng Hao, Wenjun Hu, Anna Enyu Li, Dizhi Ma, Karthik Ramani, Hankyu Moon, Yeong-Dae Kwon. arXiv 2609.23910 (Sep 2026). Compares 2DGS, PGSR, and depth-guided PGSR-D (Depth Anything V2) reconstructions in the Genesis simulator; Dexmate Vega humanoid; 8 tasks (vision-only and physical-interaction, single and bimanual); GR00T N1.7, SmolVLA, π0.5 fine-tuned on 200–300 episodes; 50 real episodes per task-policy-pipeline (576 evaluations). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23910)
- Real-sim agreement by reconstruction pipeline: 2DGS r 0.773 / MMRV 0.235 / MAE 0.246; PGSR 0.850 / 0.205 / 0.152; PGSR-D 0.960 / 0.102 / 0.050. Reconstruction metrics most associated with agreement: DINOv2 feature similarity (r 0.795) and planar completeness (r 0.759) — PSNR is a weaker predictor. Limitations: background reconstruction focus; physics gaps (mass/friction) not studied; single platform. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23910)

#### GSWorld — "Closed-Loop Photo-Realistic Simulation Suite for Robotic Manipulation" (UCLA, UCSD, Meta)
- Authors: Guangqi Jiang, Haoran Chang, Ri-Zhao Qiu, Yutong Liang, Mazeyu Ji, Jiyue Zhu, Zhao Dong, Xueyan Zou, Xiaolong Wang. arXiv 2510.20813 (Oct 2025). GSDF asset format (Gaussian-on-Mesh + URDF) wrapped around ManiSkill3 physics; robots FR3+UMI gripper, xArm6, Galaxea R1; ArUco markers for metric scale. Application (3): "reproducible benchmarking of real-robot manipulation policies in simulation." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20813); [project](https://3dgsworld.github.io)
- Sim-vs-real benchmarking: ACT and π0; 10 real trials per policy per task, 25 sim seeds; Table I e.g. Place Box ACT 50% real / 44% sim, π0 60% / 52%; Pour Sauce ACT 40% / 28%, π0 60% / 40%; Stack Cans ACT 50% / 42%; Arrange Cans π0 60% / 50%. Claims "strong correlation" but no Pearson/MMRV reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20813)

#### Real-is-Sim (Robotics and AI Institute; QUT)
- Authors: Jad Abou-Chakra, Lingfeng Sun, Krishan Rana, Brandon May, Karl Schmeckpeper, Maria Vittoria Minniti, Laura Herlant. arXiv 2504.03597 (Apr 2025); an "ICRA 2026" PDF is hosted by RAI (venue inferred from filename, flag). Embodied Gaussians digital twin continuously corrected from 3 RealSense D455 cameras at 30 Hz; the real robot tracks the simulated robot's joints, so the policy always acts in sim. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03597v1); [RAI PDF](https://rai-inst.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/realissim_paper_icra_2026-1.pdf); [project](https://realissim.rai-inst.com)
- Evaluation evidence: PushT only; four checkpoints (10k/15k/25k/50k) evaluated offline in sim (minutes) vs real (hours) show "strong correlation between evaluation curves"; no Pearson value. Limitations: validated only on a kinematic task; requires precise object geometry/Gaussian init. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03597v1)

#### Genie Sim 3.0 (3DGS scanned scenes) — see Key Question 1 (R² 0.94). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02078v2)

#### 3DGS pipelines that are training/transfer-oriented (no evaluation-correlation study found)
- SplatSim (arXiv 2409.10161): zero-shot sim2real of RGB policies; 86% real success without real training data. — [awesomepapers listing of arXiv 2409.10161](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2409.10161)
- RL-GSBridge (arXiv 2409.20291; IEEE 2025): 3DGS in RL pipeline for zero-shot transfer of grasping/pick-place. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20291v1)
- Re3Sim: "integrates Gaussian splatting with physics simulation to construct learnable, photorealistic digital twins for policy learning" (training). — [search summary via RL-GSBridge/SplatSim results](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/rl-gsbridge-3d-gaussian-splatting-based-real2sim2real-method-for-robotic-manipulation-learning)
- RoboGSim (arXiv 2411.11839): "Real2Sim2Real simulator for both high-fidelity demonstration synthesis and physics-consistent closed-loop evaluation"; reports texture/physics consistency and that sim-data policies sometimes beat real-data policies on the real robot, but no sim-vs-real policy-ranking correlation statistic was found. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.11839); [project](https://robogsim.github.io/)

### Inferences
- Across reconstruction pipelines the recurring differentiator for evaluation reliability is geometry/mesh completeness on task-relevant planar surfaces and view-level feature similarity (ReVeal), plus physics-parameter fitting for deformables (Columbia soft-body ablation: physics optimization adds +0.07 to +0.25 in r).
- Reconstruction pipelines are the only family so far with validated deformable-object evaluation (rope, plush, T-push), an area physics-twin and world-model papers list as a limitation.

### Gaps
- DISCOVERSE and "Scalable Real2Sim" (physics-aware asset generation via pick-and-place) were not opened in this session; based on titles/prior knowledge they are asset/data pipelines rather than evaluation studies — unverified here.
- No reconstruction-based study yet reports agreement for mobile manipulation or for transparent/reflective objects (SimFoundry and ReVeal both list these as open).

## Key Question 3: World-model-based evaluation (WorldEval, WorldGym, Ctrl-World, Veo, 1X, DreamDojo, IRASim, GE-Sim/EWMBench, UniSim, and 2025–2026 additions) — correlation with real results and failure modes

### Takeaway
Action-conditioned video world models now report real-vs-proxy Pearson r from 0.78 (WorldGym) to 0.99 (RoboWorld, DreamDojo) on their own setups, but every paper that discusses failure modes lists the same ones: hallucinated object interactions under contact, drift over long horizons, systematic bias (Ctrl-World/Veo underestimate; DreamDojo/Interactive World Simulator/WorldArena-tested models overestimate), and dependence on VLM or human judges whose own accuracy is 65–92%.

### Cited Findings

#### WorldEval — "World Model as Real-World Robot Policies Evaluator" (Midea Group; East China Normal U.)
- Authors: Yaxuan Li, Yichen Zhu, Junjie Wen, Chaomin Shen, Yi Xu. arXiv 2505.19017 (May 2025). Policy2Vec injects the policy's latent action embedding into Wan 2.1 (14B I2V DiT) via LoRA. Robot: bimanual AgileX (ALOHA-style), top RealSense 457, 14-D action at 50 Hz. Tasks: Bussing Table, Collect Toy (novel), Place Cup, Handover Block, Strike Block. Policies: Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA, DexVLA, π0. Judge: Gemini-2.0 video QA. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19017); [project](https://worldeval.github.io)
- Agreement: WorldEval MMRV 0.044 / Pearson 0.942 vs a real-to-sim (physics) baseline MMRV 0.261 / r 0.411 on the same tasks. Action-conditioning ablation: VQVAE MMRV 0.292 / r −0.862; one-hot 0.416 / −0.333; Policy2Vec 0.192 / 0.939. Also used as checkpoint ranker and "safety detector." Limitations: action-only conditioning; artifacts (object deformation, hallucinations, arm ghosting); dependence on pre-trained generator. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19017)

#### WorldGym / "Evaluating Robot Policies in a World Model" (Stanford, NYU, Google DeepMind)
- Authors: Julian Quevedo, Ansh Kumar Sharma, Yixiang Sun, Varad Suryavanshi, Percy Liang, Sherry Yang. arXiv 2506.00613 (v3 Sep 2025); listed as ICLR 2026 by a secondary source (unverified). Autoregressive action-conditioned video model trained on 9 Open-X datasets (Bridge V2, RT-1, ...), 256×256, 20-frame context; GPT-4o as reward (TPR 0.81, TNR 0.97 on RT-1 validation). — [project abstract](https://world-model-eval.github.io/abstract); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00613); [GitHub](https://github.com/world-model-eval/world-model-eval); [secondary: papernotes ICLR 2026](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/robotics/worldgym_world_model_as_an_environment_for_policy_evaluation/)
- Agreement: Pearson r 0.78 across the 17 OpenVLA Bridge evaluation tasks for RT-1-X, Octo, OpenVLA; mean success-rate difference 3.3%. Important caveat: real-world numbers were "taken directly from [Kim et al.]" (OpenVLA paper) — no new real rollouts. OOD probes via image editing (adversarial laptop showing a carrot image fooled OpenVLA in 15% of trials; shape discrimination near chance). Limitation: "generating highly realistic object interaction remains challenging." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00613)

#### Ctrl-World — "A Controllable Generative World Model for Robot Manipulation" (Stanford; Tsinghua) — ICLR 2026
- Authors: Yanjiang Guo, Lucy Xiaoyang Shi, Jianyu Chen, Chelsea Finn. arXiv 2510.10125 (v3 Mar 2026). Stable Video Diffusion adapted with multi-view (2 third-person + wrist) joint prediction, frame-level pose conditioning, pose-conditioned memory retrieval; trained on DROID (95,599 trajectories, 564 scenes, ~76k success / 19k failure); 2×8 H100 for 2–3 days. Policies: π0, π0-FAST, π0.5; 7 tasks (pick-place, towel folding, drawer, wipe table, close laptop, pull tissue, stack); human-judged. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125); [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf); [project](https://ctrl-world.github.io)
- Agreement (instruction-following rate, real vs world model for π0.5): pick-place 0.90 vs 0.80; fold towel 0.80 vs 0.75; drawer 0.80 vs 0.65. Authors: model "tends to underestimate the execution success rate" and struggles with "complex physics dynamics such as collisions, objects sliding away, rotations"; "can fail on tasks involving precise interactions or long-horizon reasoning"; "sensitive to initial observations." Policy improvement via imagined data: +44.7% instruction following (e.g., spatial 28.75%→87.5%). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125)
- Third-party measurements: PolaRiS reports Ctrl-World MMRV 0.22 with hallucinations during object interaction ([PolaRiS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)); WorldArena (ICML 2026) finds Ctrl-World correlates strongly with RoboTwin-simulator ground truth while Cosmos-Predict 2.5 is weaker, and both overestimate success ([WorldArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08971)).

#### Evaluating Gemini Robotics Policies in a Veo World Simulator (Google DeepMind, Gemini Robotics Team)
- arXiv 2512.10675 (Dec 11 2025; 20 alphabetical authors). Veo 2 fine-tuned on robotics data into an action-conditioned simulator generating four consistent camera views for ALOHA 2; Gemini 2.5 Flash image editing to create OOD scenes (backgrounds, distractors, objects) and hazards for "predictive red teaming." Policies: eight Gemini Robotics ("GROD") checkpoints predicting 1-s action chunks at 50 Hz; 5 bimanual tasks; 1,600+ real trials; human scoring of 8-s rollouts. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v1); [project](https://veo-robotics.github.io/)
- Agreement: nominal — paper text says "strong linear correlation" with Pearson/MMRV shown in Fig. 4 over 80 scene-instruction combinations; secondary summaries quote Pearson 0.88 / MMRV 0.03 (secondary summary only — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.10675)). OOD — "strong linear correlation (Pearson = 0.86)" and "MMRV of 0.06" for Policy A across generalization axes. Bias: "absolute values of predicted success rates are lower than their real counterparts." Limitations: contact-rich interactions with small objects; Fig. 11 hallucination where "a novel object appears spontaneously"; 1-minute+ horizons incomplete; human scoring required. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v1)

#### 1X World Model (1X Technologies, blog June 16 2025) and 1X World Model Challenge
- Blog: latent world model predicting future frames and task success for NEO; used for checkpoint selection and architecture comparison; statistical claim: "a world model with 70% accuracy can predict the superior policy with 90% success when true performance gaps reach 15%"; real-world evaluation run on most/least promising checkpoints to validate; fails on held-out objects (e.g., unfamiliar plants). No per-task Pearson published. — [1X blog](https://www.1x.tech/discover/redwood-ai-world-model); [1X PDF "Evaluating Bits, not Atoms"](https://www.1x.tech/1x-world-model.pdf)
- 1X World Model Challenge is NOT a policy-evaluation benchmark: two tracks — sampling (PSNR of predicted 77th frame given 17 past frames + states) and compression (Top-500 CE on Cosmos 8×8×8 tokens); winner Team Revontuli 23.0 dB / CE 6.6386 (arXiv 2510.07092). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07092)

#### DreamDojo — "A Generalist Robot World Model from Large-Scale Human Videos" (NVIDIA + HKUST, Berkeley, KAIST, UofT, UCSD, UW, Stanford, UT Austin)
- arXiv 2602.06949 (Feb 2026). Cosmos-Predict2.5 pretrained on 44,711 h egocentric human video (EgoDex, DreamDojo-HV); robots GR-1, Unitree G1, AgiBot, YAM. Policy-evaluation experiment: AgiBot fruit packing, 20 scenes, ~80-s rollouts, single-view state-free GR00T N1.5 variant, human scoring (5 fruits = 100%): Pearson r 0.995, MMRV 0.003. Limitations: "generated success rates often exceed real-world performance"; uncommon fast actions; no native multi-view. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06949v1); [project](https://dreamdojo-world.github.io)

#### PlayWorld — "Learning Robot World Models from Autonomous Play" (Princeton)
- Tenny Yin, ..., Anirudha Majumdar. arXiv 2603.09030 (v3 Apr 2026). DROID platform; 30 h of autonomous VLM-tasked play data; curriculum over contact difficulty. Policy evaluation: 18 policies (DP from scratch, π0 fine-tunes of varying data quality), 20 real rollouts each, 50 WM rollouts: Pearson 0.8766; human-demo-trained WM baseline "collapsed to hallucinated success." Authors: PlayWorld "does not eliminate hallucinations entirely." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09030); [project](https://robot-playworld.github.io/)

#### Interactive World Simulator for Robot Policy Training and Evaluation (Columbia, TRI, Amazon, UIUC)
- Yixuan Wang, ..., Jose Barreiros, ..., Yunzhu Li. arXiv 2603.08546 (Mar 2026). Consistency-model latent dynamics; 10+ min stable rollouts at 15 FPS on one RTX 4090; ALOHA bimanual; six real tasks (mug grasp, rope routing/collecting, T-push, box packing, pile sweeping); policies DP, ACT, π0, π0.5; 20 paired initial configurations. Reports "strong correlation" via plots with a slight positive bias (simulator > real); no numeric r extracted. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08546); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08546)

#### dWorldEval — "Scalable Robotic Policy Evaluation via Discrete Diffusion World Model" (Current Robotics; U. Toronto)
- Yaxuan Li, Zhongyi Zhou, Yefei Chen, Yaokai Xue, Yichen Zhu. arXiv 2604.22152 (Apr 2026). Unified discrete tokens (MAGVIT-v2 / LLaDA / FAST) with sparse keyframe memory and a progress token for automatic success detection; ~17k trajectories incl. ~3k failures. Agreement: LIBERO multi-view r 0.910 / MMRV 0.013; RoboTwin heterogeneous policies r 0.927; real AgileX bimanual 5 tasks r 0.918; without keyframe memory r drops to 0.786. 30–90 s per trajectory on an H800. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22152v1); [project](https://dworldeval.github.io/)

#### PiL-World — "A Chunk-Wise World Model for VLA Policy-in-the-Loop Evaluation" (Tongji; Midea AIRC)
- arXiv 2606.05773 (Jun 2026). Alternates policy inference and observation generation; trained on successes and failures; dual-arm; π0 checkpoints; tasks sort cubes / stack bowls / stack blocks. Reduces success-rate gap vs real from 63.2% (Ctrl-World baseline) to 12.0%; Pearson 0.94 across task-checkpoint settings; hallucination-free ratio 41.5%→70.1% (smaller gains on contact-rich stacking). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05773)

#### SC3-Eval — "Evaluating Robot Foundation Models via Self-Consistent Video Generation" (NVIDIA et al.)
- Wei-Cheng Tseng, Gashon Hussein, Yuzhu Dong, Allen Z. Ren, Lucy X. Shi, XuDong Wang, Sergey Levine, Zhaoshuo Li, Jinwei Gu, Florian Shkurti, Ming-Yu Liu, Quan Vuong. arXiv 2606.18610 (Jun 2026). Cosmos3-Nano fine-tuned with forward-inverse dynamics consistency, cross-view inpainting consistency, and test-time inverse-dynamics drift detection; 381 h table-bussing data; 32 GB200 GPUs, 2.2 days. Seven π0.5 checkpoints, 36–37 matched real trajectories each; blind human scoring. Closed-loop Pearson 0.929 / MMRV 0.119 vs best baseline Cosmos-Predict 2.5 at 0.897 / 0.090 (baselines also Ctrl-World, IRASim); reproduces specific real failure categories; holds on OOD "reverse bussing." Limits: 2.3 s per chunk; ~20-s episodes; single workspace. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18610); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18610v3); [project](https://weichengtseng.github.io/sc3-eval/)

#### Scalable Policy Evaluation with Video World Models (NVIDIA Research, U. Toronto, Vector Institute)
- Wei-Cheng Tseng, Jinwei Gu, Qinsheng Zhang, Hanzi Mao, Ming-Yu Liu, Florian Shkurti, Lin Yen-Chen. arXiv 2511.11520 (Nov–Dec 2025). Cosmos-Predict2-Video2World-2B with Fourier-feature action conditioning; RoboMimic (sim ground truth) and Bridge V2 real tasks (carrot, pot, eggplant, cup) with Octo-Small/Base and OpenVLA. RoboMimic agreement: Lift r 0.879 / MMRV 0.015; Can 0.860 / 0.141; Square 0.847 / 0.165; Tool Hang 0.833 / 0.217. Failure cases: object disappearance/appearance during gripper contact, multi-view inconsistency, static "replicated" predictions when gripper barely moves, VLM annotation accuracy only 65–80%; weaker correlation for long-horizon tasks. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11520); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11520v3)

#### RoboWorld — "Fast and Reliable Neural Simulators for Generalist Robot Policy Evaluation" (KAIST)
- Byeongguk Jeon, Seonghyeon Ye, JaeHyeok Doo, Sungdong Kim, Minjoon Seo, Hyungmok Son, Kimin Lee. arXiv 2607.01060 (v4 Jul 15 2026). Wan 2.1-T2V-1.3B made autoregressive with causal frame attention and per-frame action cross-attention; "Step Forcing" training enables 4-step denoising at 15.3 FPS for 300-frame rollouts; trained on DROID (3-view 2×2 grid). Evaluates 8 open RoboArena policies (π0.5, PaliGemma-FAST, ...) with 4,186 rollouts; task-progress VLM rubric (0–5). Agreement vs RoboArena real: Pearson 0.989, Spearman 0.970 (binary scoring ρ 0.922); whole RoboArena replicated in ~100 H100 GPU-hours. Failures: long-horizon object consistency under sustained contact; wrist view most prone to drift. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01060v4); [project](https://byeongguks.github.io/RoboWorld/)

#### Genie Envisioner (GE-Sim, EWMBench) and GE-Sim 2.0 (AgiBot et al.)
- Genie Envisioner (arXiv 2508.05635, Aug 2025): GE-Sim closed-loop action-conditioned video simulator "enabling closed-loop simulation and real-world-aligned evaluation of policy models"; EWMBench (arXiv 2505.09694) benchmarks the world models themselves (scene consistency, motion correctness, semantic alignment) with human-concordance analysis — it is not a policy-vs-real correlation study. — [GE arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05635v1); [EWMBench arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09694v1)
- GE-Sim 2.0 (arXiv 2605.27491, May 2026; AgiBot, BUAA, LV-NUS, TJU): dual-arm, three cameras; π0.5 closed-loop; "World Judge" per-frame success classifier (79% accuracy on WM rollouts, 87% on real video). Agreement reported as episode-level accuracy 0.81 and recall 0.82, trend slope ≈1 with small negative offset; no Pearson/MMRV. Failures: contact-sensitive tasks (pull plug, grasp-and-release), small visual signals (liquid level, flame), wrist-camera consistency. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27491); [project](https://ge-sim-v2.github.io/)

#### EnerVerse-AC (AgiBot, SJTU, CUHK)
- arXiv 2505.09723 (May 2025). Action-conditioned WM with multi-level action conditioning and ray-map multi-view; trained with failure trajectories; used as data engine and evaluator. Evidence: "EVAC and real-world evaluations showing the same performance trend in tasks like 'Take a Bottle'" across training — qualitative; no r. — [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2505.09723); [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=zIiV66Dimq)

#### IRASim (ICCV 2025)
- Trajectory-conditioned fine-grained video WM; policy-evaluation correlation of Pearson 0.99 is against the LIBERO SIMULATOR as ground truth, not real robots. — [ICCV 2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhu_IRASim_A_Fine-Grained_World_Model_for_Robot_Manipulation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)

#### GigaWorld-1 / WMBench (GigaAI; Tsinghua)
- arXiv 2607.02642 (Jul 2026). Systematic study of 7 video WMs and 4 action encodings over 324k simulated rollouts, with WMBench containing 2,989 paired real trajectories (humanoid, dual-arm, single-arm, dexterous hands). VLM evaluator: 87.80% exact / 99.16% adjacent agreement with humans (ρ 0.7574). Metric analysis: visual-fidelity group ρ 0.78 with outcome score; static-rewarding metrics anti-correlated (background consistency ρ −0.45); VLM-judged "interaction quality" ρ −0.11 ("current VLMs still cannot judge physical realism robustly"). Design findings: robotics-pretrained Cosmos-Predict2.5 beat generic SVD (0.6123 vs 0.5569); channel-concatenated control maps beat cross-attention (trajectory accuracy 0.3528 vs 0.1620); memory keeps PSNR 19.82 vs 14.46 over 0–8 s. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02642v1); [project](https://open-gigaai.github.io/giga-world-1/)

#### Others (brief)
- BWM (arXiv 2607.29302, Jul 2026): open-source action-conditioned WM; "policy evaluator for closed-loop assessment, risk anticipation, and policy ranking"; first in WorldArena Challenge; no correlation numbers in abstract. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29302)
- GeniWorld (arXiv 2608.06332, Aug 2026): URDF-rendered "visual actions"; "functions as a policy evaluator" — no numbers in abstract. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06332)
- Cosmos-Surg-dVRK (arXiv 2510.16240, Oct 2025): Cosmos WM + video classifier for surgical policies on dVRK; abstract claims strong sim/real alignment on suturing and "promising alignment" on porcine cholecystectomy; no numbers in abstract. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16240)
- WorldArena (ICML 2026; Tsinghua et al.): "policy evaluator" track uses RoboTwin simulator as ground truth; WorldArena 2.0 (arXiv 2605.17912) adds two real AgileX ALOHA tasks (pour water, wipe table). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08971); [WorldArena 2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17912v1)
- WorldSimProbe (arXiv 2608.09298, Aug 2026): "Observable Simulator Contract" diagnostics for 6 open ACWMs over 18k instances in RoboTwin/ManiSkill/LIBERO; finds "systematic action-realization degradation" and "structured failures in interaction grounding and dynamics." Sim ground truth only. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09298)
- UniSim ("Learning Interactive Real-World Simulators", arXiv 2310.06114): cited by WorldGym as a precursor; no real-vs-proxy policy correlation study found for UniSim itself. — [EWMBench/WorldGym references](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00613)

### Inferences
- The two highest world-model correlations (RoboWorld 0.989, DreamDojo 0.995) are each on a single embodiment/task family with ground truth from an existing leaderboard or a single policy; the more conservative multi-checkpoint, human-scored studies (SC3-Eval 0.929, PiL-World 0.94, Veo ~0.86–0.88, WorldGym 0.78) are probably a better estimate of current reliability.
- Bias direction is architecture/data dependent: models trained mostly on successful demos tend to "self-correct" and overestimate (DreamDojo, Interactive World Simulator, WorldArena finding), whereas models conditioned on fine-grained action sequences with weaker contact modeling underestimate (Ctrl-World, Veo). Papers that add failure data (PiL-World, EnerVerse-AC, dWorldEval ~3k failure rollouts) report smaller gaps.
- Judge quality is a hidden ceiling: VLM success judging ranges from 65–80% (Cosmos-based pipeline) through 79–87% (GE-Sim 2.0 World Judge) to 91.9% (R2S-Eval) — consistent with FailBench's finding of 0.77 best balanced accuracy and near-chance on contact-intensive assembly.

### Gaps
- Veo nominal Pearson 0.88 / MMRV 0.03 could not be confirmed from the paper's text (values are in Fig. 4); only the OOD numbers (0.86 / 0.06) are quoted verbatim.
- Bridge V2 real-task correlation numbers for "Scalable Policy Evaluation with Video World Models" were not extracted (only RoboMimic sim numbers).
- 1X publishes no per-task correlation data; the "70%/90%/15%" statement is a statistical illustration, not a measured dataset.
- No world-model paper reports agreement for mobile manipulation or for tasks longer than ~1 minute.

## Key Question 4: When is sim/proxy evaluation trustworthy? Visual gap vs. control/dynamics gap, texture/lighting sensitivity, system identification, and known cases where sim rankings disagreed with real rankings

### Takeaway
Evidence from SIMPLER, REALM, the 2026 "Practical Recipe" comparison, VISER, ReVeal and PolaRiS says that (1) both the control gap (fixable by system identification) and the visual gap (fixable by matched textures/lighting or GS rendering) must be closed, (2) rendering/geometry fidelity matters more than exact task alignment, and (3) a small dose of in-sim adaptation improves agreement while heavier adaptation overfits; documented disagreements include SIMPLER's policy-dependent errors (Open-π0 "eggplant in sink"), LIBERO's near-zero correlation, RoboDojo's π0.5 (1st real / 3rd sim), and world models' contact hallucinations.

### Cited Findings

#### Control/dynamics gap
- SIMPLER: system identification cut control loss 0.267–0.432 → 0.131 and MMRV 0.070–0.100 → 0.031; perturbing identified parameters degraded correlation; mass/friction/joint variation within plausible ranges moved success ≤15%. — [SIMPLER arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- REALM: CMA-ES over 14 joint friction/armature parameters on replayed trajectories; claims "high-fidelity simulation with aligned robot control can serve as a valuable proxy for real-world performance." — [REALM project](https://martin-sedlacek.com/realm/); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19562v1)
- X2Real: hardware calibration achieving "millimeter-level positional deviation" in trajectory replay before reporting 0.84 correlation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27449v1)
- Soft-body real2sim-eval: physics-parameter optimization raised r from 0.694→0.944 (toy packing) and 0.832→0.901 (rope). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04665)
- RobotArena ∞: physics engines "struggle with fine-grained contact dynamics (e.g., inserting chargers)." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571)

#### Visual gap: textures, lighting, shadows, rendering fidelity
- SIMPLER: green-screen + matched foreground textures MMRV 0.050 vs 0.087; Octo-Base success swung 0%→29.3% with robot-arm texture while RT-1-X was robust (confirmed on real robot with a wrapped arm) — i.e., visual sensitivity is policy-specific. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- VISER: specular highlights (pot geometry 10%→90%) and soft shadows (spoon-on-towel 12%→49%) are decisive; hard shadows collapse success to near zero. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06311)
- PolaRiS: GS rendering > ray tracing > untextured for correlation, with co-training partially compensating. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- ReVeal: agreement rises monotonically with reconstruction fidelity (2DGS r 0.773 → PGSR 0.850 → PGSR-D 0.960); DINOv2/SigLIP feature similarity predicts agreement better than PSNR; planar completeness of task surfaces matters (r 0.759). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23910)
- Soft-body real2sim-eval: removing color alignment dropped r 0.915→0.529 on T-push. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04665)
- NVIDIA perspective paper ("Robot Policy Evaluation for Sim-to-Real Transfer: A Benchmarking Perspective", arXiv 2508.11117; Yang, Eppner, Tremblay, Fox, Birchfield, Ramos): attributes 24–30% sim-to-real drops "largely to visual discrepancies rather than contact physics alone"; lighting/camera-pose changes degrade success 30–50%; ~200 high-quality scenes can beat ~10k procedural ones. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11117)
- COLOSSEUM: camera-pose perturbations are the least predictable in sim (R² 0.46–0.52) vs texture/color/lighting (0.74–0.94). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191)

#### Fidelity vs task alignment; adaptation dose
- Practical Recipe (Tsinghua / Shanghai Qi Zhi; 11,800 sim + 1,115 real rollouts; π0, π0-FAST, π0.5, GR00T N1.6/N1.7; DROID): Observation 2 — swapping objects (banana→corn/zucchini) changed absolute success but not policy order, so "proxy ability of a simulator is primarily governed by simulator fidelity rather than residual object-level task mismatch"; Observation 1 — "a reliable simulator should make policies fail like the real world" (perturbation hierarchy behavior > layout > vision ≈ language preserved only by REALM); Observations 3–4 — 10 demos/task tuning improved ρ 0.700→0.875 and sensitivity r 0.875→0.970, but 20 demos overfit to sim artifacts. VLA-Arena vision perturbations had ρ 0.000 with real. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10366)
- PolaRiS: correlation stable after hundreds of co-training steps, degrades beyond ~1k; OOD co-training data as good as in-domain. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- SimFoundry: without any adaptation, PolaRiS scored r 0.32 on SimFoundry's tasks vs SimFoundry 0.911 — illustrating how adaptation-dependent some pipelines are. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28276v3)
- Scoring granularity: RoboWorld task-progress rubric ρ 0.970 vs binary ρ 0.922; SimFoundry sub-task scoring 0.90→0.95. — [RoboWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01060v4); [SimFoundry](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28276v3)

#### Documented disagreements between proxy and real rankings
- SIMPLER is policy-dependent: Open-π0 "put eggplant in sink" very low in sim, high in real; AutoEval concludes SIMPLER "cannot provide a reliable policy evaluation" across tasks. — [AutoEval arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278)
- SIMPLER on DROID-aligned tasks: ρ 0.400, MMRV 0.128; language perturbation r 0.086. — [Practical Recipe](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10366)
- LIBERO: all four DROID policies ~90–95%, correlation with real near zero (PolaRiS). — [PolaRiS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881)
- SIMPLER Variant Aggregation on Drawer tasks: r 0.486 / MMRV 0.235 vs Visual Matching 0.942 / 0.027 — protocol choice alone flips reliability. — [SIMPLER](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941)
- RoboDojo: π0.5 1st in real-world evaluation but 3rd in simulation; "Strong performance in the Standard setting does not necessarily transfer to randomized scenes." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434)
- TRI LBM: two "seen" tasks had higher real than sim success (timeouts suspected). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1)
- Colosseum V2: R² only 0.49 with sim overpredicting (SRsim = 0.86·SRreal + 15.1), mean |Δ| 12.3 pp. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27759)
- RoboLab: perfect rank order (ρ 1.00) but r 0.68 — rankings can survive while absolute calibration fails. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09860v3)
- World models: Ctrl-World MMRV 0.22 with interaction hallucinations (PolaRiS); WorldArena found both Ctrl-World and Cosmos-Predict 2.5 "consistently overestimate success rates"; Veo underestimates; DreamDojo overestimates. — [PolaRiS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881); [WorldArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08971); [Veo](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v1); [DreamDojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06949v1)
- Single-view world models cause "severe partial observability and ... hallucinations, such as an object snapping into the gripper without prior physical contact"; adding wrist-camera prediction reduces this (statement surfaced in search results pointing to Ctrl-World ICLR 2026 / RoboWorld / PiL-World; exact source sentence not individually confirmed). — [search result set](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)

#### Judge reliability (shared by sim and world-model pipelines that use VLM scoring)
- FailBench (arXiv 2609.03611, Sep 2026): 2,197 manipulation attempts from 14 sources; best of 13 VLM detectors reaches 0.77 balanced accuracy; <0.60 on contact-intensive assembly; "systematic bias toward predicting success under ambiguous evidence"; cropping intervention +2.4 pp. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03611)
- GigaWorld-1: VLM-judged interaction quality ρ −0.11 with outcomes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02642v1)

#### Meta-level critiques
- Position paper (Nanjing U., arXiv 2606.15032): policy-ranking agreement appears in only ~12 of 40+ decision-focused world-model papers; exploitability gaps (optimizer finds model-predicted successes that fail in reality) "essentially unreported"; recommends closed-loop, full-horizon, policy-ranking and exploitability tests under policy-induced distribution shift. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)
- RobotArena ∞ authors on why they did not measure correlation: multi-task real evaluations "free of human biases" are "very difficult to execute accurately." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23571)

### Inferences
- Rank agreement (MMRV/Spearman) is systematically easier to achieve than calibration (Pearson/absolute success): RoboLab (ρ 1.00, r 0.68), Colosseum V2 (offset +15 pp), Veo/Ctrl-World (underestimate), DreamDojo (overestimate) all show rankings surviving while absolute numbers drift — so proxy evaluations should be reported as rankings with margins rather than as success-rate replacements.
- The documented failures cluster where contact dominates (insertion, charger plugging, assembly, stacking) and where the policy is unusually sensitive to appearance (Octo-Base arm texture, Open-π0 eggplant) — i.e., proxies are least trustworthy exactly for the policies/tasks where the visual-or-dynamics gap interacts with a brittle policy.
- Because 2026 papers on DROID increasingly validate against the same RoboArena leaderboard, a proxy that matches RoboArena may still be wrong about tasks/embodiments RoboArena does not cover; cross-embodiment evidence (R2S-Eval humanoid, ReVeal Vega, Genie Sim G1/G2, Veo ALOHA 2) is still single-lab.

### Gaps
- No controlled study isolates visual vs dynamics gap on the same tasks with modern VLAs other than SIMPLER's 2024 ablations (RT-1/Octo era) and the soft-body ablations (xArm, four policies); the "visual gap dominates" claim in the NVIDIA perspective paper is a literature synthesis, not a new measurement.
- No published list of systematic proxy-vs-real rank inversions across many policies; the inversions above are isolated anecdotes recovered from individual papers.
- Exploitability (policies optimized against the proxy) remains unmeasured for all simulators and world models surveyed, per the position paper.
