# Remote-Access / Hosted / Distributed / Crowdsourced Real-Robot Evaluation Platforms and Real-Robot Competitions for Manipulation (as of 2026-10)

Scope note: this file covers "evaluation-as-a-service" (policy is submitted/served and run on physical robots operated by someone else) plus recurring real-robot competitions. Locally reproduced kits (YCB, FMB, VLA-REPLICA, RoboPlayground), real-to-sim proxies (SIMPLER, RobotArena-∞, WorldEval, PolaRiS, RoboWorld) and pure statistics papers are out of scope and only cross-referenced. All facts below were checked by opening the cited source during this session unless explicitly marked "[unverified]" or "[aggregator]".

---

## KQ1. What hosted / remote / distributed real-robot evaluation platforms exist? (catalog of verified platforms)

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 there are roughly a dozen live or recently-live systems where an external party runs your policy on physical manipulators. They fall into four families: (a) crowdsourced/federated pairwise evaluation (RoboArena), (b) autonomous 24/7 cells with learned reset + success classifiers (AutoEval, HALTER), (c) centralized operator-run "remote-robot" fleets with fixed task suites and leaderboards (RoboChallenge/Table30, RoboDojo-RealEval, Humanoid Everyday cloud eval, PhAIL, ArmnetBench, UniBot, UMI Arena), and (d) hybrid distributed-execution/central-verification infrastructures that ship standardized kits and cryptographically verify locally recorded runs (ManipulationNet). Older cloud-robotics predecessors (Real Robot Challenge/TriFinger, OCRTOC, CloudGripper, Robotarium) established the "submit code, robot cluster executes" paradigm.

### Cited Findings

**1. RoboArena — Distributed Real-World Evaluation of Generalist Robot Policies (CoRL 2025)**
- Paper: "RoboArena: Distributed Real-World Evaluation of Generalist Robot Policies", Atreya, Pertsch, Lee, Kim, ... Finn, Levine (33 authors); arXiv v1 2025-06-22, v2 2025-11-29; CoRL 2025 (PMLR v305) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18123); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/atreya25a.html); website [robo-arena.github.io](https://robo-arena.github.io/)
- Network: evaluators at Stanford, UC Berkeley, U. Washington, U. Montreal, NVIDIA, UPenn, UT Austin and Yonsei (paper says "seven academic institutions" + NVIDIA); hardware is the DROID platform: "Franka Panda 7DoF robot arm, a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel-jaw gripper, a ZED-mini stereo wrist camera and one or multiple external ZED 2 stereo cameras" — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Submission: policy authors host their own inference server; evaluators receive only an IP address, which "ensures that policies are run correctly, equipped with sufficient inference compute, and proprietary models can be evaluated without sharing model weights" — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Protocol: evaluators freely choose task and scene but must run double-blind A/B pairs with matched initial conditions; per episode they log a 0–100 progress score, a binary preference, and free-form language feedback — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Ranking: extended Bradley–Terry with per-task difficulty τ_t and policy-task offsets, fit by approximate-ML EM; task-aware ranking outperformed plain Bradley–Terry/Elo — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Scale/validation in paper: 612 pairwise comparisons (4,284 total episodes incl. oracle ratings) over 7 DROID policies (π0-flow-DROID, π0-FAST-DROID, PG-flow, PG-FAST, PG-FAST+, PG-FSQ, PG-Bin); Pearson correlation with oracle ranking 0.91 vs 0.57 for a conventional centralized protocol; "converges to high-quality rankings within just 100 pairwise comparisons" — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Incentive/credit system: one credit per pairwise evaluation run, redeemable for evaluations of your own policy; sponsored credits exist for groups without robots; new policies pass a format check and a supervised "test environment" run before general release — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)
- Launch announced 2025-06-20 ("Submit your policies today and join the leaderboard") — [K. Pertsch on X](https://x.com/KarlPertsch/status/1936144625066295731); the site states the benchmark "will be running live through Dec 2026, with potential extensions" [reported via search snippet of robo-arena.github.io; the live leaderboard page is JS-rendered and could not be scraped directly] — [RoboArena site](https://robo-arena.github.io/)
- Public leaderboard snapshot (April 2026, reported by NVIDIA): DreamZero 1750 Elo, π0.5 1622, π0-FAST 1592, π0 1475; NVIDIA describes RoboArena as "one of the few public real-world, open-ended evaluations" — [NVIDIA Developer Blog, 2026-06-15](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/pretrained-to-imagine-fine-tuned-to-act-the-rise-of-world-action-models/)
- Critique: Chris Paxton (2026-06-13) reports that incomplete evaluations led to "benchmark hacking" concerns and RoboArena "now includes confidence indicators for low-sample results"; evaluator supply lags submissions — [It Can Think! substack](https://itcanthink.substack.com/p/what-do-robotics-leaderboards-tell) (note: that article's summary mislabels RoboArena as simulation; the primary paper and NVIDIA blog confirm it is real-robot)
- Stated limitations: DROID-only embodiment; hard to run single-variable controlled studies; robustness to adversarial evaluators not studied; Goodhart risk — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2)

**2. AutoEval — Autonomous Evaluation of Generalist Robot Manipulation Policies in the Real World (CoRL 2025)**
- Paper: Zhou, Atreya, Tan, Pertsch, Levine (UC Berkeley); arXiv 2503.24278; CoRL 2025 (PMLR v305) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24278); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/zhou25a.html); [website](https://auto-eval.github.io/); [code (MIT)](https://github.com/zhouzypaul/auto_eval)
- Hardware: WidowX 250 6-DoF + Logitech C920, 256×256 top-down; three Bridge-AutoEval cells built, two public — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)
- Tasks: 5 Bridge-V2 scene tasks (open/close drawer, eggplant→sink, eggplant→basket, fold cloth); public dashboard exposes 4 (drawer open/close, eggplant to yellow basket / blue sink) — [website](https://auto-eval.github.io/)
- Automation: success classifier = PaliGemma-3B fine-tuned with QLoRA on ~1,000 labeled images/scene (deployed at >95% acc); reset = OpenVLA fine-tuned on 50–100 teleop demos or scripted; safety = workspace bounds, motor-effort limits, auto motor reboot, reset-success classifier escalates to Slack-paged human after ~3 failures — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)
- Throughput & fidelity: ~500 episodes per 24 h per cell (~60k env steps/day); >99% reduction in human supervision time; Pearson r = 0.942 vs human-run evals; ~3 human interventions per 24-h run; 20-min robot rest every 6 h — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); [website](https://auto-eval.github.io/)
- Submission: user runs a policy server (template provided; 256×256 RGB + 8-D proprio + language in, 7-D delta EE action out), exposes it via ngrok/bore, submits IP:port on web dashboard; jobs queued like a cluster scheduler; downloadable report with videos — [website](https://auto-eval.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/zhouzypaul/auto_eval)
- Results in paper (successes /50): OpenVLA 40/46/1/0/13; Open-π0 29/46/7/47/12; Octo 1/5/0/0/4; MiniVLA 33/49/38/0/8; SuSIE 1/18/0/0/9 (order: open drawer / close drawer / to basket / to sink / fold cloth) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)
- Status: website lists the 4 tasks as available "until Jan 1, 2026" with 2026 tasks "TBD"; no leaderboard, only per-job reports — [website](https://auto-eval.github.io/)
- Limitations (self-stated): 1–3 h human setup + ~5 h total per new task; binary success only; tabletop only; no camera/lighting/distractor robustness sweeps — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)

**3. RoboChallenge / Table30 (Dexmal + Hugging Face, launched Oct 2025) and Table30 V2 (Mar 2026)**
- Paper: "RoboChallenge: Large-scale Real-robot Evaluation of Embodied Policies", 37 alphabetical authors (Dexmal, Hugging Face), arXiv 2025-10-20 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17950); site [robochallenge.ai](https://robochallenge.ai)
- Fleet (v1): 10 machines — UR5 (+Robotiq, 125 Hz RTDE), Franka Panda (+Robotiq), Cobot Magic ALOHA (dual 6-DoF), ARX-5; Intel RealSense RGB-D main/wrist/side cameras; policies see 224×224 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1)
- "Remote robot" paradigm: user keeps weights local; pulls timestamped RGB/depth/proprio via API and pushes actions into a FIFO action queue with durations; advance job-scheduling notice so users can spin up GPUs; NAT-friendly (user calls the server, no public endpoint needed) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1)
- Table30: 30 tabletop tasks; up to 1,000 demo episodes/task released for fine-tuning; 10 rollouts per task per model; metrics = success rate + 0–10 staged progress score with 0.5-pt retry penalty and termination after 4 retries; two tracks (specialist = per-task policy; generalist = one policy per embodiment) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1); [Table30 V2 CVF abstract](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/GigaBrainChallenge/html/Ma_Table30_V2_Evaluating_Generalized_Models_by_Real_Robots_at_Scale_CVPRW_2026_paper.html)
- Reset: human testers use "Visual Task Reproduction" (reference image overlaid on live feed) to restore initial scenes; paper distinguishes "stability" (reproducibility) from "fairness" (relative ranking) and proposes a not-yet-implemented blind "comparative protocol" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1)
- Paper baselines (avg SR / avg score): π0.5 task-specific 43.7% / 62.2; π0 28.3% / 47.6; CogACT 11.7% / 21.8; π0.5-multi (generalist) 17.7% / 31.3; π0-multi 9.3% / 20.6; soft-body and temporally-dependent tasks hardest (5–8% SR) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1)
- Self-stated limitations: no enforcement that the submitted model is the claimed model; fixed reference test cases create overfitting risk — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1)
- Scale as of early 2026: "Since launching in October 2025, Table 30 has powered over 80,000 real-world robot task executions, supported 20+ models on the leaderboard" — [RoboChallenge news page via search](https://robochallenge.ai/news) (page is JS-rendered; figure confirmed in search snippet only)
- Table30 V2 (announced 2026-03-24; CVPR 2026 Workshop paper "Table30 V2: Evaluating Generalized Models by Real Robots at Scale", Yunchao Ma et al.): emphasizes generalization — multi-task, zero-shot (unseen objects/backgrounds/disturbances) and in-domain vs out-of-domain protocols; 30 tasks = 12 retained + 18 new dual-arm tasks (rope/cloth, tool use, bimanual coordination); embodiments ARX5, UR5, ALOHA, new DOS-W1 mobile dual-arm; "300% throughput increase" via more hardware and relaxed initialization tolerance; preview ran as the GigaBrain Challenge 2026 @ CVPR real-robot track — [CVF abstract](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/GigaBrainChallenge/html/Ma_Table30_V2_Evaluating_Generalized_Models_by_Real_Robots_at_Scale_CVPRW_2026_paper.html); [QbitAI 2026-03](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/03/391744.html); [HF dataset Table30v2 (1.01 TB, 30 task folders)](https://huggingface.co/datasets/RoboChallenge/Table30v2)

**4. ManipulationNet (Rice University + NIST consortium; arXiv Mar 2026)**
- Paper: "ManipulationNet: An Infrastructure for Benchmarking Real-World Robot Manipulation with Physical Skill Challenges and Embodied Multimodal Reasoning", Chen, Kimble, Adelson, Asfour, ... Goldberg, Kragic, ... Zhu, Hang (24 authors), arXiv 2026-03-04 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04363); site [manipulation-net.org](https://manipulation-net.org/)
- Architecture: hybrid centralized–decentralized — standardized object kits are mailed to teams who run tasks on *their own* robots ("any manipulator, any end-effector, any sensor, and any policy"); an mnet-client (ROS-compatible) receives live task instructions from mnet-server and uploads results; central human committee validates — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1); [site](https://manipulation-net.org/)
- Anti-cheating: independent camera records the whole run with a one-time submission code visible; server issues random requests during execution, client hashes the corresponding frames in real time; committee checks code visibility, hash match and video–status consistency — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1)
- Tracks/tasks: Physical Skills — Peg-in-Hole (5 shapes × 4 clearances 3 mm→0.02 mm, transparent acrylic board), Cable Management (C-clip/round peg/U-clip/Y-clip fixtures, server-issued routing targets), Grasping in Clutter (16 YCB objects; sparse/dense/stacked; declutter rate, grasp SR, time), Threading & Fastening and Belt Routing "coming soon"; Embodied Reasoning — Language-conditioned Tabletop Manipulation, Block Arrangement (5 colors × 10 blocks; language/visual/mixed prompts) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1); [site](https://manipulation-net.org/)
- Timeline/status: first submission Nov 2025 (Linkerbot Research, teleoperated multi-fingered system); first fully autonomous submission Mar 2026 (UC Berkeley AUTOLAB, cable management); anonymous submissions enabled Apr 2026; "EBiM Competition" collaboration announced Jul 2026; "30+ participating teams"; partners include NIST, ASTM, DLR, KIT, KTH, Tsinghua, UT Austin, UT Dallas, CU, CMU, MIT, Autodesk, NVIDIA — [site](https://manipulation-net.org/)
- Positioning: argues an "impossible trinity" (object sets lack evaluation, competitions lack accessibility, simulators lack realism); baselines in paper are labeled preliminary — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1)

**5. RoboDojo-RealEval (HKU MMLab-led consortium; arXiv Jul 2026)**
- Paper: "RoboDojo: A Unified Sim-and-Real Benchmark for Comprehensive Evaluation of Generalist Robot Manipulation Policies", Tianxing Chen et al. (46 authors; HKU, Berkeley, THU, PKU, Stanford, MIT, ... maintained by "AI MMLab Club"), arXiv 2026-07-05 (v3 2026-07-08) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04434); site robodojo-benchmark.com; code github.com/RoboDojo-Benchmark/RoboDojo
- Real suite: 18 tasks on 3 bimanual platforms (ARX X5, Piper, Piper X; 6 tasks each) covering long-horizon, precision, memory, generalization; 10 trials/task → 180 trials per policy — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)
- Remote eval: "supports remote evaluation without requiring participants to release checkpoints or source code"; users run a policy server speaking the official deployment protocol; fixed arm/camera/lighting rig with touchscreen web UI; initial layouts restored by transparent overlay; all trials recorded and "independently scored by three evaluators under a double-blind protocol" with sub-step credit — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)
- Leaderboard policy: official entries must be run through the online system and release checkpoint/code via "XPolicyLab" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)
- Real-world results (10 policies): π0.5 12.8% SR / 22.9 score (best); InternVLA-A1 7.2% / 12.0; GalaxeaVLA (G0) 4.4% / 9.0; Xiaomi-Robotics-0 3.9% / 7.9; several at 0% (e.g., DM0, StarVLA-α on some embodiments); human teleop 100% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)

**6. ArmnetBench v0.1 (Armnet, arXiv Jul 2026) — managed low-cost arm farm (not yet an open submission service)**
- Paper: Selvaraj, Uttini, Kuosmanen (Armnet), arXiv 2026-07-27; data in LeRobot v3.0 + RoboMeter formats, CC-BY 4.0 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24481)
- Hardware: 3 cells (2 single-arm SO-101, 1 bimanual), 3 cams @20 fps, Raspberry Pi 5; ~$359/single-arm cell, $477/bimanual cell — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)
- Protocol: one operator supervises 3 cells; manual resets with randomized object poses; three-way human labels (success / suboptimal 3.5% / failure) via web panel; ~10 s operator time per rollout; 3,118 labeled episodes (2,518 rollouts + 600 demos); 12 tasks, 7 policies, 50 demos/task fine-tuning — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)
- Results (strict SR): π0.5 47.6%, π0 35.1%, GR00T N1.7 29.4%, Diffusion Policy 26.7%, ACT 19.2%, MolmoAct 2 18.9%, SmolVLA 15.0% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)
- Limitations: each task ran on one cell so cross-cell reproducibility untested; no fixed rollout time limits; no external submissions described — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)

**7. PhAIL (Positronic Robotics, arXiv May 2026) — open-to-submission single-task real-robot leaderboard with distributional metrics**
- Paper: "PhAIL: A Real-Robot VLA Benchmark and Distributional Methodology", Sergey Arkhangelskiy, arXiv 2026-05-28; site/leaderboard [phail.ai](https://phail.ai) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710)
- Setup: Franka Research 3 + Robotiq 2F-85, shoulder + wrist RGB; bin-to-bin order picking of 4 object types; "open to submissions" via the open-source positronic framework (only weights + obs-to-action code differ) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1)
- Protocol: scheduler randomly picks which model runs next so the operator is blind; automated detector + manual review of ~42% of episodes; public run-explorer for audit; ~995 rollouts incl. 396 human-teleop reference; 26–46 episodes per model — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1)
- Metrics: time-to-success CDFs, Human-Relative Throughput (HRT) with bootstrap CIs, KS tests. Results: human RMST 10.5 s / HRT 100%; OpenPI π0.5 77.7 s / 13.8%; GR00T N1.6 77.2 s / 13.3%; ACT 100.9 s / 10.5%; SmolVLA 165.8 s / 6.4%; best VLA ~7× slower than human; a camera/tote side swap shifts GR00T completion by 22 pp — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1)

**8. Humanoid Everyday cloud evaluation platform (USC + TRI, Oct 2025) — first humanoid-specific cloud eval**

### Inferences
- Three distinct trust models have emerged: (i) *trust the evaluator network* (RoboArena: double-blind pairs + statistical aggregation), (ii) *trust the central fleet* (RoboChallenge, RoboDojo, PhAIL, UMI Arena: organizer-run hardware, user keeps weights), (iii) *trust cryptographic provenance* (ManipulationNet: local execution with server-issued challenges and hashed video). Only (ii) gives absolute success rates comparable across submissions; (i) gives only relative rankings; (iii) depends on human review.
- Automated reset/success detection (AutoEval, HALTER) is still confined to short-horizon, single-scene tasks; every large multi-task fleet (RoboChallenge, RoboDojo, ArmnetBench, Humanoid Everyday) still uses human operators with image-overlay resets.

## KQ4. How do these platforms handle fairness, reset automation, success detection, statistical ranking, and cost/throughput?

### Takeaway
No platform has solved all four at once: AutoEval automates reset and success detection but only for 4–5 single-step tasks on one robot; RoboArena solves cross-site comparability with double-blind pairwise preferences and a task-aware Bradley–Terry model but cannot report absolute success; RoboChallenge/RoboDojo/TRI achieve controlled initial conditions with human image-overlay resets and staged rubrics at the cost of operator labor; ManipulationNet secures authenticity cryptographically; PhAIL pushes statistical rigor (CDFs, bootstrap CIs, KS tests) but on a single task.

### Cited Findings
- **Blinding / fairness**: RoboArena evaluators "do not know which policies they are evaluating" and must match initial conditions within each A/B pair — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2); RoboDojo uses three independent double-blind scorers — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1); PhAIL randomizes model order so the operator is blind — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1); TRI randomizes policy order within shared-initial-condition bundles with blinded evaluators — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1); RoboChallenge acknowledges it cannot verify the submitted model is the claimed model and that fixed test cases risk overfitting — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1); ManipulationNet uses one-time codes, server-issued random frame-hash challenges and committee review — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1)
- **Reset automation**: AutoEval — learned OpenVLA reset policies / scripted resets, reset-success classifier, ~3 human interventions per day — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); HALTER — scene-graph + LLM reset for long-horizon, 76% restoration vs 52% (AutoEval-style) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19413); RoboChallenge, RoboDojo and TRI all use human resets guided by overlay of a reference image on the live camera — [RoboChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1); [RoboDojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1); [TRI](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1); Humanoid Everyday has no automatic reset — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08807); ArmnetBench manual reset ~10 s/rollout incl. scoring — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)
- **Success detection**: AutoEval VLM (PaliGemma-3B QLoRA) classifier at >95% accuracy — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); RoboArena human 0–100 progress + preference + text — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2); RoboChallenge 0–10 staged score with retry penalties — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1); TRI binary milestone rubrics, QA on 27% of rollouts with 2.31% label discrepancy — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1); PhAIL automated detector + manual review of 42% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1); ArmnetBench three-way labels — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1); REAL-I staged points with task time limits — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13679v1); Robothon/euROBIN task boards self-report completion and time to a dashboard — [GitHub](https://github.com/peterso/robothon-grand-challenge); Origami pass/fail by human grand master — [site](https://robotic-origami-challenge.github.io/); reliability of VLM judges is itself benchmarked by FailBench (arXiv 2609.03611) [not fetched; pointer only] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03611)
- **Ranking statistics**: RoboArena task-aware Bradley–Terry via EM; Pearson 0.91 vs oracle; ~100 pairs to converge — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2); public RoboArena numbers are reported as Elo-style scores (e.g., 1750/1622) — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/pretrained-to-imagine-fine-tuned-to-act-the-rise-of-world-action-models/); TRI sequential hypothesis testing, Welch t-test, CLD at 95% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1); PhAIL HRT with bootstrap CIs and KS tests, N≈35 per cell "still below what binary metrics need" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29710v1); RoboChallenge 10 rollouts/task, RoboDojo 10 trials/task × 18 tasks = 180 — [RoboChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1); [RoboDojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)
- **Cost / throughput**: AutoEval ~500 episodes/day/cell, >99% less human time, 1–3 h setup per task — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); ArmnetBench $359–$477 per cell, 1 operator : 3 cells — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1); RoboChallenge >80,000 executions in ~5 months on a 10-robot fleet, V2 claims 300% throughput gain — [robochallenge.ai/news (snippet)](https://robochallenge.ai/news); [QbitAI](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/03/391744.html); TRI 1,800 real rollouts on 9 stations — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1); RoboArena provides no cost figures but uses credits as the currency (1 evaluation run = 1 credit) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v2); CloudGripper 32 cells, 10 Gbit/s — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12786)
- **Access model / privacy**: weights never leave the submitter in RoboArena, RoboChallenge, RoboDojo, Humanoid Everyday, AutoEval (policy server) — [sources above]; REAL-I and OCRTOC/RRC require uploading Docker images/code — [REAL-I](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13679v1); [OCRTOC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11446); ManipulationNet requires no upload of policy at all, only verified video/logs — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1)

### Inferences
- Policy-server architectures (user-hosted inference) have become the de facto standard because they simultaneously solve IP protection, inference compute, and NAT issues; the trade-off is that latency and user-side bugs become confounds that only RoboChallenge's queue-length feedback partially mitigates.
- Progress/partial-credit scoring is now near-universal (RoboArena 0–100, Table30 0–10, RoboDojo sub-steps, TRI milestones, REAL-I staged points); binary success alone persists only in fully automated cells (AutoEval).

### Gaps
- No platform publishes per-episode monetary cost; throughput figures exist only for AutoEval, ArmnetBench and RoboChallenge.
- Inter-rater reliability numbers exist only for TRI (2.31%/6.25%) and are absent for RoboChallenge, RoboDojo and RoboArena.

### Inferences
- Rankings are regime-dependent: π0.5 wins when policies are fine-tuned per task on organizer demos (Table30 specialist, ArmnetBench) and in open-ended zero-shot DROID scenes it is beaten by DROID-native WAMs; multi-task generalist settings compress everyone toward 10–20% SR (Table30 generalist, RoboDojo).
- Company models that top RoboChallenge (DM0, Spirit v1.5, GigaBrain, Qwen-RobotManip) have mostly not been evaluated on RoboArena, and vice versa (DreamZero), so no single cross-platform SOTA can be asserted.
