## Key Question 1 — Best-practice and critique papers (what do they recommend, and what is wrong with current practice?)

### Takeaway
A small but fast-growing literature (TRI 2024 → 2026) argues that "success rate over ~10 trials, no CIs, no initial-condition control" is scientifically inadequate; it recommends blind interleaved A/B trials, image-overlay initial-condition control, rubric-based partial credit, Bayesian/sequential statistics with multiplicity correction, and explicit failure-mode reporting. 2026 critique papers extend this to benchmark-validity failures (shortcut solvability, non-significant SOTA claims, overfitting, data-source dependence) and to the unverifiability of "physical reasoning" claims from success rates alone.
(한국어 요약: 2024~2026년 사이 "성공률 10회, 신뢰구간 없음, 초기조건 통제 없음" 관행을 비판하고 블라인드 A/B 인터리빙, 이미지 오버레이 초기조건 통제, 루브릭 부분점수, 베이지안/순차검정+다중비교 보정, 실패모드 보고를 권고하는 문헌이 TRI를 중심으로 형성됨.)

### Cited Findings

**Core best-practice paper**
- "Robot Learning as an Empirical Science: Best Practices for Policy Evaluation" — Hadas Kress-Gazit (Cornell / TRI), Kunimatsu Hashimoto, Naveen Kuppuswamy, Paarth Shah, Phoebe Horgan, Gordon Richardson, Siyuan Feng, Benjamin Burchfiel (Toyota Research Institute). arXiv:2409.09491 (v2, Sept 2024; arXiv preprint, no peer-reviewed venue found). Diagnosis: the dominant metric is "success rate ... with little to no information regarding the number of runs, the initial conditions, and the success criteria, little to no narrative description of the behaviors and failures observed, and little to no statistical analysis." — [arXiv 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09491)
- Its four recommendation groups: (1) Experiment setup — unambiguous success definition written by the behavior designer *before* evaluation, initial conditions controlled "through image overlays or markings in the scene", blind A/B interleaving of policies, separation of demonstrator and evaluator roles, evaluating in one session to avoid environmental drift; (2) Metrics beyond success — "semantic" metrics (task-specific rubrics/checklists of subgoals; Signal Temporal Logic specs) and "performance" metrics (STL robustness = how close to success/failure; smoothness via SPARC spectral arc length); (3) Analysis/reporting — report trial counts not just percentages, timing (A/B, single session), visual documentation of ICs, relationship between eval and training distribution, Bayesian posterior over success probability or frequentist interval estimates; (4) Qualitative failure-mode descriptions with categories, frequencies and visuals. — [arXiv HTML 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491)
- Case-study scale in that paper: pancake task compared Policy A 15/18 = 83.3% vs Policy B 11/17 = 64.7%, and the Bayesian (Bernoulli, uniform prior) posterior gives "a .11 probability that B actually performs better than A"; the shirt-folding case used 20 evaluations per policy; the energy-bar example used 10 ICs × 2 runs across two days to *detect* an unintended IC distribution shift. "Providing a point estimate for success and performance metrics can be misleading." — [arXiv HTML 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491)

**TRI follow-ups that operationalise the above**
- "Statistical Thinking for Robot Policy Evaluation: From Rigorous A/B Testing to Effective Visualization" — TRI Medium post (2025). Explains Bonferroni-corrected pairwise tests (k policies → k(k−1)/2 pairwise tests, each at α/#tests so a global 95% level holds) and Compact Letter Display (CLD): policies sharing no letter are statistically separated. (Page returned HTTP 403 to my fetch; details come from search-engine snippets, so treat specific wording as partially verified.) — [TRI Medium](https://medium.com/toyotaresearch/statistical-thinking-for-robot-policy-evaluation-from-rigorous-a-b-testing-to-effective-0ae886fbd68d)
- "A Careful Examination of Large Behavior Models for Multitask Dexterous Manipulation" — TRI LBM Team, arXiv:2507.05331 (July 2025); a version appears in Science Robotics (DOI 10.1126/scirobotics.aea6201; page not opened). This is the most complete *worked example* of the best-practice protocol: "Each real-world task was evaluated with 50 rollouts per task per policy per condition" and 200 per task in simulation; 1,800 real-world rollouts and >47,000 sim rollouts in total; evaluators "did not know which policy was being tested (blind testing), policy ordering was randomized"; real-world evaluation uses randomized "test bundles" where "each bundle corresponds to a single initial condition with a randomized policy ordering"; initial conditions set with image overlays ("By matching the robot's environment to the overlay, we mitigate the uncertainty in the initial conditions"); statistics: Bernoulli success with "a uniform prior", posteriors shown as violin plots, "uniform Dirichlet prior" for multi-task task-completion, sequential hypothesis test for binary outcomes (STEP), Welch's t-test for rubric scores, Bonferroni correction for a "global 95% confidence level", CLD letters; metrics: Success Rate and Task Completion (milestone rubric: +1 per completed milestone / #milestones); distribution-shift conditions in the real world = "station shift" (unseen workstations) and "object-centric shift" (≥5 novel instances per key manipuland). QA: "∼27% of nearly 2700 real-world evaluation rollouts" re-scored by separate QA staff → "QA success rate discrepancy was 2.31% and overall rubric question discrepancy was 6.25%". — [arXiv HTML 2507.05331](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331); [Science Robotics DOI](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scirobotics.aea6201)

**Statistical-comparison methods (sequential testing, bounds, active selection)**
- "Is Your Imitation Learning Policy Better than Mine? Policy Comparison with Near-Optimal Stopping" (STEP = Sequential Testing for Efficient Policy comparison) — David Snyder, Asher James Hancock, Apurva Badithela, Emma Dixon, Patrick Miller, Rares Andrei Ambrus, Anirudha Majumdar, Masha Itkina, Haruki Nishimura (Princeton + TRI). RSS 2025. arXiv:2503.10966. Problem: one-sided test H0: p1 ≤ p0 vs H1: p1 > p0 on paired Bernoulli outcomes; STEP builds decision regions by linear programming over the sufficient statistic so the evaluator can "stop conducting trials early without sacrificing the probabilistic correctness" or "safely append additional trials ... without inadvertent p-hacking". Baselines: Lai's asymptotic sequential procedure, SAVI (safe anytime-valid inference), Barnard's batch test (shown to violate Type-I control when applied sequentially — a p-hacking demonstration), and an SPRT oracle. Reduces trials "by up to 32%" vs SOTA baselines. Hardware: bimanual Franka FoldRedTowel (50 trials; 36-pt gap detected in 19–23 trials), CleanUpSpill (50 trials; 52-pt gap detected in 7–14 trials), WidowX CarrotOnPlate comparing Octo-Base vs OpenVLA (100 trials; 9-pt gap → no significant difference; authors estimate "N_max = 500 trials" would be needed). Current practice characterised as "only a small number of trials (e.g., 10–60)". Multi-policy extension via Bonferroni. — [arXiv abs 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966); [arXiv HTML 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10966)
- "Beyond Binary Success: Sample-Efficient and Statistically Rigorous Robot Policy Comparison" — David Snyder, Apurva Badithela, Nikolai Matni, George Pappas, Anirudha Majumdar, Masha Itkina, Haruki Nishimura. arXiv:2603.13616 (Mar 2026; cs.RO/stat.AP; venue not stated). Extends sequential comparison to non-binary metrics ("discrete partial credit task progress to continuous measures of episodic reward or trajectory smoothness") using safe anytime-valid inference; "up to 70% reduction in evaluation burden compared to standard batch methods" and up to 50% vs existing sequential procedures; policies separate faster with fine-grained progress metrics than with binary success. — [arXiv 2603.13616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616)
- "How Generalizable Is My Behavior Cloning Policy? A Statistical Approach to Trustworthy Performance Evaluation" — Joseph A. Vincent, Haruki Nishimura, Masha Itkina, Paarth Shah, Mac Schwager, Thomas Kollar (Stanford MSL + TRI). arXiv:2405.05439 (abs says "submitted to the IEEE"; an IEEE Xplore record exists, 10638686 — likely RA-L; not verified). Gives "a tight lower-bound on robot performance in an arbitrary environment" from few rollouts via stochastic ordering / CDF bounds, validated in sim and hardware incl. "rigorously comparing two policies tested in out-of-distribution settings"; code for the confidence bounds is open-source. — [arXiv 2405.05439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05439); [project page](https://tri-ml.github.io/stochastic_verification/)
- "Efficient Evaluation of Multi-Task Robot Policies With Active Experiment Selection" — Abrar Anwar, Rohan Gupta, Zain Merchant, Sayan Ghosh, Willie Neiswanger, Jesse Thomason (USC). CoRL 2025 (PMLR v305). arXiv:2502.09829. Treats evaluation as active testing: a surrogate over (policy × task) performance with natural-language task priors and a "cost-aware expected information gain heuristic"; on existing real-robot and sim evaluation data they report ~50% reduction in cost to estimate mean performance (search snippet) — [arXiv 2502.09829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09829); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/anwar25a.html)
- "Active Real-World Factor-Based Evaluation for Generalist Robot Policies" — Andrew Liao, Hanchen Cui, Karthik Desingh, Aryan Deshwal. arXiv:2607.14439 (July 2026). Frames evaluation as sequential experimental design over a structured factor space (object pose, camera viewpoint...), fits a probabilistic surrogate and picks configurations by information gain; "2331 real-world evaluations across 3 tasks with 3 factor variations"; saves "at least 20–40% of trials compared to typical random testing"; motivates that "current practice is to use narrow test suites that can miss critical failure modes". — [arXiv 2607.14439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14439)
- "Reliable and Scalable Robot Policy Evaluation with Imperfect Simulators" (SureSim) — Apurva Badithela, David Snyder, Lihan Zha, Joseph Mikhail, Matthew O'Kelly, Anushri Dixit, Anirudha Majumdar (Princeton). arXiv:2510.04354 (Oct 2025; ICRA 2026 per search snippet, not verified on page). Formalises combining few paired real + many sim rollouts as prediction-powered inference with non-asymptotic mean-estimation CIs; "20–25%" hardware-effort savings for the same bound width. (Belongs mainly to the real-to-sim slice but is the main statistical bridge paper.) — [arXiv 2510.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354)

**Benchmark-validity / position papers (2025–2026)**
- "What Are We Actually Benchmarking in Robot Manipulation?" — Tianchong Jiang, Xiangshan Tan, Samuel Wheeler, Luzhe Sun, Tewodros W. Ayalew, Matthew Walter (TTI-Chicago / Argonne / UChicago). arXiv:2606.04233 (June 2026). Four failure modes that invalidate benchmarks as capability proxies: shortcut solvability, lack of statistical significance, creeping overfitting, data-source dependence. Audits LIBERO, CALVIN, SimplerEnv, RoboCasa, RoboTwin 2.0 (all sim, but the diagnostics transfer). Numbers: "In March 2026 alone, 79 arXiv papers reported results on LIBERO"; "Only 19.8% of LIBERO SOTA claims are provably significant", 19.7% on SimplerEnv, 53.3% RoboCasa, 73.7% RoboTwin 2.0; a 90M-param DINO+MLP probe without language reaches 99.0% on LIBERO-Spatial vs OpenVLA 84.7%; CALVIN ATC drops 4.17→3.14 for X-VLA when block poses are merely resampled within the training range. Recommendations: test distributions should "cover broad variation in objects and scenes", and protocols should "specify what score gap counts as a statistically significant improvement over the previous SOTA"; proposes an evaluation-standards hierarchy (bitwise determinism → per-episode agreement → aggregate agreement → protocol & statistical reproducibility). — [arXiv HTML 2606.04233](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04233v1)
- "Position: Vision-Language-Action Models Cannot Be Verified to Perform Physical Reasoning" — Taozhao Chen, Ian Manchester, Huaming Chen. arXiv:2606.30686 (June 2026). Argues that task success rates cannot distinguish semantic matching / distributional overlap from genuine physical generalization; proposes evaluation designs with controlled variation that separately measure semantic vs physical generalization "enabling causal attribution without model internals access." — [arXiv 2606.30686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30686)
- "Inductive Generalization for Robotic Manipulation" — Annabella Macaluso, Haochen Zhang, Ishaan Masilamony, Yingshan Chang, Yonatan Bisk. arXiv:2606.20999 (June 2026). Position: "visuomotor policies test performance by interpolation on known distributions using unstructured domain shifts (e.g. lighting, clutter, diverse objects)"; argues for testing "inductive capacity ... on progressively harder, out-of-distribution task variants"; reports SOTA VLAs fail such structured tests. — [arXiv 2606.20999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20999)
- "Robot Policy Evaluation for Sim-to-Real Transfer: A Benchmarking Perspective" — Xuning Yang, Clemens Eppner, Jonathan Tremblay, Dieter Fox, Stan Birchfield, Fabio Ramos (NVIDIA). arXiv:2508.11117 (Aug 2025). Perspective paper proposing a 4-level task taxonomy (T1 single-motion, T2 continuous-motion, T3 multi-step, T4 long-horizon w/ memory), five scene-variation axes (object placement, number of objects/distractors, texture, lighting, camera pose) and a metric set spanning discrete (completion, success, failure modes), continuous (smoothness, path length), temporal (inference time, episode duration) and sim-to-real fidelity measures; cites 24–30% sim-to-real drops and 30–50% drops from lighting/camera changes. — [arXiv HTML 2508.11117](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11117)
- "Experiences from Benchmarking Vision–Language–Action Models for Robotic Manipulation" — Yihao Zhang, Yuankai Qi, Xi Zheng (Macquarie Univ.). arXiv:2511.11298 (Nov 2025; page lists an embedded-AI conference venue). Real-world bimanual (ALOHA Mobile) + MuJoCo, 4 tasks × 4 models (ACT, OpenVLA-OFT, RDT-1B, π0), "50 trials per model per evaluation setting", metrics = Success Rate + Time-to-Success + Instruction-following Accuracy, all with Wilson-score 95% CIs; two-level failure taxonomy (task-level: grasp 32%, release/placement timing 18%, drift under occlusion 15%, instruction adherence 10%; model-level: perception misalignment 9%, temporal inconsistency 6%, language grounding 6%) over 180 annotated failures. — [arXiv HTML 2511.11298](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11298v1)
- "Benchmarking Vision-Language-Action Models on SO-101: Failure and Recovery Analysis" — Yi Yu, Xinchuan Qiu. arXiv:2606.08881 (June 2026). Low-cost real-world benchmark (π0.5, SmolVLA, Wall-X, ACT) that adds "a structured failure taxonomy, semantic- and execution-level failure decomposition, and recovery-aware evaluation metrics"; finds "execution instability emerges as the dominant failure source". — [arXiv 2606.08881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08881)
- "No Free Checker: A Survey of Verifiers for Robot Policies" — Yang Wan, Xihang Yue, Zhirui Liu, Ziyuan Chu, Shuxun Wang, Yuhan Chen, Xiaonan Jiang, Xukun Zhu, Yubo Dong, Linchao Zhu. arXiv:2609.09250 (Sept 2026; 33 pp, 202 refs). Surveys ~150 verifiers (success detectors, reward models, runtime monitors, safety filters, temporal-logic specs) along two axes — *availability* (cost, earliness, frequency of verdicts) and *credibility* (how much a high score reflects real success vs. being gameable) — grouped into human, rule-based/formal, learned/pretrained, model-intrinsic; central finding "credibility falls as availability rises". Reading list: [GitHub ZJUSCL/Awesome-Robot-Verifier](https://github.com/ZJUSCL/Awesome-Robot-Verifier). — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)
- Informal but influential critique: "Evaluating π0 in the Wild: Strengths, Problems, and the Future of Generalist Robot Policies" — Jie Wang, Matthew Leonard, Kostas Daniilidis, Dinesh Jayaraman, Edward S. Hu (Penn GRASP/PAL Lab, 2025 blog). 300+ trials on a Franka FR3 across 7 task categories using progress score + success; average progress 42.3%; prompt sensitivity example: "close the white lid of the toilet" 100% vs "close the toilet" 0%; adopts an explicit NLP-style "vibe-check" philosophy instead of a fixed benchmark. — [Penn blog](https://penn-pal-lab.github.io/Pi0-Experiment-in-the-Wild/)

- "RB2: Robotic Manipulation Benchmarking with a Twist" — Sudeep Dasari, Jianren Wang, Joyce Hong, Shikhar Bahl, Yixin Lin, Austin Wang, Abitha Thankaraj, Karanbir Chahal, Berk Calli, Saurabh Gupta, David Held, Lerrel Pinto, Deepak Pathak, Vikash Kumar, Abhinav Gupta (CMU/FAIR/NYU/WPI/UIUC). ### Inferences
- The TRI line (Kress-Gazit 2024 → STEP 2025 → LBM 2025 → Beyond-Binary 2026) is the only internally consistent *end-to-end protocol* (IC overlays + blind randomized bundles + 50 trials + rubric TC + Bayesian/sequential tests + CLD + QA re-scoring) currently demonstrated at scale; an awesome-list "methodology" section can present it as the reference protocol.
- The 2026 critique papers (Jiang et al., Chen et al., Macaluso et al.) shift the debate from "how many trials" to "does the benchmark measure the capability at all" — i.e., construct validity; this is a distinct sub-category worth a separate heading.
- Statistical methods papers are still almost entirely from the TRI/Princeton group plus USC; there is no community-wide standard (e.g., no agreed CI type or minimal N) yet.

## Key Question 2 — Generalization taxonomies and perturbation-axis protocols used in the real world

### Takeaway
Real-world generalization is now tested along fairly convergent axes — visual (background, lighting, distractors, object appearance, camera pose), semantic/instruction (paraphrase, novel nouns, spatial references, internet knowledge), behavioral/action (object pose, size/shape, novel objects, embodiment) — with STAR-Gen (22 axes) being the most granular formalisation and factor-decomposition studies (Xie et al. 2023, COLOSSEUM 2024/2026) establishing that camera pose and textures are hardest while backgrounds/lighting are easier.
(한국어 요약: 시각/의미/행동 축으로 수렴 중이며, STAR-Gen(22축)이 가장 세밀한 분류. 요인분해 연구들은 카메라 포즈·텍스처가 가장 어렵고 배경·조명은 상대적으로 쉽다는 일관된 순서를 보고.)

### Cited Findings

**Formal taxonomies**
- "A Taxonomy for Evaluating Generalist Robot Policies" (STAR-Gen = Systematic Taxonomy of the Axes of Robot Generalization) — Jensen Gao, Suneel Belkhale, Sudeep Dasari, Ashwin Balakrishna, Dhruv Shah, Dorsa Sadigh (Stanford / Google DeepMind / Princeton). arXiv:2503.01238 (v1 Mar 2025, v3 Feb 2026); journal ref on abs page: IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L). Project page: stargen-taxonomy.github.io. Taxonomy built on policy modalities — Visual (changes initial observation only), Semantic (changes language only), Behavioral (changes the expert action distribution) and their intersections, 22 axes total: V-AUG, V-SC, V-OBJ, V-VIEW; S-PROP, S-LANG, S-MO, S-AFF, S-INT; B-HOBJ, B-HSC; VB-POSE, VB-ISC, VB-MOBJ, VB-ROB, VB-SYM; SB-ADV, SB-SMO, SB-NOUN, SB-VRB; VS-PROP; VSB-NOBJ. Real-world instantiation "BridgeV2-STAR": 4 base tasks (put carrot on plate, put knife on plate, flip pot upright, put plate in sink), 55 perturbation conditions over 13–14 axes, 5 trials per model per condition, 885 trials total, models OpenVLA / MiniVLA / π0 (co-fine-tuned on base-task demos to define the in-distribution reference). Protocol notes: perturbations are "atomic" (one change relative to in-distribution base demos), success functions written per task, 100-timestep cap, and environmental drift monitored by comparing live images to reference demonstrations. Finding: "existing generalist policies tend to struggle on most axes. In particular, semantic generalization is mostly weak, despite the use of language model backbones." — [arXiv abs 2503.01238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01238); [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01238v3)
- "Decomposing the Generalization Gap in Imitation Learning for Visual Robotic Manipulation" — Annie Xie, Lisa Lee, Ted Xiao, Chelsea Finn (Stanford / Google DeepMind). arXiv:2307.03659 (July 2023; CoRL 2023 — venue from memory). Real-robot protocol: Google robot "pick" skill, 6 picking tasks; factors = lighting (2 new), distractor sets (3), table textures (3), backgrounds (3), camera poses (2); "For each factor of interest, we conduct two evaluation trials in each of the six tasks ... success rate averaged across the 12 trials"; object positions randomised each episode because "it is generally difficult to precisely replace an object to its original position in physical setups". Generalization gap defined as P_T − P_F (train-env success minus success under factor shift). Findings: "New camera positions are the hardest to generalize to while new backgrounds are the easiest"; new textures harder than new backgrounds; in Factor World (sim, 19 tasks × 11 factors) the gap shrinks "from a maximum gap of 0.4 to less than 0.1" when training environments go from 5 to 100. — [arXiv 2307.03659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03659); [project page](https://sites.google.com/view/generalization-gap)
- "THE COLOSSEUM: A Benchmark for Evaluating Generalization for Robotic Manipulation" — Wilbert Pumacay, Ishika Singh, Jiafei Duan, Ranjay Krishna, Jesse Thomason, Dieter Fox (UCSP / USC / UW / AI2 / NVIDIA). RSS 2024. arXiv:2402.08191. Simulation benchmark (20 RLBench tasks) whose abstract says "14 axes of environmental perturbations"; the perturbation list in the HTML enumerates 12 named factors: manipulation-object colour/texture/size, receiver-object colour/texture/size, light colour, table colour, table texture, distractors, background texture, camera pose (the remaining two "axes" are the no-perturbation and all-perturbations conditions — my reading, not verified). Real-world counterpart: 4 tasks (insert onto square peg, slide block to target, scoop with spatula, setup chess) on a Franka Panda + Kinect-2, all factors replicated physically, "10 episodes, for 3 separate runs" per condition; sim–real correlation R² = 0.614 overall (per-factor 0.46–0.94; 7/12 factors "strong"); 3D-printable object models released for replication. Findings: distractor count, target-object colour and lighting degrade most; single perturbations cost 30–50%, all-at-once ≥75%. — [arXiv 2402.08191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191); [RSS 2024 paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p133.pdf); [project](https://robot-colosseum.github.io/)
- "Colosseum V2: Benchmarking Generalization for Vision-Language-Action Models" — Jeremy Morgan, Hyeonho Oh, Prajwal Vijay, Jincen Song, Ashvin Arora, Hojung Lim, Alina Du, Jesse Thomason, Gaurav S. Sukhatme, Ishika Singh (USC; IIT Madras; Columbia). arXiv:2605.27759 (v4; page says accepted Aug 2026, venue not shown). 19 perturbations in three axes — Visual (11: object colours/textures, lighting, table/background, distractors, camera pose), Language (4: paraphrase, instruction from another task, random English words, no instruction), Action (3: manipulation-object size, receiving-object size, randomised initial poses) + an all-perturbations condition. Real-world: five single-arm tasks (RaiseCube, RotateArrow, LiftPegUpright, PickDishFromRack, PickSodaFromCabinet), "20 trials per task-condition pair"; absolute-success sim-real R² = 0.49 but perturbation *effects relative to baseline* preserved (12.3 pt mean absolute difference after baseline normalisation). — [arXiv HTML 2605.27759](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27759)

**De-facto axes in major generalist-policy papers (real-world)**
- RT-1 (Brohan et al., Google, arXiv:2212.06817; RSS 2023 — venue from memory): "over 3000 real-world trials"; categories = seen tasks (>200 instructions), unseen tasks (21 novel instructions recombining seen skills/objects), distractor robustness (30 tasks), background robustness (22 tasks), long-horizon SayCan kitchens (15 instructions, ~10 steps each) with difficulty levels L1 (new counter-top layout and lighting), L2 (+ unseen distractors), L3 ("drastically new task settings, new task objects or objects in unseen locations"). — [arXiv HTML 2212.06817](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06817v2)
- RT-2 (arXiv:2307.15818; CoRL 2023 — venue from memory): ~6,000 trials; generalization split into unseen objects / unseen backgrounds / unseen environments, each with easy and hard variants (>280 tasks); "emergent" capability suite = symbol understanding, reasoning, human recognition; "all four models are evaluated one after another in the exact same conditions" (A/B); emergent instructions run 5× each, generalization tasks 1–5×. — [arXiv HTML 2307.15818](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15818)
- Open X-Embodiment / RT-X (arXiv:2310.08864; ICRA 2024 — venue from memory): "3600 evaluation trials across 6 different robots" at multiple labs; protocol = reuse "the same evaluation and robot embodiment as in the respective publications"; axes = in-distribution (small-data vs large-data domains), OOD (unseen objects/backgrounds/environments), and emergent skills (objects/skills absent from RT-2 data but present in Bridge). — [arXiv HTML 2310.08864](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08864)
- BridgeData V2 (Homer Walke et al., arXiv:2308.12952; CoRL 2023 — venue from memory): WidowX 250 (~$4k); 8 seen + 6 unseen tasks; unseen split into {unseen objects/seen env, unseen objects/unseen env, seen objects/unseen env}; "10 trials for each task, varying the positions of objects and distractors"; plus a cross-institution zero-shot test of 3 tasks "at a second lab with different robot setup, camera placement, lighting, and object instances". This toy-sink/kitchen WidowX setup became the de-facto shared real-world eval scene for Octo/OpenVLA/STAR-Gen/STEP. — [arXiv HTML 2308.12952](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12952)
- Octo (arXiv:2405.12213; RSS 2024 — venue from memory): zero-shot + fine-tuning on "9 robot setups at 4 institutions"; "10 trials per task and varied objects positions between trials"; qualitative split into in-distribution / novel objects & environments / novel skills ("struggles to generalize to skills not seen"). — [arXiv HTML 2405.12213](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12213)
- OpenVLA (arXiv:2406.09246; CoRL 2024 — venue from memory): WidowX "17 tasks with 10 trials each" (170 rollouts/policy) + Google robot "12 tasks with 5 trials each" (60); explicit categories — Visual (unseen backgrounds, distractors, object colours), Motion (unseen positions/orientations), Physical (unseen sizes/shapes), Semantic (unseen target objects, instructions, internet concepts), Language grounding (correct target among several); scoring 0/1 with 0.5 partial credit on some tasks; "A/B evaluations, using the same tasks with the same sets of initial robot and object states"; "Average success rates ± StdErr computed across 170 total rollouts per approach". Fine-tuning eval: 129 rollouts per approach on Franka. — [arXiv HTML 2406.09246](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246v3)
- DROID (arXiv:2403.12945; RSS 2024 — venue from memory): "6 tasks in 4 different locations including lab, office, and household settings"; each task has an "in-distribution" condition (demo distribution + noise on robot/object positions) and an "out-of-distribution" condition (distractors, switched manipulated object); "10 rollouts for each task setting and method"; success averaged across tasks "with standard error". — [arXiv HTML 2403.12945](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12945)
- π0 (Physical Intelligence, arXiv:2410.24164, Nov 2024; RSS 2025 — venue from memory): categories = out-of-box (zero-shot), language following, fine-tuning to new dexterous tasks, complex multi-stage tasks; "normalized score averaged over 10 episodes per task and method, where an episode receives a score of 1.0 for a full success, and a fractional score for partial success"; rubric examples: bussing-easy = 7 objects, 1 point per correctly sorted object; laundry folding 4-step rubric; Tupperware-in-microwave 4 milestones (open, grasp, insert, close); no CIs/error bars in figures. — [arXiv HTML 2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164v3)
- π0.5 (arXiv:2504.16054, Apr 2025): generalization = *new homes*: "three kitchens and three bedrooms in real homes that were not seen during training" plus "mock home environments" for "controlled, reproducible quantitative comparisons"; "10 trials per policy and task" in mock homes; metric = "percentage of steps in each task that were completed successfully"; language following measured by "language following rate ... how often the robot selects the object indicated" vs "success rate"; object axis split into in-distribution categories (new instances) vs out-of-distribution categories; environment-scaling study with 3/12/22/53/82/104 training locations; averages only, no CIs. — [arXiv HTML 2504.16054](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054v1)
- Gemini Robotics (Google DeepMind, arXiv:2503.20020, Mar 2025): generalization benchmark of "85 tasks in total, of which 20% are within the training distribution, 28% evaluate visual generalization, 28% evaluate instruction generalization, and 24% evaluate action generalization"; visual = new distractors/background/lighting; instruction = typos, new language, rephrasing/level of detail; action = different initial positions, new object instances; metrics = binary success and a continuous "progress score"; long-horizon dexterous specialists evaluated with "20 trials per task for each model" (12 for the spelling game); report cites "A/B testing and statistical analysis" but gives few details. — [arXiv HTML 2503.20020](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020)
- GR00T N1 (NVIDIA, arXiv:2503.14734, Mar 2025): GR-1 humanoid, 13 real tasks in 4 categories (object-to-container pick-and-place 5, articulated 3, industrial 3, multi-agent coordination 2); "average success rate over 10 trials for each task" (5 for Pack Machinery with a 30 s limit and object-count scoring); partial scoring "to capture model behavior across different execution phases"; 10%-data vs full-data settings; sim benchmarks (RoboCasa, DexMimicGen, GR-1 Tabletop) as complement. — [arXiv HTML 2503.14734](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734)
- Experiences-from-Benchmarking (Zhang/Qi/Zheng 2025) uses a compact 3-level adaptability axis: ID, spatial-OOD (seen objects), instance+spatial OOD (unseen objects). — [arXiv HTML 2511.11298](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11298v1)
- VLA-REPLICA (Huang, Zhang, Tang, Xiang; UT Dallas; arXiv:2605.20774, May 2026) — primarily a physical benchmark (other slice), but its OOD axes are a useful minimal template: object colour (ID red/blue/purple → OOD yellow/green), object count (2–3 → 3–4), memory/counting repetitions (1,3 → 2,4,5), spatial deviation from training peaks; 50 ID + 40 OOD scenes, 5 runs each. — [arXiv HTML 2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1)
- ATOM-Bench (BAAI FlagEval; arXiv:2606.16826, June 2026; real-world) factorises tabletop manipulation into "motor atoms and instruction atoms"; 30 atomic + 24 held-out compositional tasks; metrics = Atomic Score (AS) and Compositional Failure Share (CFS) separating weak-skill failures from failed recombination; 2,700 physical rollouts over five policies. — [arXiv 2606.16826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16826)

**Sim-only taxonomies frequently borrowed for real-world protocols (brief contrast)**
- GemBench (Garcia et al., "Towards Generalizable Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation: A Benchmark and LLM-guided 3D Policy", arXiv:2410.01345; ICRA 2025 — venue from memory): 4 levels — L1 new placements (31 variations), L2 novel rigid objects (28), L3 novel articulated objects (21), L4 unseen long-horizon tasks (12). — [arXiv 2410.01345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01345)
- VLABench (Zhang et al., arXiv:2412.18194, Dec 2024): 100 task categories, 2000+ objects; competencies = mesh & texture understanding, spatial relationship, semantic instruction, physical laws, knowledge transfer/reasoning, long-horizon reasoning; progress-score metric. — [arXiv 2412.18194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18194)
- LIBERO-Plus (Fei et al., arXiv:2510.13626; CVPR 2026 per search): 10,030 perturbed tasks over 7 dimensions (camera viewpoint, robot initial state, language, lighting, background texture, sensor noise, object layout); success can fall "from 95% to below 30%" under camera/initial-state shifts. — [arXiv PDF 2510.13626](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13626)
- INT-ACT (NYU AI4CE, arXiv:2506.09930, 2025): SimplerEnv-based probes in three categories — object diversity, language complexity, vision-language thinking; conclusion that VLM "intentions" survive but execution does not. — [project page](https://ai4ce.github.io/INT-ACT/)
- Eva-VLA (arXiv:2509.18953, 2025): turns physical variation into continuous optimisation over 3D object transforms, illumination, adversarial regions; >60% failure rates on OpenVLA, up to 97.8% in long-horizon. — [arXiv 2509.18953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18953)
- EBench (Shanghai AI Lab et al., arXiv:2606.18239, 2026; simulation): 26 mobile-manipulation tasks diagnosed along 5 capability + 4 generalization dimensions. — [arXiv 2606.18239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18239)

### Inferences
- Across papers the *names* differ (OpenVLA: visual/motion/physical/semantic/language-grounding; Gemini: visual/instruction/action; STAR-Gen: visual/semantic/behavioral; Colosseum V2: visual/language/action) but they map onto the same three modalities (observation, instruction, required action); STAR-Gen is the natural "Rosetta stone" for an awesome list.
- Factor-difficulty orderings are consistent across independent studies: camera pose and texture shifts are hardest, background/lighting/distractors easier (Xie et al. real robot; LIBERO-Plus sim; Colosseum identifies distractors+colour+lighting as largest in its setting — so distractor sensitivity depends on training-data diversity).
- Only a minority of protocols (π0.5 real homes, DROID 4 locations, BridgeData V2 second lab, LBM "station shift") test *environment/site* generalization; most "generalization" is within one evaluation cell.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the exact mapping from Colosseum's "14 axes" to its 12 named factors (my interpretation only).
- GemBench/INT-ACT/LIBERO-Plus venues and some policy-paper venues (RT-1, RT-2, OXE, Octo, OpenVLA, DROID, π0) are from memory, not confirmed on the opened pages.
- No real-world study found that measures *embodiment* generalization (STAR-Gen VB-ROB) with controlled protocols beyond OXE's cross-robot aggregates.

## Key Question 3 — Metrics beyond binary success (who proposed/popularised each?)

### Takeaway
Partial-credit progress scores (π0, Gemini Robotics, RoboArena, LBM "Task Completion"), time-to-success / throughput (Experiences-from-Benchmarking; π*0.6 "tasks per hour"), MTBF-style continuous-operation reliability (DYNA-1, π*0.6 deployments), pairwise preference with Bradley–Terry ranking (RoboArena), VLM-based success detection (Du et al. 2023 → AutoEval → StepEval; reliability capped ~0.77 balanced accuracy per FailBench) and chained-task metrics (SayCan plan vs. execution success; CALVIN average chain length) are the main alternatives — but no paper yet standardises them for real-world VLA reporting.
(한국어 요약: 부분점수/진행도, 시간·처리량, 연속운용 신뢰성(MTBF류), 쌍대선호+BT 랭킹, VLM 자동판정, 체인 태스크 지표가 주요 대안이나 표준화는 안 됨.)

### Cited Findings

**Task progress / partial credit / rubrics**
- Kress-Gazit et al. 2024 propose task-specific *rubrics* (subgoal checklists) and Signal Temporal Logic specs with quantitative robustness as the "semantic" and "performance" complements to success. — [arXiv HTML 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491)
- π0 (2024) popularised the per-task "normalized score" (1.0 full success, fractional partial success) with published rubrics (bussing: 1 pt per correctly sorted object of 7; microwave: 4 milestones). — [arXiv HTML 2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164v3)
- π0.5 (2025): "percentage of steps in each task that were completed successfully" plus a separate language-following rate. — [arXiv HTML 2504.16054](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054v1)
- Gemini Robotics (2025): "progress score, a metric that provides a more continuous measure than binary task success"; per-task definitions in Appendix C.1.3. — [arXiv HTML 2503.20020](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020)
- TRI LBM (2025): "Task Completion (TC)" = completed milestones / total milestones, analysed with Dirichlet posteriors and Welch's t-test; QA re-scoring discrepancy 6.25% on rubric questions vs 2.31% on success. — [arXiv HTML 2507.05331](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331)
- GR00T N1 (2025) uses "a partial scoring system to capture model behavior across different execution phases"; OpenVLA (2024) allows 0.5 partial credit on some tasks. — [GR00T N1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734); [OpenVLA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246v3)
- RoboArena (Atreya, Pertsch, Lee et al., 21 authors / 8 institutions; CoRL 2025; arXiv:2506.18123) asks evaluators for a continuous progress score "∈[0…100]", a binary preference and free-text explanation per pairwise episode. — [arXiv HTML 2506.18123](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v1)
- "Beyond Binary Success" (2026) shows statistically that partial-credit progress metrics separate policies faster than binary success. — [arXiv 2603.13616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616)
- RoboEval (Wang et al., UW/AI2/NVIDIA; arXiv:2507.00435, 2025, simulation) instruments every task with "standardized metrics that quantify efficiency, coordination, and safety/stability, as well as outcome measures that trace stagewise progress and localize failure modes" — the most complete *sim* template for structured metrics. — [arXiv 2507.00435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00435)
- NVIDIA perspective (Yang et al. 2025) lists discrete (completion/success/failure modes), continuous (smoothness, optimality, path length) and temporal (inference time, episode duration) metrics as a recommended set. — [arXiv HTML 2508.11117](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11117)

**Time, throughput, reliability (MTBF-style), interventions**
- Experiences-from-Benchmarking (2025) adds Time-to-Success (TTS) and Instruction-following Accuracy (IAA) alongside SR, each with Wilson 95% CIs. — [arXiv HTML 2511.11298](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11298v1)
- π*0.6 / RECAP (Physical Intelligence, arXiv:2511.14759, Nov 2025) defines throughput as "the number of successfully completed tasks per hour" and reports that RECAP "more than doubles task throughput and roughly halves the task failure rate" on the hardest tasks; success criteria are time-boxed per task (e.g., espresso: all steps within 200 s without critical errors; box assembly within 600 s); long-duration evidence: espresso for ~13 h continuous, laundry "fold 50 different novel laundry items in a new home", 59 boxes in a factory. — [arXiv HTML 2511.14759](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14759); [PI blog](https://www.pi.website/blog/pistar06)
- DYNA-1 (Dyna Robotics, 2025 research post) reports "99.4%" task success, "850+" napkins in 24 h, "zero interventions, full shift reliability", at "~60%" of human speed — an industry-style continuous-operation metric set (success × throughput × interventions over a shift). — [dyna.co/research/dyna-1](https://www.dyna.co/research/dyna-1)
- The AutoEval system (Zhou, Atreya, Tan, Pertsch, Levine; UC Berkeley; CoRL 2025; arXiv:2503.24278) reports its own reliability as interventions per 24 h (3 human interventions over ~850 episodes). Intervention counting as a metric for *policies* is established in HRI (Steinfeld et al. 2006 "Common Metrics for Human-Robot Interaction": interventions per unit time) but I found no VLA paper that adopts a formal MTBF/MTBI definition for policy comparison. — [AutoEval HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); [Steinfeld et al. 2006](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub4/steinfeld_aaron_m_2006_1/steinfeld_aaron_m_2006_1.pdf)

**Pairwise preference + ranking models**
- RB2 (2021) was first to argue that only *rankings* (not absolute numbers) transfer across labs; local Tukey-HSD rankings pooled by Plackett–Luce. — [arXiv 2203.08098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08098)
- RoboArena (2025) introduced double-blind pairwise A/B with identical initial conditions within a pair and a task-difficulty-aware Bradley–Terry model (policy ability θ, task difficulty τ, policy–task interaction ψ) fit by EM with clipped Newton updates rather than online Elo; 612 pairwise comparisons / 4,284 episodes across 7 institutions on DROID; "converges to high-quality rankings within just 100 pairwise comparisons" and correlates better with oracle rankings than conventional fixed-task evaluation (Pearson + MMRV). — [arXiv HTML 2506.18123](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v1)
- Ranking-fidelity metrics MMRV (mean maximum rank violation) and Pearson r — introduced by SIMPLER ("Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation", arXiv:2405.05941) for sim-vs-real agreement and reused by AutoEval (Pearson 0.942, MMRV 0.015 vs human evals) and RoboArena. — [SIMPLER](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05941v1); [AutoEval HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)

**VLM-based automatic success detection / scoring**
- Origin: "Vision-Language Models as Success Detectors" — Yuqing Du, Ksenia Konyushkova, Misha Denil, Akhil Raju, Jessica Landon, Felix Hill, Nando de Freitas, Serkan Cabi (DeepMind). arXiv:2303.07280 (Mar 2023; CoLLAs 2023 — venue from memory). Frames success detection as VQA ("SuccessVQA") with Flamingo; beats bespoke reward models OOD on sim household and real-robot manipulation. — [arXiv 2303.07280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07280)
- AutoEval (2025) fine-tunes a VLM (PaliGemma) on ~1000 teleop images per task for binary success; ">95%" held-out accuracy, ~96% during 24-h runs; 50 rollouts per policy per task; ">99%" human-time reduction. — [AutoEval HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)
- StepEval — "Score the Steps, Not Just the Goal: VLM-Based Subgoal Evaluation for Robotic Manipulation" — Ramy ElMallah, Krish Chhajer, Chi-Guhn Lee (Toronto). CoRL 2025 Eval&Deploy workshop. arXiv:2509.19524. Produces a per-subgoal binary success vector per trajectory → per-subgoal success rates, model-agnostic offline process. — [arXiv 2509.19524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19524)
- FailBench — "How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?" — Zaruhi Navasardyan, Tatul Danielyan, Hrant Davtyan. arXiv:2609.03611 (Sept 2026). 2,197 attempts from 14 sources (12 real); best of 13 VLM detectors reaches "only 0.77 mean balanced accuracy"; "<0.60" on contact-intensive assembly; "systematic bias toward predicting success under ambiguous evidence"; fine-tuned failure detectors underperform general VLMs. — [arXiv 2609.03611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03611)
- "Robot Critics that Sweat the Small Stuff" — Sruthi Sudhakar, Junbang Liang, Sreehari Rammohan, Pavel Tokmakov, Richard Zemel, Carl Vondrick (Columbia/TRI). arXiv:2606.21572 (June 2026). VLM critics struggle with "small visual differences between success and failure"; refines critics with paired success/failure supervision. — [arXiv 2606.21572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21572)
- The "No Free Checker" survey (2026) is the systematic map of this space (credibility vs availability). — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)

**Long-horizon / chained-task metrics**
- SayCan ("Do As I Can, Not As I Say", arXiv:2204.01691, 2022): 101 instructions from 7 families; "plan success rate" (correct skill sequence regardless of execution) vs "execution success rate" (whole system succeeds): mock kitchen 84%/74%, real kitchen 81%/60%. This plan-vs-execution decomposition is the standard long-horizon split reused by RT-1 (L1–L3 kitchens). — [arXiv HTML 2204.01691](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01691)
- CALVIN (Mees, Hermann, Rosete-Beas, Burgard; RA-L 2022; arXiv:2112.03227; simulation): LH-MTLC protocol with 1000 chains of 5 instructions; policy advances "only if the agent successfully completes the current task"; reported as success per chain length and average completed length (baseline MCIL 48.9% first subtask → 0.08% full chain). The "average chain length" convention migrated to real-world multi-stage reporting (e.g., π0.5 step-percentage; Jiang et al. 2026 use CALVIN ATC). — [arXiv HTML 2112.03227](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.03227)
- Real-world long-horizon practice: π0 "complex multi-stage tasks" scored by milestone rubric; Gemini Robotics long-horizon dexterous tasks with 20 trials and progress score; π*0.6 time-boxed success + throughput. (See above citations.)

### Inferences
- Partial-credit "progress" has become the de-facto second metric in frontier-lab papers since 2024, but rubric *definitions* are rarely released in machine-readable form; the LBM QA numbers (6.25% rubric disagreement vs 2.31% success disagreement) quantify that rubric scoring is less reliable than binary success.
- Throughput/MTBF-style metrics appear only in deployment-oriented reports (π*0.6, DYNA-1) and are not yet used for cross-paper comparison; an awesome list could flag this as an open standardisation need.
- VLM judges are now good enough for *coarse* binary success in fixed scenes (AutoEval ~96%) but FailBench shows ~0.77 balanced accuracy in the general case, so human verification remains the credible gold standard for headline claims.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed robot-learning paper found that formally defines MTBF / mean-time-between-interventions for manipulation policy comparison (only company posts and HRI metrics).
- Did not locate a paper that benchmarks inter-rater reliability of human *progress-score* annotation beyond the LBM QA figures.

## Key Question 4 — Typical evaluation scale and statistical validity; sources of irreproducibility

### Takeaway
Most major real-world papers run 10 trials per task per policy (BridgeData V2, Octo, OpenVLA, DROID, π0, π0.5, GR00T N1), 5 in some (OpenVLA Google robot, STAR-Gen, VLA-REPLICA) and 20 (Gemini Robotics, Colosseum V2) or 50 (TRI LBM, AutoEval, Zhang et al. 2025) in the more rigorous ones; with n = 10 a 95% CI on a 70% success rate spans roughly 40–89%, and a 20-point gap needs ~50–75 trials per policy to detect with 80% power, so most reported differences are not statistically resolvable. Documented irreproducibility sources are lighting, camera pose, object placement, hardware calibration/wear, prompt wording, environmental drift across sessions and evaluator bias.
(한국어 요약: 대부분 태스크당 10회; n=10에서 70% 성공률의 95% CI는 약 40~89%로 매우 넓음. 20%p 차이를 검출하려면 정책당 50~75회 필요. 조명·카메라 포즈·물체 배치·하드웨어 캘리브레이션/마모·프롬프트 문구·세션 간 드리프트·평가자 편향이 문서화된 비재현성 원인.)

### Cited Findings

**Trial counts actually used (per task per policy, real world)**
- 10: BridgeData V2 (20 in scaling study) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12952); Octo — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12213); OpenVLA WidowX (5 on Google robot) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246v3); DROID — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12945); π0 — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164v3); π0.5 mock homes — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054v1); GR00T N1 (5 for one timed task) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734).
- 5: STAR-Gen BridgeV2-STAR (5 per condition, 885 total) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01238v3); RT-2 emergent instructions (5×; generalization 1–5×) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15818); VLA-REPLICA (5 runs per scene) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1).
- 12 (2 trials × 6 tasks per factor): Xie et al. 2023 real robot — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03659).
- 20: Gemini Robotics (12 for one task) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020); Colosseum V2 real — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27759); Kress-Gazit shirt-folding example (20/policy) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491).
- 30 (10 episodes × 3 runs per condition): COLOSSEUM real — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08191v1).
- 50: TRI LBM (per task per policy per condition; 1,800 real rollouts total) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331); AutoEval — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1); Zhang/Qi/Zheng 2025 — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11298v1); STEP hardware tasks (50; 100 for CarrotOnPlate) — [link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10966).
- Aggregate campaign sizes: RT-1 >3,000 trials; RT-2 ~6,000; OXE 3,600 across 6 robots; Liao et al. 2,331; ATOM-Bench 2,700; RoboArena 4,284 episodes.
- STEP's characterisation of the field: "only a small number of trials (e.g., 10–60)" because of "the large human effort needed to reset the environment between trials and the substantial wall-clock time imposed by limited inference throughput of large policies". — [arXiv HTML 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10966)

**What the statistics papers say this buys you**
- STEP: with 100 paired trials a 9-point gap (Octo-Base vs OpenVLA on CarrotOnPlate) was *not* significant and ~500 trials would be needed; 36–52-point gaps resolve in 7–23 trials. — [arXiv HTML 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10966)
- Kress-Gazit et al.: 15/18 vs 11/17 leaves an 11% posterior probability that the "worse" policy is better; "Reducing the number of evaluations makes any conclusions weaker". — [arXiv HTML 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491)
- Jiang et al. 2026 (sim audit): only ~20% of SOTA claims on LIBERO/SimplerEnv are provably significant — illustrating the same issue in the sim leaderboards that feed real-world claims. — [arXiv HTML 2606.04233](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04233v1)
- Reporting practice: OpenVLA and DROID report ± standard error over pooled rollouts; π0, π0.5, Octo, GR00T N1 report means only; Zhang/Qi/Zheng use Wilson CIs; LBM uses Bayesian posteriors + sequential tests + CLD; Gemini Robotics mentions "A/B testing and statistical analysis" without details. (Citations as above.)

**Initial-condition control, blinding, evaluator effects**
- Image-overlay initial-condition matching: recommended by Kress-Gazit et al. ("image overlays or markings in the scene") and used operationally by TRI LBM ("matching the robot's environment to the overlay") and VLA-REPLICA (reference-image overlay program, AprilTag camera calibration to ±0.01 m / ±1°, 5600 K light box). — [2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491); [2507.05331](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331); [2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1)
- Drift monitoring: STAR-Gen compares live observations against reference demonstration images and re-adjusts the scene when drift is detected. — [arXiv HTML 2503.01238v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01238v3)
- Blind / interleaved A/B: RT-2 ("all four models are evaluated one after another in the exact same conditions"), OpenVLA ("A/B evaluations ... same sets of initial robot and object states"), TRI LBM (blind, randomized test bundles), RoboArena (double-blind pairs; evaluators see only policy-server IPs). — citations above.
- Evaluator bias quantified: LBM QA re-scoring found 2.31% disagreement on success and 6.25% on rubric items; AutoEval does *not* report inter-rater variance (treats human eval as oracle). — [2507.05331](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331); [2503.24278](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v1)
- Role separation: Kress-Gazit et al. recommend that the demonstrator not be the evaluator and that one consistent person/group run all evaluations. — [2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491)

**Documented irreproducibility sources**
- Cross-lab setup variance: RB2 measured a 20% change in pouring metrics between two labs. — [arXiv 2203.08098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08098)
- Camera pose is the hardest factor (Xie et al. real robot; LIBERO-Plus 95%→<30%); textures/table position next; backgrounds/lighting/distractors easier but data-dependent. — [2307.03659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03659); [LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13626)
- Lighting and camera changes cost 30–50% success (COLOSSEUM; NVIDIA perspective). — [2402.08191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191); [2508.11117](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11117)
- Environmental drift within/between sessions (sunlight, background) motivates single-session A/B (Kress-Gazit) and explains why π0.5 uses mock homes for quantitative numbers while real homes are the final qualitative check. — [2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491); [2504.16054](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054v1)
- Hardware: VLA-REPLICA names servo-offset differences across SO-101 arms and normalises via calibration matrices; a 2026 paper on "Taming VLAs under Robot Execution Errors" evaluates on "a new arm and another used for one year" (search snippet only; arXiv 2609.37334, not opened). — [2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1)
- Prompt wording: Penn π0 study (100% vs 0% from a paraphrase); STAR-Gen semantic axes; Colosseum V2 language perturbations. — [Penn blog](https://penn-pal-lab.github.io/Pi0-Experiment-in-the-Wild/)
- Sim-real agreement for perturbation *effects* is moderate (COLOSSEUM R² 0.614; Colosseum V2 absolute R² 0.49 but relative effects preserved), so sim robustness numbers are only a partial proxy. — [2402.08191](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08191v1); [2605.27759](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27759)
- Cross-site reproduction success case: VLA-REPLICA second setup built by a novice reproduced ID 0.49 vs 0.48 and OOD 0.55 vs 0.50 average success. — [2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1)

### Inferences (own calculations, flagged)
- Binomial CIs at common trial counts (computed by me with Wilson and Clopper–Pearson at 95%): 7/10 → Wilson 0.40–0.89 (CP 0.35–0.93); 5/10 → 0.24–0.76; 10/10 → 0.72–1.00; 14/20 → 0.48–0.86; 35/50 → 0.56–0.81; 70/100 → 0.60–0.78. I.e., a "70% vs 90%" headline at n = 10 (7/10 vs 9/10) has a one-sided Fisher exact p ≈ 0.29; the same proportions at n = 50 give p ≈ 0.011.
- Approximate per-policy sample sizes for a one-sided two-proportion test (α = 0.05, power 0.8, normal approximation, computed by me): 30-pt gap (50→80%) ≈ 30 trials; 20-pt gap (50→70%) ≈ 73, (70→90%) ≈ 48; 10-pt gap (60→70%) ≈ 280, (80→90%) ≈ 157. These are consistent with STEP's empirical finding that a 9-pt gap was unresolved at 100 trials.
- Therefore the de-facto 10-trial protocol can only reliably certify differences of roughly ≥40 points; multi-task aggregation (e.g., OpenVLA's 170 pooled rollouts) is what makes the frontier-lab headline comparisons meaningful, and only when tasks are paired across policies.

### Gaps
- No systematic survey quantifying how many real-world VLA papers report CIs or trial counts (the only audit, Jiang et al. 2026, covers sim benchmarks).
- Could not open arXiv 2609.37334 (hardware-wear VLA paper) or the TRI blog to extract their specific numbers.
- No study found that isolates *operator/teleoperator* effects on evaluation outcomes (as opposed to on data collection).

## Key Question 5 — Existing surveys and awesome-lists that already categorise real-world manipulation evaluation (2024–2026), and how to position a new list

### Takeaway
No existing list is dedicated to *real-world* manipulation evaluation methodology: general manipulation lists bury "real-world evaluation" as a sub-bullet of benchmarks, benchmark lists are domain- or simulator-centric, the one "embodied evaluation" list contains fabricated entries, and the closest curated resources are the verifier reading list (ZJUSCL) and the 2026 surveys on VLA datasets/benchmarks and world-model evaluation. A list organised by (a) physical kits, (b) hosted/remote platforms, (c) real-to-sim proxies, (d) methodology/statistics/taxonomies would be novel.
(한국어 요약: 실세계 평가 방법론 전용 리스트는 없음. 기존 리스트는 시뮬/데이터셋 중심이고 실세계 평가는 하위 항목 수준. 제안된 4분류 구조는 차별성이 있음.)

### Cited Findings
- **BaiShuanghao/Awesome-Robotics-Manipulation** (GitHub; companion surveys "Towards a Unified Understanding of Robot Manipulation: A Comprehensive Survey", arXiv:2510.10903, and the T-RO version "Embodied Robot Manipulation in the Era of Foundation Models", arXiv:2512.22983). Six top-level sections (tasks; high-level planning; low-level action modeling; bottlenecks; applications; simulators/benchmarks/datasets). The benchmark section splits into grasp datasets, single-embodiment simulators (single-arm, bimanual, long-horizon, generalization, robustness), **single-embodiment real-world evaluation**, cross-embodiment simulators/benchmarks, trajectory datasets, embodied-QA/affordance datasets, video benchmarks; real-to-sim evaluation is only implicit. Entries include IMBench, REALM, RoboArena, SIMPLER, RoboDojo, DuoBench, RoboTwin. — [GitHub](https://github.com/BaiShuanghao/Awesome-Robotics-Manipulation)
- **HaoranZhangumich/Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks** (GitHub, 17 stars, "under development"): 8 domains (manipulation, locomotion, navigation, HRI, safety, simulation, generalist, other) with comparable tables (task count, metrics, robots, sensor modalities, simulator backend, data-source type: human_demonstration / motion_planner / synthetic, dataset size); real vs sim is encoded via data-source/simulator columns rather than as a category. — [GitHub](https://github.com/HaoranZhangumich/Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks)
- **zwq2018/awesome-embodied-evaluation-CookeyLab** (GitHub): headings Benchmarks (general manipulation; long-horizon & multi-task; VLA; simulation environments), Evaluation Tools & Frameworks, Evaluation Papers (methodology; metrics & analysis; generalization & robustness), Datasets, Leaderboards, Standards & Reports. **Caution:** its "Evaluation Methodology" entries pair titles with wrong arXiv IDs — e.g., it lists "Toward Standardized Evaluation of Robotic Manipulation" as arXiv 2204.12509, which is actually a quantum-key-distribution satellite paper — so the list appears partly machine-generated and should not be relied on. — [GitHub](https://github.com/zwq2018/awesome-embodied-evaluation-CookeyLab); [arXiv 2204.12509 (actual content)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12509)
- **ZJUSCL/Awesome-Robot-Verifier** — reading list for "No Free Checker: A Survey of Verifiers for Robot Policies" (arXiv:2609.09250, Sept 2026): organises ~150 verifiers by judgment source (human / rule-based & formal / learned & pretrained / model-intrinsic) and by availability vs credibility. - **natnew/awesome-physical-ai** and **RayYoh/Awesome-Robot-Learning** and **DravenALG/awesome-vla-wam** — general Physical-AI / robot-learning / VLA lists that include benchmark sections but no evaluation-methodology taxonomy (surfaced by search; headings not individually verified). — [awesome-physical-ai](https://github.com/natnew/awesome-physical-ai); [Awesome-Robot-Learning](https://github.com/RayYoh/Awesome-Robot-Learning); [awesome-vla-wam](https://github.com/DravenALG/awesome-vla-wam)

Three pillars (datasets, benchmarks, data engines); categorises corpora by embodiment diversity, modality composition, action-space formulation; analyses benchmark "task complexity and environment structure jointly" and flags gaps in compositional-generalization and long-horizon evaluation; little on real-world protocol statistics. — [arXiv 2604.23001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23001)

Catalogues 160 benchmarks (2017–2026) in four lanes (policy suites, embodied agents, world-model evaluation, prediction-to-action bridges) by evaluation mode, capability and model family; 138/160 model-agnostic, only 11 compare VLA vs world models. — [arXiv 2609.29669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29669)

- Survey: "A Survey of Robotic Navigation and Manipulation with Physics Simulators in the Era of Embodied AI" (arXiv:2505.01458, 2025) — simulator-centric; notes "Real-world trials have lower throughput but higher external validity". — [arXiv HTML 2505.01458](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01458v1)
- Perspective with taxonomy: NVIDIA "Robot Policy Evaluation for Sim-to-Real Transfer: A Benchmarking Perspective" (arXiv:2508.11117) — task levels T1–T4 × five perturbation axes × metric families; a usable column schema for a list. — [arXiv HTML 2508.11117](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11117)
- Methodology-centric community resources: RSS 2025 Robot Evaluation Workshop; CoRL 2025 Eval&Deploy workshop (accepted-paper list is itself a mini-catalogue of 2025 evaluation methods). — [CFP](https://groups.google.com/g/ml-news/c/-ZPMa7ZoJKk); [eval-deploy.github.io](https://eval-deploy.github.io/)

### Inferences
- Existing lists categorise by *domain* (manipulation/locomotion/...), by *simulator*, or by *data source*; none categorise by *evaluation protocol properties* (trial count, IC control, blinding, statistics, metric type, generalization axes). A table with those columns — populated from the protocol facts in Key Questions 2–4 — would be the clearest differentiator for the user's list.
- The verifier survey/list (ZJUSCL) should be cross-linked rather than duplicated; likewise the TRI protocol papers can be grouped as a "statistics & protocol toolkit" sub-section with links to released code (STEP, stochastic_verification bounds, rliable).

### Gaps
- Did not verify headings for awesome-physical-ai, Awesome-Robot-Learning, awesome-vla-wam beyond search snippets.
- No survey found (as of 2026-10-01) that focuses specifically on *real-world* evaluation protocols of VLA/generalist policies; the closest are the NVIDIA perspective (sim-to-real framing) and the TRI best-practice paper.
- GitHub star counts / last-update dates were not captured except for Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks (17 stars).
