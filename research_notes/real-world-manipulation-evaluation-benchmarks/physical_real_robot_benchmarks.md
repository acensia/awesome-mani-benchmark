# Physical (Lab-Reproducible) Real-Robot Manipulation Benchmarks — Catalog as of Oct 2026

Scope note: this file covers benchmarks that a lab reproduces *physically on its own robot(s)*. Remote/cloud/crowdsourced evaluation (RoboArena, RoboChallenge, AutoEval, TOTO, Real Robot Challenge, OCRTOC, EBiM, Humanoid Everyday cloud eval), real-to-sim proxies (SIMPLER, RobotArena∞, PolaRiS, REALM, X2Real), and evaluation-statistics work are other researchers' slices and get at most a one-line flag here. Sim-only benchmarks that surfaced during search (GemBench, Colosseum V2, EBench, RoboCerebra, RoboTwin, BiCoord, DexVerse, DexJoCo, RGBench, LeHome) are explicitly excluded from the main catalog and noted in the Gaps.

Verification status: every entry below was checked by opening the arXiv abstract/HTML page, the project page, the NIST page, or Semantic Scholar metadata during this session unless marked "(unverified)". Citation counts are from the Semantic Scholar Graph API batch query run 2026-10-01 and are approximate adoption proxies only.

## KQ1. Classic object-set / protocol benchmarks — what do they standardize?

### Takeaway
The "classic" generation (2015–2023) standardizes *objects* (YCB, ACRV APB, EGAD, Household Cloth Object Set, CEPB), *physical artifacts* (NIST Assembly Task Boards, Robothon electronic task board, Box-and-Blocks box, GRASPA printable layouts), and *written protocols + metrics* (the 14-paper IEEE RA-L 2020 Special Issue on Benchmarking Protocols for Robotic Manipulation). They are hardware-agnostic by design: the lab brings any arm/hand and follows the protocol. Reproducibility comes from purchasable/printable objects, placement stencils or printable layouts, and clinically borrowed scoring (BBT, SHAP), not from fixing the robot.

### Cited Findings

#### YCB Object and Model Set + Benchmarking Protocols (Yale–CMU–Berkeley)
- Paper: "Benchmarking in Manipulation Research: The YCB Object and Model Set and Benchmarking Protocols," Calli, Walsman, Singh, Srinivasa, Abbeel, Dollar; IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine 22 (2015) 36–52; objects of daily life with different shapes, sizes, textures, weight and rigidity plus "some widely used manipulation tests"; provides high-resolution RGB-D scans, physical properties, geometric models, and "a framework and a number of example task protocols" — [arXiv 1502.03143](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03143)
- The companion dataset paper "Yale-CMU-Berkeley dataset for robotic manipulation research" (IJRR 2017) has ~465 citations and the RAM paper ~333 — the most-cited physical manipulation benchmark artifacts in this catalog — [Semantic Scholar batch query, 2026-10-01](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)
- The official site advertises the set as 77 standardized household objects — [ycbbenchmarks.com object breakdown page](https://www.ycbbenchmarks.com/wp-content/pgs/ycb_object_set__a_breakdown_of_its_77_benchmark_objects.html); main site: [ycbbenchmarks.com](http://www.ycbbenchmarks.com/)
- Secondary summary: the YCB project introduced a Manipulation Protocol Template requiring researchers to specify initial robot state, exact object placement and success metrics, and the community has extended it with protocols for hand design, bimanual manipulation, grasp resilience, grasp planning, in-hand manipulation, pick-and-place, aerial manipulation, picking-in-clutter, Rubik's cube and assembly — [Emergent Mind summary (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/ycb-object-and-model-set). - Task types: grasping, pick-and-place, in-hand, bimanual, assembly (via the RA-L special issue below). Hardware: none fixed.
- Adoption: YCB objects are reused as the object vocabulary by SceneReplica (16 YCB objects), Cruciani in-hand benchmark (YCB meshes), ManipulationNet grasping-in-clutter (16 YCB objects), CEPB (subset of 40 objects from YCB + APB + T-LESS), AHAP and Elliott–Connolly hand benchmarks — see entries below.

#### NIST Assembly Task Boards (ATB 1–4) + MOAD
- Four boards: ATB#1 "peg insertions, gear meshing, electrical connector insertions, and nut threading"; ATB#2 "alignment and insertion of collars and pulleys, handling flexible parts, meshing/threading belts, actuating tensioners, and threading bolts"; ATB#3 cable routing and connector insertion ("tracking, placement, and weaving loose parts"); ATB#4 wire-harness assembly (routing, grouping, bundling, harness removal). Each board ships with fabrication instructions, benchmark templates, test protocols, laser-cut designs, CAD, STL files, kit trays, performance data, statistical-analysis guidelines and scoring metrics. Used in IROS 2017 RGMC, World Robot Summit 2018, IROS 2020 Manufacturing Track, ICRA 2022 RGMC — [NIST assembly page](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/robotic-grasping-and-manipulation-assembly/assembly)
- Protocol paper: Kimble, Falco, Messina, Van Wyk, Sun, Shibata, Uemura, Yokokohji, "Benchmarking Protocols for Evaluating Small Parts Robotic Assembly Systems," IEEE RA-L 2020, doi 10.1109/LRA.2020.2965869; ~110 citations — [NIST publication page](https://www.nist.gov/publications/benchmarking-protocols-evaluating-small-parts-robotic-assembly-systems); [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)
- Deformable extension (ATB4 / belts / cables): "Performance measures to benchmark the grasping, manipulation, and assembly of deformable objects typical to manufacturing applications," Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2022, doi 10.3389/frobt.2022.999348 — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2022.999348/full)
- NIST states the MOAD dataset "will reduce the barrier of entry for the usage of the NIST ATBs and enable the development of novel solutions for robot manipulation for flexible material assemblies" — [NIST benchmarks & datasets page](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/benchmarks-and-datasets-tackle-real-world-robotic)
- Downstream reuse: REASSEMBLE dataset is "built around the NIST Assembly Task Board 1" (see KQ2); ManipulationNet's peg-in-hole kits and Peg-in-Bench (KQ3) use the same 3 mm / 1 mm / 0.1 mm / 0.02 mm clearance ladder; Industrial Dexterity Benchmark (KQ3) cites the NIST boards as lineage.
- Task types: assembly / insertion / fastening / cable routing (DLO). Hardware: none fixed. Metrics: completion time, scored sub-tasks.

#### Hand-level dexterity protocols (for completeness)

- "Benchmarking Multi-Object Grasping," Chen, Frumento, Pagnanelli, Cei, Keth, Gafarov, Gong, Ye, Baracca, D'Avella, Bianchi, Sun (USF/Pisa/Sant'Anna), arXiv Mar 2025, "under review for a robotics journal": three protocols — Only-Pick-Once, Accurate pick-transferring, Pick-transferring-all — for pile and surface scenarios — [arXiv 2503.20820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20820)

### Inferences
- The classic generation deliberately trades specificity for generality (the RA-L 2020 editors' own framing): hardware is never fixed, so cross-lab comparability rests on object sets and written protocols; this is why most of them report *baselines on the authors' own robot* rather than multi-lab leaderboards.
- The only classic artifacts with a visible multi-institution footprint are YCB (object vocabulary reused by at least six later benchmarks), the NIST ATBs (competitions 2017–2022, REASSEMBLE, ManipulationNet, Peg-in-Bench) and EGAD (182 citations). GRASPA, BBT, CEPB, HCOS, in-hand and bimanual-cloth protocols sit in the 24–86 citation range, consistent with "known but rarely re-run outside originating groups."

## KQ2. Reproducible real-robot *learning* benchmarks with standardized hardware or 3D-printable setups (2019–2025)

### Takeaway
From 2019 on, benchmarks began fixing the *whole cell*: low-cost arms (REPLAB ~$2k, ROBEL, TriFinger ~$5k), or a widely owned arm (Franka Panda) plus 3D-printed task objects (FurnitureBench, FMB, RAMP, IndustReal), plus pre-collected demonstration datasets so a lab can compare learning algorithms. FurnitureBench (219 citations; 10 external reproductions reported with <16% performance gap) and FMB (127 citations) are the most adopted. Several items often listed as "benchmarks" are *not* lab-reproducible real-world protocols: GemBench and Colosseum V2 are sim-only, BEHAVIOR Robot Suite is a hardware/teleop/policy framework, and THE COLOSSEUM is a sim benchmark whose real-world side is a 3D-printable perturbation replication, not a standalone protocol.

### Cited Findings

#### REPLAB (Berkeley / UPenn) — ICRA 2019
- Yang, Zhang, Pong, Levine, Jayaraman; "reproducible and self-contained hardware stack (robot arm, camera, and workspace) that costs about 2000 USD, occupies a cuboid of size 70x40x60 cm, and permits full assembly within a few hours"; grasping benchmark template + 92k grasp attempts dataset + RL 3D reaching baseline — [arXiv 1905.07447](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07447); ~45 citations — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)

#### ROBEL (Google / Berkeley) — CoRL 2019
- Ahn, Zhu, Hartikainen, Ponte, Gupta, Levine, Kumar; open-source low-cost robots: D'Claw (9-DoF three-fingered hand; manipulation) and D'Kitty (locomotion); benchmark tasks with dense/sparse objectives and hardware-safety metrics; results "can be replicated across copies of the robots located in different institutions"; [roboticsbenchmarks.org](http://www.roboticsbenchmarks.org) — [arXiv 1909.11639](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11639); ~158 citations — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)

#### TriFinger (MPI-IS / NYU) — CoRL 2020
- Wüthrich et al.; "inexpensive open-source robotic platform (~$5,000) capable of safe autonomous operation"; 1 kHz control, C++/Python front-ends; experiments on optimal control, RL, throwing, writing — [arXiv 2008.03596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03596); ~83 citations — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch). #### DeepClaw (SUSTech) — AIM 2020
- Wan, Wang, Liu, Yang, Song; "reconfigurable benchmark of robotic hardware and task hierarchy" — a minimal robot cell that hosts arms/grippers/cameras from various vendors; Tic-Tac-Toe, bin-clearing, jigsaw tasks on three hardware configurations — [arXiv 2005.02588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02588); ~6 citations — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)

#### RB2 — Ranking-Based Robotics Benchmark (CMU / FAIR / NYU / WPI / UIUC) — NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks

### Inferences
- The two learning-era benchmarks with evidence of cross-lab reproduction are FurnitureBench (10 external reproductions reported by the authors) and ROBEL (authors claim replication across institutions); for the rest, "reproducible" is a design claim without published multi-lab results.
- Franka Panda/FR3 + RealSense + 3D-printed parts became the de-facto "standard cell" (FurnitureBench, FMB, RAMP, IndustReal, ATOM-Bench single-arm, Industrial Dexterity Benchmark) before SO-100/SO-101 arrived as the low-cost alternative in 2026 (KQ3).

### Gaps
- No "ManiSkill real counterpart" benchmark with a published physical protocol was found; ManiSkill-based real experiments appear only as per-paper validations.
- RB2's exact hardware (arm, gripper, cameras) and the pooled-ranking math were not legible from the fetched PDF; the project website could not be reached.
- IndustRealKit contents and RAMP adoption outside Oxford were not verified.

## KQ3. Newer (2024–2026) real-world benchmarks for VLA / generalist, bimanual, dexterous, deformable, mobile, humanoid

### Takeaway
2026 saw a burst of real-robot VLA benchmarks. The ones a lab can reproduce locally fall into three design families: (i) low-cost SO-101 cells with a fully specified BOM and lighting/camera/placement protocol (VLA-REPLICA ~$1,050; SO-101 VLA benchmark), (ii) 3D-printable task kits for a named commercial platform (DuoBench on FR3 Duo, BusyBox for Mobile ALOHA, POMDAR for anthropomorphic hands, Peg-in-Bench, Industrial Dexterity Benchmark boards), and (iii) task-spec + data releases where reproduction depends on buying the listed objects (GM-100 with Taobao links, ATOM-Bench with mask-guided placement, LongBench, UMI-Bench). Several high-profile "real-world benchmarks" are actually centrally run (ManipArena, FolDeX/FoldChallenge, ArmnetBench) or hybrid kit-plus-server (ManipulationNet, RoboDojo-RealEval) — flagged as overlapping slice (a).

### Cited Findings

#### Generalist / VLA, single- or dual-arm tabletop

**VLA-REPLICA (UT Dallas IRVL) — arXiv May 2026**
- Huang, Zhang, Tang, Xiang; "low-cost, reproducible benchmark … built from off-the-shelf components … consistent results across independently constructed setups" — [arXiv 2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20774)
- BOM: SO-101 follower arm (~$200), RealSense D455 (~$425), webcam ($13.98), photography light box ($152.99), object set ($215.99) ≈ $1,050; 32"×32"×32" light box with 5600 K LED panels at specified distances; white PP background; camera poses fixed via snap-hook PVC mounts, AprilTag pose calibration with tolerances, plus visual overlay; object placement via reference-image overlay; 10 tasks (put bread on plate, bowl on coaster, stack blocks, fold towel, open oven, clean whiteboard, pour pepper n times, lift bowl, press button, collect blocks); 500 demos (50/task); 50 ID + 40 OOD test scenes, 5 trials each; binary success; models ACT, DiT-D/F, SmolVLA, X-VLA, π₀, π₀.₅; cross-setup replication: mean 0.49 vs 0.48 (ID) and 0.55 vs 0.50 (OOD); limitations: single low-cost embodiment, small task scale, residual hardware/lighting/placement differences — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1)
- Project page has setup guide, purchase link, leaderboard (ID/OOD tracks, 3×5 = 15 trials per task), code [github.com/IRVLUTD/VLAReplica](https://github.com/IRVLUTD/VLAReplica), HF dataset; "A user with no prior knowledge of the benchmark was able to build the setup within one hour" — [irvlutd.github.io/VLAReplica](https://irvlutd.github.io/VLAReplica/)

**Benchmarking VLA Models on SO-101: Failure and Recovery Analysis — arXiv Jun 2026**

**GM-100 — The Great March 100 (SJTU / SII / Robbyant) — arXiv Jan 2026**
- Wang, Li et al. (30+ authors); "first step towards a robot learning Olympics"; 100 detail-oriented tasks; two platforms (Agilex Cobot Magic, Mobile-ALOHA-style; Dobot Xtrainer, ALOHA-style); >13,000 teleop trajectories (100 train + 30 aligned test per task); baselines DP, π₀, π₀.₅, GR00T; metrics SR, Partial SR, action MSE/L1; tasks specified by written descriptions, object purchase links (mainly Taobao), human template videos, success criteria at [rhos.ai/research/gm-100](https://www.rhos.ai/research/gm-100); authors note real-world testing is "highly costly" and release results gradually — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11421v1); code [github.com/yuz1wan/GM-X](https://github.com/yuz1wan/GM-X); ~6 citations — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)

**LongBench (Fudan) — arXiv Apr 2026**
- Chen, Jia, Yang, Fu, Li, Zhang; >1,000 real-world episodes; Context-Independent vs Context-Dependent regimes; six policies — [arXiv 2604.16788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16788)

- Jin et al. (19 authors); "local-first" benchmark for UMI-style policies that "aligns data collection, scene reset, policy execution, result logging, and task-factor analysis within a unified protocol" — [arXiv 2606.10382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10382)

**Experiences from Benchmarking VLA Models (Macquarie Univ.) — arXiv Nov 2025**
- Zhang, Qi, Zheng; ALOHA Mobile (dual 7-DoF arms, 1 m × 1 m bench, 3 RealSense D405); 4 tasks (Clean Dish, Put Sponge into Pot, Unzip Bag, Folding Shorts); ACT, OpenVLA-OFT, RDT-1B, π₀; metrics SR, Time-to-Success, Instruction Adherence Accuracy across ID / spatial-OOD / instance+spatial-OOD; releases evaluation framework and failure videos — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11298v1). - No locally reproducible humanoid manipulation benchmark with a published physical protocol was found; HumanoidBench, SIMPLE, Labimus are sim-only (search results only, not opened).

#### Centrally run or arm-farm "benchmarks" (flag → slice a, listed for de-duplication)
- ManipArena (Sun Yat-sen Univ. / X Square Robot / MBZUAI / Tsinghua; arXiv Mar 2026, v2 Jul 2026): 20 tasks (10 tabletop execution, 5 tabletop semantic, 5 mobile), 10,812 trajectories / 13.5 M frames / ~188 robot-hours; all trials "conducted at controlled green-screen facilities with fixed lighting, camera placement, and workspace geometry"; stratified 10-trial protocol (4 in-domain, 4 shifted, 2 held-out), 10-point partial credit, SR = trials ≥9/10; best tabletop 951/1500 (63.4%, WALL-OSS-0.5) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28545); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28545)
- RoboDojo (HKU MMLab + Berkeley, Tsinghua, PKU, Stanford, MIT, CMU, NUS, NTU, CUHK, Imperial, SJTU, Northwestern, HKUST-GZ; arXiv Jul 2026): 42 sim + 18 real tasks on three bimanual embodiments (ARX X5, Piper, Piper X; 6 tasks each), fixed arm/camera/light mounts, touchscreen reset/record/score, layouts replayed via transparent overlay, 10 attempts per task, double-blind scoring by 3 evaluators; "supports both local deployment and remote cloud-based evaluation"; [robodojo-benchmark.com](http://robodojo-benchmark.com/), code [github.com/RoboDojo-Benchmark/RoboDojo](https://github.com/RoboDojo-Benchmark/RoboDojo) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1). Hybrid: local deployment is documented, so partially in scope.
- ArmnetBench v0.1 (Selvaraj, Uttini, Kuosmanen; arXiv Jul 2026): fleet of largely 3D-printable SO-101 cells (3 cameras, Raspberry Pi, networked power plug); 7 policies × 12 tasks (single + bimanual), 50 demos/task, 2,518 rollouts + 600 reference demos, 3-way labels (success/suboptimal/failure), data in LeRobot v3.0 / RoboMeter on Hugging Face; run on the authors' arm farm with on-site supervision — [arXiv 2607.24481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24481); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481)
- HALTER — "From Rollout to Reset: A Graph-Based Harness for Autonomous Long-Horizon Manipulation Evaluation" (Jiang, Yang, Jiang, Bertasius, Szafir, Lioutikov; arXiv Sep 2026): scene-graph + LLM scoring/reset harness on a Franka across four long-horizon tasks; restores scenes in 76% of episodes vs 52% for AutoEval — evaluation *tooling*, not a benchmark — [arXiv 2609.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19413)

### Inferences
- Among the 2026 VLA benchmarks, only VLA-REPLICA publishes a cross-setup replication experiment (0.49 vs 0.48 mean success); DuoBench, BusyBox, POMDAR, Peg-in-Bench and the ADI boards publish printable/BOM artifacts but no second-site results yet; GM-100, ATOM-Bench, LongBench, UMI-Bench rely on purchasable-object lists plus overlay/grid placement and have single-site results.
- Scene-reset standardization has converged on two cheap techniques — semi-transparent reference-image overlay on the live camera (SceneReplica 2023 → VLA-REPLICA, ATOM-Bench "mask-guided placement," RoboDojo) and physical grids/stencils (ACRV stencils → UMI-Bench 5 cm acrylic grid) — rather than fiducials.
- The low-cost SO-101 is becoming the 2026 equivalent of REPLAB/ROBEL (VLA-REPLICA, SO-101 VLA benchmark, ArmnetBench), which should ease multi-lab replication but limits payload/precision tasks.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed venue is yet listed for most 2026 entries (VLA-REPLICA, DuoBench, ATOM-Bench, UMI-Bench, GM-100, LongBench, POMDAR, Peg-in-Bench, FolDeX, ManipArena, ManipulationNet); treat venues as "arXiv preprint" until confirmed.
- LongBench, UMI-Bench, SO-101 benchmark: project pages give little hardware/protocol detail; purchase lists and calibration docs were not inspected.
- No reproducible real-world benchmark specific to humanoid manipulation or to mobile manipulation (post-2023) was found.

## KQ4. How do these benchmarks address the reproducibility problem (lighting, camera pose, object placement, reset, hardware differences)?

### Takeaway
Five recurring mechanisms: (1) fixed, documented object/parts sets — purchasable (YCB, APB, HCOS, GM-100 purchase links) or printable (EGAD, FMB, FurnitureBench, RAMP, DuoBench, BusyBox, POMDAR, Peg-in-Bench, COLOSSEUM perturbation objects) or precision-manufactured kits (NIST ATB, ManipulationNet acrylic boards); (2) placement aids — stencils (APB), grids (UMI-Bench), reference-image overlay (SceneReplica, VLA-REPLICA, ATOM-Bench, RoboDojo), randomness levels + GUI (FurnitureBench), defined randomization zones (FMB); (3) controlled illumination/background — light box at 5600 K (VLA-REPLICA), white curtain (FMB), green-screen enclosure (ManipArena), fixed lights (RoboDojo); (4) camera pose fixing — AprilTag calibration + overlay (VLA-REPLICA), fixed mounts (RoboDojo, FMB calibration procedure), wrist-only observation to sidestep third-person camera drift (UMI-Bench); (5) hardware fixing or cost-floor — a single named platform (Franka Panda, FR3 Duo, SO-101, Stretch, Fetch) or self-built low-cost cell (REPLAB, ROBEL, TriFinger). Instrumented artifacts (Robothon DR.J board, BusyBox Raspberry Pi) additionally automate success detection.

### Cited Findings
- Lighting/background: VLA-REPLICA uses a 32-inch photography light box with three LED panels at specified distances at 5600 K and a white PP sheet — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1); FMB specifies a white curtain environment and camera calibration procedure — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08553v3); ManipArena uses a 3 m × 3 m reconfigurable green-screen enclosure with fixed lighting — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28545); RoboDojo-RealEval fixes arm, camera and lighting mounts — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434v1)
- Camera pose: VLA-REPLICA calibrates camera pose with AprilTag estimation against a target pose with tolerances plus visual overlay — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1); SceneReplica avoids AR markers entirely and relies on image overlay with consistent camera pose — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15620); UMI-Bench excludes third-person cameras from policy input — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10382)
- Object placement/reset: ACRV APB uses stencils — [arXiv 1609.05258](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05258); UMI-Bench uses a 5 cm acrylic grid and per-scene JSON — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10382); ATOM-Bench "mask-guided placement" with 10 fixed seeds — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16826); FurnitureBench three randomness levels + initialization GUI — [project page](https://clvrai.github.io/furniture-bench/); FMB 20×20 cm and 35×35 cm randomization zones — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08553v3); DuoBench randomizes within specified boundaries — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11901v1)
- Hardware differences: DeepClaw explicitly showed "hardware variations significantly impact learning performance" across three cells — [arXiv 2005.02588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02588); GRASPA normalizes metrics by platform limits (workspace, payload) to allow comparing different arms — [arXiv 2002.05017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05017); GM-100 collects on two different ALOHA-style platforms to test cross-platform generalization — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11421v1); the SO-101 benchmark defines a universal action space all SO-101 arms must share — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08881v2)
- Manufacturing tolerance as a reproducibility spec: FMB 1–2 mm print tolerance — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08553v3); ManipulationNet ±20 μm acrylic boards — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1); Peg-in-Bench 0.05 mm resin printing, 0.02 mm clearance level dropped as unprintable — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00906)
- Automated scoring: Robothon board microcontroller verifies time and subtask completion and streams at 20 Hz — [GitHub](https://github.com/peterso/robotlearningblock); BusyBox modules are Raspberry-Pi-controlled with state-based success checking — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05441); FurnitureBench and DuoBench rely on manual stage tracking (DuoBench lists this as a limitation) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11901v1)
- Residual non-reproducibility acknowledged by authors: VLA-REPLICA — "small differences in hardware, lighting, and object placement may still introduce inconsistencies"; UMI-Bench — "operator reset variation, calibration drift, lighting changes, and hardware timing effects"; ManipArena — scores are "empirical estimates under a fixed protocol" — [VLA-REPLICA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1), [UMI-Bench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10382), [ManipArena HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28545)

### Inferences
- Benchmarks that fix the *embodiment* (SO-101, FR3 Duo, Franka+Robotiq) get tighter reproducibility but narrower audiences; protocol-only benchmarks (YCB, NIST, RA-L 2020) get broad applicability but comparisons across different robots remain confounded — exactly the trade-off the RA-L 2020 editors described.
- Trial budgets are small: 5–50 trials per task (VLA-REPLICA 15–25, ATOM-Bench 10 seeds, RoboDojo 10, ManipArena 10, UMI-Bench 50, SceneReplica 100 total). Statistical treatment of such budgets is slice (c), but the writer should note that most physical benchmarks report raw success rates without confidence intervals.

### Gaps
- Few benchmarks publish inter-site variance; only FurnitureBench (<16% gap across 10 reproductions) and VLA-REPLICA (two setups) give numbers.
- No benchmark found that standardizes robot *controller* parameters (impedance gains, control frequency) across labs beyond FMB/FurnitureBench's 10 Hz statement.

## KQ5. Which benchmarks are widely adopted vs. rarely used outside the originating lab?

### Takeaway
Adoption proxies (citations, downstream reuse, competitions, documented external reproductions) put YCB and the NIST ATBs in a class of their own, followed by FurnitureBench, FMB, EGAD, ROBEL, HomeRobot OVMM and THE COLOSSEUM. Most RA-L 2020 protocols, GRASPA, BBT, CEPB, RB2, REPLAB, RAMP, SceneReplica and DR.J remain in the ~15–90 citation band with little evidence of re-execution elsewhere. All 2026 benchmarks are too new to judge; ManipulationNet (15 cit.) and GM-100 (6 cit.) lead so far.

### Cited Findings
- Semantic Scholar citation counts (batch query 2026-10-01): YCB IJRR dataset 465; YCB RAM protocols 333; FurnitureBench 219; EGAD 182; THE COLOSSEUM 182; HomeRobot OVMM 159; ROBEL 158; FMB 127; IndustReal 122; NIST ATB protocols 110; bimanual cloth 86; ACRV APB 85; TriFinger 83; BRS 59; in-hand 53; REPLAB 45; GRASPA 37; RB2 37; BBT 35; HCOS 30; RAMP 26; REASSEMBLE 25; CEPB 24; SceneReplica 18; DR.J 17; ManipulationNet 15; GM-100 6; DeepClaw 6; λ 5; ICRA 2024 Cloth Competition 4 — [S2 Graph API batch](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch); FurnitureBench single lookup — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/f80de0e8d343166f8e9b2cf844253d7b24b443d4)
- Documented external reproductions: FurnitureBench "reproduced by 10 independent participants with performance gaps under 16%" — [project page](https://clvrai.github.io/furniture-bench/); VLA-REPLICA second setup within 0.01–0.05 mean success — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20774v1); Robothon board deployed on 55+ units — [GitHub](https://github.com/peterso/robotlearningblock); ROBEL replication across institutions (author claim) — [arXiv 1909.11639](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11639); RB2 run across two labs — [NeurIPS D&B](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/3988c7f88ebcb58c6ce932b957b6f332-Abstract-round2.html)
- Competition uptake: NIST ATBs at IROS 2017, WRS 2018, IROS 2020, ICRA 2022 RGMC — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/robotic-grasping-and-manipulation-assembly/assembly); RGMC continues in 2026 — [RGMC 2026 site](https://sites.google.com/view/rgmcomp); HomeRobot OVMM challenge at NeurIPS 2023 — [ovmm.github.io](https://ovmm.github.io/)
- Reuse as building blocks: YCB objects in SceneReplica, Cruciani in-hand, CEPB, ManipulationNet, AHAP, Elliott–Connolly (sources in KQ1/KQ3); NIST ATB1 in REASSEMBLE — [arXiv 2502.05086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05086); NIST clearance ladder in ManipulationNet and Peg-in-Bench — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00906)
- Community self-assessment: the RA-L 2020 editors state "there are essentially no robotic manipulation benchmarks that are widely adopted by the research community" — [special-issue intro](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/wp.wpi.edu/dist/f/375/files/2022/03/Introduction-to-the-Special-Issue-on-Benchmarking-Protocols-for-Robotic-Manipulation.pdf); ManipulationNet (2026) still argues object sets "risk questionable authenticity without formal evaluation" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04363v1); DuoBench's related-work table lists FurnitureBench as the only prior real-world benchmark with 3D-printable assets — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11901v1)

### Inferences
- Citation counts overstate *execution* adoption: EGAD and COLOSSEUM are cited mostly as datasets/sim tools; the clearest evidence of other labs physically re-running a protocol is for YCB-based grasping studies, NIST ATB competitions, FurnitureBench and the Robothon board.
- For the awesome list, a reasonable adoption tagging is: Widely adopted — YCB, NIST ATB; Adopted (multi-lab evidence) — FurnitureBench, ROBEL/D'Claw, Robothon DR.J board, HomeRobot OVMM; Cited but rarely re-run — FMB, EGAD, GRASPA, BBT, RA-L 2020 protocols, RB2, REPLAB, TriFinger (local), RAMP, SceneReplica, CEPB, HCOS; Too new — all 2026 entries.

### Gaps
- Citation counts for TriFinger-based RRC, GRASPA-fying the Panda, ACRV APB journal version, BusyBox, VLA-REPLICA, DuoBench, ATOM-Bench, UMI-Bench, POMDAR, Peg-in-Bench, FolDeX and LongBench were not retrieved (rate limits); no systematic "re-execution count" exists for any benchmark.
- Current physical availability of kits (YCB set, HCOS, NIST boards on-demand purchase, ManipulationNet kit distribution status) was not verified.
