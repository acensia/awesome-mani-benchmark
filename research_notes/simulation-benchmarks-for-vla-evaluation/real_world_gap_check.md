# Gap Check: Real-World Robot Manipulation Evaluation Works Missing from the First Survey (2023 – October 2026)

Scope note: only works with physical-robot evaluation are catalogued. Each candidate was compared against the 236 names in `already_listed.txt` as it stood at the start of this session (2026-10-02). "Opened" means the paper, abstract page or project page was fetched in this session; anything taken only from a search snippet is marked as such. The 43 verified additions are in `real_world_gap_check.entries.json` (17 physical, 14 proxy, 9 competition, 3 hosted). Evaluation-tooling works have no group of their own in the catalog; they are filed under "physical" with the tag "Automated" and a note.

## KQ0. Handed-over leads (RADAR, LabDex, CEBench, RoboChess Challenge, ManiGuard-Bench)

### Takeaway
Four of the five leads are real-world works absent from the list and were added. ManiGuard-Bench is already listed as a simulation diagnostic and qualifies only as a weak proxy: success and safe-success correlate with real results, violation rate does not, and the authors disclaim calibrated prediction.

### Cited Findings
- RADAR ("Real-world Autonomous Dynamics And Reasoning", submitted 2026-02-11) evaluates VLAs in a self-contained robot cell: a collaborative arm (example given: 6-DOF UR5e with Robotiq 2F-85), a wrist RealSense D435, two fixed RealSense D415 cameras for 3D reconstruction, and an actuated task platform on a mechanical stage for automatic reset. Success is measured by 3D IoU; a client–server–worker architecture is "designed to decouple policy submission from physical execution" for "24/7 autonomous evaluation". Results over 32 trials per model: π0.5 17/32 (expected 3D IoU 0.2605), π0 2/32, π0-FAST 2/32, RDT 0/32, OpenVLA 0/32. No project page or code link appears in the paper. — [arXiv 2602.10980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10980), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10980)
- LabDex (submitted 2026-08-19) is "a large-scale real-world dataset and benchmark for dexterous manipulation in chemistry laboratories": Franka Research 3 + XHand, three RealSense D435i cameras, Manus Quantum glove + VIVE tracker teleoperation; 7 atomic-skill categories (30+ atomic skills, 20+ objects), 10+ compositional skills, 1 long-horizon workflow; 200 demonstrations per task by default; 26 atomic skill instances evaluated with 50 trials each; overall average DP 0.02, ACT 0.34, π0.5 0.57. — [arXiv 2608.18618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18618), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18618)
- CEBench is introduced inside the LLaVA-VLA method paper "Rethinking the Practicality of Vision-language-action Model: A Comprehensive Benchmark and An Improved Baseline" (accepted to ICRA 2026). Simulation: CALVIN and RoboTwin. Real: Cobot-Magic dual-arm mobile robot with Piper arms, 8 fixed-base tasks + 2 mobile manipulation tasks, 1.6k expert trajectories, 100 evaluation episodes per fixed-base task and 10 per mobile task, with domain randomization; LLaVA-VLA 44.2% seen, 30.7% domain-randomized. — [arXiv 2602.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22663), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22663)
- RoboChess Challenge is hosted by the CoRL 2026 Workshop on GPU-Accelerated Simulation to Scalable and Generalizable Robot Policy Learning (organizers from NVIDIA, Lambda, UW–Madison, Adobe Research). Tracks: Simulation (MuJoCo, Isaac Lab, Genesis) and Real-World (LeRobot SO-101, Franka, UR, Piper, Yam/Rebot); winners announced 2026-11-09 in Austin. The page does not say who operates the real robots or how real-track runs are scored. — [Workshop page](https://sites.google.com/view/corl-workshop-robochess)
- ManiGuard-Bench (200 locked base tasks, 1,000 scenarios; OmniGibson / Isaac Sim) includes a matched real-robot study on a Franka Panda: 12 cells (2 task families × ID + 2 OOD axes × 3 policies), 30 rollouts per cell per domain. Pearson r = 0.91 (task success), 0.88 (safe success), 0.09 (violation rate). Authors: "Simulation predicts which policy is safer, but not how unsafe it is" and call it "an initial study of sim-to-real safety transfer rather than calibrated prediction of real-world safety". — [arXiv 2608.17386 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)

### Inferences
- ManiGuard-Bench should stay a simulation diagnostic. A cross-reference in the proxy group is defensible only with the caveat that violation rate does not transfer (r = 0.09) and scene matching is approximate. It is not in the JSON because it is already catalogued.
- RADAR is architecturally a hosted autonomous station (the same idea as AutoEval), but public access is unverified; it is grouped "hosted" with that caveat.

### Gaps
- RADAR: no public submission URL, code or hardware bill of materials found.
- RoboChess: real-track rules and submission deadline were placeholders at fetch time.

## KQ1. Benchmarks hidden inside dataset or method papers

### Takeaway
The large dataset papers the brief named (AgiBot World, RoboMIND, Galaxea, RoboCOIN, ARIO, AIRoA MoMa) do not define a benchmark others can run; their evaluations are in-house, and where a public evaluation exists it is a separate, already-listed challenge. The real omissions are smaller dataset-plus-benchmark papers in specialised domains (SutureBot, LabDex, DexHoldem, REBOOT, DexH2R, The Imitator Game, ABC) and benchmarks defined inside method papers (CEBench, BiAssemble).

### Cited Findings
- RoboMIND (107k trajectories, 479 tasks, four embodiments; RSS 2025 per a third-party list) evaluates ACT, Diffusion Policy and BAKU on 45 tasks and several VLAs in-house; RoboMIND 2.0 (310K dual-arm trajectories, 739 tasks, six embodiments, plus digital twins) is a dataset-and-model paper. Neither abstract describes a hosted evaluation or leaderboard. — [RoboMIND arXiv 2412.13877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13877), [RoboMIND 2.0 arXiv 2512.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24653)
- AgiBot World Colosseo (1M+ trajectories, 217 tasks) is a dataset + GO-1 policy paper; its public evaluation vehicle is the already-listed AgiBot World Challenge. — [arXiv 2503.06669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06669)
- Galaxea Open-World Dataset (500 h, 150+ tasks, 50 scenes, one embodiment) describes a benchmark of tabletop, device-operation and bed-making tasks in normal and few-shot settings, run in-house for the G0 model (search summary of the paper; the paper itself was not opened). — [arXiv 2509.00576](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00576v1)
- RoboCOIN (180K demonstrations, 15 platforms, 421 tasks), ARIO (about 3M episodes under one data standard), AIRoA MoMa (25,469 HSR episodes), HABIT (10K+ human-present episodes, 60 tasks) and the 1,500-hour XR-2 household dataset are dataset papers whose abstracts report in-house experiments only. — [RoboCOIN 2511.17441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17441), [ARIO 2408.10899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10899), [AIRoA MoMa 2509.25032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25032), [HABIT 2606.31682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31682), [XR-2 2609.03591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03591)
- ABC ("Scalable Behavior Cloning with Open Data, Training, and Evaluation", 2026-06-25; UC Berkeley, MIT, Amazon FAR, XDOF, CMU) has no "benchmark" in its title but ships an evaluation stack: ABC-130K (3,500 h, 130K episodes, 195 tasks), ABC-Sim (400 h of sim teleoperation, 20 MuJoCo tasks "for sim-to-real correlation") and ABC-Eval ("100 hours of real-world policy rollouts, released with evaluation scores"). — [arXiv 2606.27375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27375), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27375)
- SutureBot (NeurIPS 2025) pairs a 1,890-demonstration dVRK dataset with a physical evaluation protocol (ten full suture procedures per policy, UV-marker insertion error). — [arXiv 2510.20965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20965)
- DexHoldem (2026-05-18): 1,470 teleoperated ShadowHand demonstrations over 14 card-and-chip primitives plus "a standardized physical policy benchmark"; π0.5 best task completion 61.2%, scene-preserving success 47.5%; 33 closed-loop hand-level rollouts with 12.1% completion. Nine policies × 80 trials is from a search summary of the HTML. — [arXiv 2605.18727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18727)
- REBOOT (2026-09-18): real-robot dataset and benchmark for precision assembly, 2,160 demonstrations over 18 tasks, five shared phases, half expert and half recovery demonstrations; ACT-style, diffusion and π0-FAST policies scored by phase-level completion. — [arXiv 2609.22591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22591)
- DexH2R (ICCV 2025): real-world human-to-robot handover dataset on a teleoperated ShadowHand, 4,282 handover trials, 39 participants, 56 objects, test set of 803 trajectories split by unseen objects / unseen subjects. — [arXiv 2506.23152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23152), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23152)
- The Imitator Game (2026-08-23): four-level benchmark of imitation from human video with IG-10K (20,000+ paired episodes, 50+ tasks, 6 domains, real and simulated) and "Imitator Arena, an open platform for blind A/B human evaluation"; real domain on a dual-arm Realman robot; nine models, all below 13% zero-shot success on unseen tasks. — [arXiv 2608.22301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22301), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22301)
- BiAssemble (ICML 2025) introduces, inside a method paper, "a real-world benchmark featuring globally available objects with reproducible broken parts, along with their corresponding 3D meshes"; categories: wine glass, plate, beer bottle, bowl, mug, teapot; two Franka Pandas and an Azure Kinect. — [arXiv 2506.06221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06221), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06221)
- CEBench: see KQ0. — [arXiv 2602.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22663)

### Inferences
- "Dataset paper with a benchmark" splits into two kinds. Very large generalist datasets (AgiBot World, RoboMIND, Galaxea, RoboCOIN) do not publish a reusable protocol. Mid-sized domain datasets (surgery, laboratory, card play, precision assembly, handover) do, because a fixed hardware cell and a fixed task list are what make the dataset useful. The first survey searched the first kind's vocabulary ("generalist", "VLA") and missed the second kind.
- None of the added dataset benchmarks has a public leaderboard; reuse means rebuilding the same cell (ShadowHand, dVRK, XHand), which limits adoption.

### Gaps
- BridgeData V2, DROID, Open X-Embodiment and RH20T evaluation sections were not re-opened in this session; the first survey's methodology file already documents their in-house protocols, and none is known to define a task suite for outside use.
- Galaxea's benchmark description comes from a search summary, not from the opened paper.
- Release status (data, code, hardware files) was confirmed only for SutureBot's dataset link; for LabDex, DexHoldem, REBOOT, DexH2R, IG-10K and ABC it was not checked.

## KQ2. Missed physical benchmarks 2023–2026, by sub-area

### Takeaway
Thirteen physical benchmarks were added (the "physical" group in the JSON holds 17 because four tooling entries from KQ6 are filed there). They cluster in sub-areas the first survey reported as thin: dexterous hands (LabDex, DexHoldem, DexH2R), humanoid tool use (HumanoidToolBench), tactile sensing (TacO), assembly (REBOOT, BiAssemble, CT benchmark), surgery (SutureBot), mobile manipulation (CEBench, ASTM WK92144) and grasping (a 5,040-experiment cross-lab study). No real-world benchmark dedicated to memory, deformables beyond the listed ones, kitchens, or dynamic objects was found that is not simulation-first.

### Cited Findings
- Humanoid / tool use — HumanoidToolBench (posted 2026-10-01): 18 tasks over three scenarios, three execution levels and two tool-set modes; ToolBook dataset of 3.1k demonstrations "collected in simulation and on a real Unitree G1" (3,003 simulation + 91 real); seven policies evaluated in simulation and three on the real robot. — [arXiv 2610.02089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02089)
- Dexterous / laboratory automation — LabDex: see KQ0. — [arXiv 2608.18618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18618)
- Dexterous — DexHoldem and DexH2R: see KQ1. — [arXiv 2605.18727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18727), [arXiv 2506.23152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23152)
- Tactile — TacO (2026-05-21): "an imitation learning pipeline and real-world evaluation of six tactile sensors across 3 tasks" (FSR, FlexiTac, eGaIn, eFlesh, Daimon, contact microphone) on Franka arms at "two institutions located over 2,000 miles apart", ACT policies, plus an open-source hardware repeatability test kit; pick-and-place with FlexiTac 0.85 vs 0.75 vision-only. — [arXiv 2605.21976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21976), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21976)
- Assembly — REBOOT and BiAssemble: see KQ1. — [arXiv 2609.22591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22591), [arXiv 2506.06221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06221)
- Human-robot collaboration / assembly — the CT benchmark (2024-02-01) is "a benchmark and model set designed to facilitate the testing and evaluation of human-robot collaborative assembly scenarios", compared in manual, automatic and collaborative setups by assembly time and NASA-TLX workload; collaborative assembly took 70.8% longer than manual. — [arXiv 2402.00708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00708)
- Surgical — SutureBot: see KQ1. — [arXiv 2510.20965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20965)
- Mobile manipulation — CEBench's real part (8 fixed-base + 2 mobile tasks on Cobot-Magic): see KQ0. — [arXiv 2602.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22663)
- Mobile manipulation / standards — ASTM committee F45 is developing a mobile-manipulator test standard (work item WK92144) based on a NIST paper: a low-cost test table whose top can lie flat, tilt to 45° or stand at 90°, with an optional shaker, and tasks such as S-shaped path tracking and peg insertion (article dated 2024-11-30; status "proposed / in development"). — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/astm-developing-testing-standards-for-mobile-manipulators/)
- Grasping — "A Benchmarking Study of Vision-based Robotic Grasping Algorithms" (2025-03, replacing arXiv 2307.11622) compares two learned and two analytical grasp planners with an existing protocol under varied lighting, backgrounds, camera noise and grippers, in simulation and on real robots, partly in two laboratories; 5,040 experiments; "experiment recordings and our benchmarking software are publicly available". — [arXiv 2503.11163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11163)
- Imitation from human video — The Imitator Game: see KQ1. — [arXiv 2608.22301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22301)
- Generalization with automatic evaluation — RADAR: see KQ0. — [arXiv 2602.10980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10980)

### Inferences
- Humanoid and mobile manipulation remain thin even after this pass: HumanoidToolBench's real-robot part covers a small subset of its tasks, CEBench has two mobile tasks, and the ASTM method measures positioning accuracy, not learned policies.
- Several "real-world" claims in 2026 titles turn out to be simulation-first with a small real check (ManiFeel, MoDeSuite, WatchAct, GCA-Bench, ZETA); these belong to the simulation catalog, with the real check noted.

### Gaps
- Memory, kitchen/household, cloth beyond FolDeX and the ICRA 2024 competition, dynamic or moving objects: searches returned only simulation benchmarks or items already listed. No new real-world benchmark found.
- The HAND ERC benchmarking page (hand-erc.github.io/benchmarking) could not be fetched; its accompanying review was opened and names no physical system-level benchmark beyond POMDAR, NIST boards, AHAP and the Dyson cloth set, all already listed.
- Tool use, language-conditioned and safety sub-areas: only simulation-first or offline benchmarks surfaced (RoboWits, One Word Different Action, ResponsibleRobotBench).

## KQ3. Missed hosted platforms and leaderboards

### Takeaway
Three hosted or centrally operated evaluation efforts were missing: TOTO (2023, a shared remote Franka), RADAR (2026, an autonomous evaluation cell with a submission architecture) and Robocurve (2026, a company that runs independent real-robot evaluations and publishes an open-source evaluation framework). No further public real-robot leaderboard was found beyond those already listed.

### Cited Findings
- TOTO ("Train Offline, Test Online: A Real Robot Learning Benchmark", ICRA 2023): "provides remote users with access to shared robotic hardware"; Franka Emika Panda with a RealSense D435; scooping and pouring; 1,000–2,000 trajectories per task; "Users submit policies through Google Forms for evaluation on our real-world setup"; "evaluation turnaround time is currently around 12 hours"; 18 trials per method for seen and for unseen locations; initial methods "remotely contributed by five institutions". — [arXiv 2306.00942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00942), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.00942)
- TOTO was also run as a NeurIPS 2023 competition with a simulation phase qualifying for a real-robot phase (search summary; competition page not opened). — [NeurIPS 2023 competitions](https://nips.cc/virtual/2023/events/Competition)
- Robocurve is a Public Benefit Corporation "backed by Y Combinator" that states "We are an independent evaluator for robotics"; it announced USD 10M seed funding on 2026-09-14 and publishes reports on frontier models controlling physical arms (block stacking, Tower of Hanoi, Jenga, bimanual manipulation). — [robocurve.org](https://robocurve.org/)
- Robocurve's Inspect Robots is an MIT-licensed evaluation framework ("Open source evals for physical AI") with adapters for I2RT YAM bimanual arms, Franka FR3/Panda, AgiBot A2, Unitree G1 arms, SO-100/101, WidowX and ROS arms, and integrations with Isaac Lab and XPolicyLab. — [GitHub robocurve/inspect-robots](https://github.com/robocurve/inspect-robots)
- A third-party paper documents a commissioned Robocurve evaluation: "The real trials were run and graded by Robocurve, a third-party robot evaluator, on its own robot setup under its own protocol"; 200 trials (5 tasks × 2 policies × 20) with staged rubrics, per-trial scores published. — [arXiv 2610.00731 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00731)
- RADAR: see KQ0. — [arXiv 2602.10980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10980)
- XPolicyLab (2026-08-10) is infrastructure rather than a platform: one adapter standard for 42 policies; "The same adapters serve RoboTwin, RoboDojo simulation, and standardized real-robot evaluation through one interface." — [arXiv 2608.09892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09892)

### Inferences
- The first survey's physical-benchmark notes name TOTO in their scope line as belonging to the hosted-platform researcher, but the hosted-platform notes never mention it; it was lost at the hand-off between two researchers.
- Robocurve is a new category: fee-for-service independent evaluation without a leaderboard. It would not surface in arXiv searches because the company has no paper.

### Gaps
- Whether TOTO still accepts submissions in 2026 was not checked.
- Robocurve's individual reports (models, tasks, trial counts) were not opened; only the homepage, the repository and one client paper were.
- Chinese-language leads could not be opened (domain blocked by the fetch tool): a 2026-09-02 report that CAICT launched an "embodied-intelligence real-scene application capability assessment", and the industry standard YD/T 6770—2026 on embodied-intelligence benchmark testing in simulated and real environments (in force from 2026-06-01). Both are known only from search snippets and are not in the JSON. — [Tencent News (not opened)](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260902A0B97F00), [Xinhua (not opened)](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260401/20e6b03022bb49ef991e19b1ccb9bb6d/c.html)

## KQ5. Missed proxy evaluators validated against real results

### Takeaway
Fourteen proxies were added. Twelve of them are policy-evaluation experiments inside papers whose titles are about a world model, a real-to-sim generator or a training stack, not about evaluation, which is why keyword searches on "policy evaluation" missed them. Reported agreement ranges from r = 0.85 to r = 0.97, usually over a handful of tasks or checkpoints.

### Cited Findings
- WorldComposer ("From Seeing to Simulating", 2026-04-17; Peking University, Lightwheel): panorama-to-simulation scenes in Isaac Sim with digital-cousin edits; bimanual LeRobot SO-101 arms; ACT, DP, SmolVLA, π0 across generalization levels; "The Pearson correlation coefficient yields a high value of r=0.91". — [arXiv 2604.15805 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15805)
- ABC-Sim (MuJoCo): 12 checkpoints, three tasks, 50 trials per task; "For the strict success metric, we observe a Pearson correlation of r=0.85 (p=4.2×10^-4, n=12) and for task progress we observe r=0.91 (p=5.0×10^-5, n=12)". — [arXiv 2606.27375 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27375)
- OSCAR (2026-06-03): skeleton-conditioned video world model (Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B fine-tune) evaluating the seven open DROID policies on the RoboArena leaderboard over 65 manually retained sessions; skeleton conditioning: MMRV 0.571, Spearman +0.750, Pearson +0.852. — [arXiv 2606.04463 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04463v2)
- Kaedim reconstruction study (2026-09-30): two simulated twins of one I2RT YAM bimanual cell, 5 tasks × 2 policies (π0.5, MolmoAct2), 20 runs per cell, scored against 200 real trials graded by Robocurve; "Pearson correlation between the ten simulated and real cell means is r = 0.90 for the authored reconstruction and 0.51 for the default"; mean score error 6.97 vs 17.54 points; harness and per-trial scores released. — [arXiv 2610.00731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00731), [code](https://github.com/Kaedim/yam-sim-harness-oss)
- RoboART / Predictive Red Teaming (2025-02-10; Google DeepMind, Princeton): image editing plus a policy-specific anomaly detector predicts degradation under off-nominal conditions; "500+ hardware trials in twelve off-nominal conditions"; "less than 0.19 average difference between predicted and real success rates". — [arXiv 2502.06575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06575)
- WEAVER (2026-06-11): multi-view latent world model; agreement measured with human-labelled imagined rollouts against real success "averaged over 20 trials per task"; after fine-tuning, Pearson correlation 0.87, with higher correlation and lower MMRV than Ctrl-World. — [arXiv 2606.13672 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13672)
- Hi-WM (2026-04-23): closed-loop rollouts in a world model for post-training; three real tasks, two policy backbones; "the two success rates are strongly correlated, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of r=0.953". — [arXiv 2604.21741 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21741)
- Mem-World (CoRL 2026): "policy success rates in Mem-World are highly correlated with their real-world success rates across the five tasks (r=0.97, p<0.001)", against r = 0.85 for Ctrl-World. — [arXiv 2606.18960 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18960)
- RoboSnap / DROID-Sim (2026-07-07): single-image real-to-sim scenes; real-only fine-tuned π0.5 policies; "Pearson correlation (r=0.887) and MMRV (0.0066)"; DROID-Sim built from 564 DROID scenes. — [arXiv 2607.06699 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06699)
- Agentic Real2Sim (2026-07-21): VLM agents convert recordings into simulation twins; across six pick-and-place tasks "simulation and real-world performance exhibit a strong Pearson correlation of r=0.920 (p=0.009)". — [arXiv 2607.19190 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19190)
- ViTacWorld (2026-07-24): visuo-tactile world model; 12 pairs of aggregate success rates over 50 trials each; "Pearson r=0.863; Spearman ρ=0.790", mean absolute error 12.33 points; "ViTacWorld underestimates success by 9.0 points on average". — [arXiv 2607.22530 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22530)
- DexTouch-WM (IROS 2026 workshop): tactile world model for dexterous hands; per-task Pearson for the human+robot model 0.972 / 0.939 / 0.889 / 0.577 (MMRV 0–0.067) on four tasks; robot-only model 0.400 / 0.945 / 0.906 / 0.334. — [arXiv 2609.20649 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20649)
- HapticWorld (2026-09-25): world simulator predicting joint torque; "the success rates measured inside HapticWorld closely match real-world evaluation"; no coefficient reported. — [arXiv 2609.31924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31924)
- GeniWorld (2026-08-06): "GeniWorld exhibits a strong positive correlation between real-world and simulated performance in standard in-domain settings"; shown as a scatter plot without a coefficient. The first survey's proxy notes mention GeniWorld in a catch-all row but it is not in the list. — [arXiv 2608.06332 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06332)
- Offline metric, not added to the JSON: CI-MSE (2026-06-29) reports Spearman −0.87 between validation error and rollout performance across 27 checkpoints (−0.61 for raw MSE); the headline number is from simulation, with real-world experiments on four tasks described as consistent. — [arXiv 2606.29898 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29898)

### Inferences
- Tactile and force-conditioned proxies (ViTacWorld, DexTouch-WM, HapticWorld) are a new sub-family absent from the first survey, which stated that no proxy covered tactile policies.
- The agreement numbers are not comparable across papers: Agentic Real2Sim correlates across tasks, ABC-Sim across checkpoints co-trained on simulation data, OSCAR against a leaderboard ranking on hand-selected sessions, Kaedim across ten task-policy cells.
- Two entries carry a commercial interest that the reader should know: Kaedim sells the asset pipeline it evaluates and paid for the real trials; ABC-Sim's correlation holds for policies co-trained with its own simulation data.

### Gaps
- Robot platforms, task counts and policy counts for WEAVER, Hi-WM, Mem-World and RoboSnap correlation studies were not extracted.
- Masked Visual Actions (r = 0.982) and DriftWorld (r up to 0.99) report correlation against simulator ground truth (Push-T, Robomimic), so they were excluded; Masked Visual Actions also has a small real-world check that was not analysed. — [arXiv 2607.19343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19343), [arXiv 2607.15065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15065)
- BWM and WorldArena 2.0 were named in the first survey's notes; BWM's physical-robot result is for its data-engine role, and its policy-evaluator ranking is on the RoboTwin-based WorldArena benchmark, so it was not added. WorldArena 2.0's two real tasks were not re-examined. — [arXiv 2607.29302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29302), [arXiv 2605.17912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17912)
- More world-model papers with a buried evaluation experiment probably exist; only one arXiv query family ("world model" + "policy evaluation") was enumerated.

## KQ6. Evaluation tooling released for others (success detection, reset, VLM scoring, statistics)

### Takeaway
Five tooling works were added (Eval-Actions, RoboReward, FailBench, PRM-as-a-Judge 1.5, and Inspect Robots under the Robocurve entry). The first survey did know the statistics literature; its methodology notes cover sequential-testing papers, but the catalog has no group for tools, so none of them reached the list.

### Cited Findings
- Eval-Actions (2026-01-26; earlier title "Trustworthy Evaluation of Robotic Manipulation: A New Benchmark and AutoEval Methods"): "13K+ teleoperated and policy-generated real-robot episodes covering 150+ tasks and approximately 52 hours" on platforms including ARX R5 and UR5, with expert-grading, rank-guided and chain-of-thought labels; AutoEval-S reaches SRCC 0.81 / 0.84 and success-detection accuracy 90.6% / 91.0%. — [arXiv 2601.18723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18723)
- RoboReward (2026-01-02): "a robotics reward dataset and benchmark built on large-scale real-robot corpora from Open X-Embodiment (OXE) and RoboArena", plus 4B/8B reward models; "We release the full dataset, trained reward models, and evaluation suite". — [arXiv 2601.00675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00675)
- FailBench (2026-09-03): "2,197 manipulation attempts across 14 public sources (12 real-world, 2 simulated)"; 13 VLM-based detectors; "the best model achieves only 0.77 mean balanced accuracy"; near chance (<0.60) on contact-intensive assembly; "a systematic bias toward predicting success under ambiguous evidence". — [arXiv 2609.03611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03611)
- PRM-as-a-Judge 1.5 (2026-08-14): "a toolkit for robot process assessment that turns rollout videos into dense progress curves", applied to RoboDojo real-world and simulation rollouts from the leaderboard frozen on 3 July 2026, with RoboPulse++ to test the process reward model; released as an open-source suite. — [arXiv 2608.14284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14284), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14284)
- Inspect Robots: see KQ3. — [GitHub](https://github.com/robocurve/inspect-robots)
- Robometer (RSS 2026) is a general reward model trained on RBM-1M (over one million trajectories) with code and weights released; WEAVER uses it as a reward label source. It is a training-oriented reward model and was not added. — [arXiv 2603.02115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02115)
- StepEval (CoRL 2025 Eval&Deploy workshop) is a design blueprint for VLM-based per-subgoal scoring: "Rather than proposing new benchmarks or APIs, our contribution is to outline design principles". Not added. — [arXiv 2509.19524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19524)
- "Active Real-World Factor-Based Evaluation for Generalist Robot Policies" (2026-07-16) chooses test configurations by expected information gain; "2331 real-world evaluations across 3 tasks", saving "at least 20-40% of trials". No released tool was verified. — [arXiv 2607.14439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14439)
- "Beyond Binary Success" (2026-03-13) gives an anytime-valid sequential test for policy comparison on partial-credit and continuous metrics, with "up to 70% reduction in evaluation burden compared to standard batch methods"; it and the earlier STEP paper are already in the first survey's methodology notes but not in the list. — [arXiv 2603.13616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616), [arXiv 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966)
- "No Free Checker: A Survey of Verifiers for Robot Policies" (2026-09-08) surveys roughly 150 success detectors, reward models, monitors and formal verifiers; a ready source for extending a tooling group. — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)
- vla-eval (AllenAI, 2026-03-14) and DeepInsight (2026-06-16) are evaluation harnesses; vla-eval "supports 14 simulation benchmarks", so it is simulation tooling. — [arXiv 2603.13966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13966), [arXiv 2606.17574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17574)

### Inferences
- If the catalog wants tooling, it needs a fifth group (or a tag) rather than squeezing tools into "physical"; the four tooling entries in the JSON are flagged in their notes.
- FailBench is a caution for every listed platform that scores with a VLM judge: the best judge is at 0.77 balanced accuracy and biased toward "success".

### Gaps
- No released automatic-reset system other than the already-listed AutoEval and HALTER was found.
- Code availability for STEP and "Beyond Binary Success" was not checked in this session.
- FailBench's download location was not found on the abstract page.

## KQ8. Diagnosis: why the first survey missed these works

### Takeaway
The first survey was strong on papers that call themselves a real-world benchmark for generalist policies and weak everywhere the work is real-world evaluation but is labelled as something else: a world model, a dataset, a competition in another domain, a company service, a standard, or a tool. Six patterns explain the 43 omissions; keyword bias and scope framing account for most of them, recency for about a quarter, and venue bias for few.

### Cited Findings
- Keyword evidence: an arXiv abstract search for the exact phrase "real-world benchmark" with "robot" and "manipulation" returns 27 papers, and for "real-robot benchmark" 11 papers; every benchmark paper among them (RoboDojo, ATOM-Bench, UMI-Bench, SO-101 benchmark, VLA-REPLICA, LongBench, ManipulationNet, FMB, FolDeX, PhAIL, GarmentLab) is already listed, except REBOOT (posted 2026-09-18), BiAssemble and Eval-Actions. — [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=%22real-world+benchmark%22+AND+robot+AND+manipulation&searchtype=abstract&order=-announced_date_first&size=100), [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=%22real-robot+benchmark%22&searchtype=abstract&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- Keyword evidence for proxies: an arXiv abstract search for "world model" + robot + "policy evaluation" + manipulation returns 24 papers; the ones with "Eval", "evaluation" or "Evaluator" in the title are listed (SC3-Eval, WorldEval, WorldGym, Scalable Policy Evaluation, Interactive World Simulator), while those titled as models are not (OSCAR, WEAVER, Hi-WM, Mem-World, GeniWorld, ViTacWorld, DexTouch-WM, HapticWorld, Agentic Real2Sim). — [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=%22world+model%22+AND+robot+AND+%22policy+evaluation%22+AND+manipulation&searchtype=abstract&order=-announced_date_first&size=200)
- Hand-off evidence: the first survey's physical-benchmark file names TOTO in its scope line as a remote platform that is another researcher's slice; the remote-platform file does not contain the string "TOTO". The remote-platform file's ICRA roster line names "Sim2Real Challenges" and "AI for Robotic Surgery (dVRK)" without creating entries. The proxy file's catalog row 40 groups "BWM, GeniWorld, WorldArena, WorldArena 2.0, WorldSimProbe, EWMBench" as a single catch-all row. — files in `research_notes/real-world-manipulation-evaluation-benchmarks/` (local; see [TOTO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00942) and [ICRA 2026 competitions](https://2026.ieee-icra.org/program/competitions/) for the works themselves)
- Roster coverage: the first survey cites competition rosters for ICRA 2025 and ICRA 2026 only; the ICRA 2024 roster lists 11 competitions including the Food Topping Challenge and the Robotic Sim2Real Challenge, and IROS 2024 hosted the Robotic Construction Challenge. — [ICRA 2024 competitions](https://2024.ieee-icra.org/program/competitions/), [RCC page](https://air.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/robochallenge/construction/iros2024.html)
- Existing lists do not compensate: the Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks manipulation section has ten entries, of which one (FurnitureBench) is real-world; the Awesome-Robotics-Manipulation "Basic Manipulation in Real World" table has five rows, all already listed. — [HaoranZhangumich/Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks](https://github.com/HaoranZhangumich/Awesome-Robotic-Benchmarks), [BaiShuanghao/Awesome-Robotics-Manipulation](https://github.com/BaiShuanghao/Awesome-Robotics-Manipulation)
- Surveys do not compensate either: the TMLR 2026 VLA survey states "Since real-robot evaluation is costly and operationally complex, most benchmarks are implemented in simulation environments", and the 2026 audit "What Are We Actually Benchmarking in Robot Manipulation?" examines only LIBERO, CALVIN, SimplerEnv, RoboCasa and RoboTwin 2.0. — [arXiv 2604.23001 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23001), [arXiv 2606.04233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04233)
- Non-paper sources: Robocurve has a website and a GitHub repository but no paper; the Humanoid Olympic Games is a Substack post; the World Humanoid Robot Games is covered by Wikipedia and news media. — [robocurve.org](https://robocurve.org/), [Substack](https://generalrobots.substack.com/p/benjies-humanoid-olympic-games), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Humanoid_Robot_Games)
- Recency: six additions are dated within one month of the first survey (FailBench 2026-09-03, DexTouch-WM 2026-09-17, REBOOT 2026-09-18, HapticWorld 2026-09-25, Kaedim study 2026-09-30, HumanoidToolBench 2026-10-01), and Robocurve's funding announcement is dated 2026-09-14. — [2609.03611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03611), [2609.20649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20649), [2609.22591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22591), [2609.31924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31924), [2610.00731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00731), [2610.02089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02089), [robocurve.org](https://robocurve.org/)
- Venue: of the 43 additions, the ones with a confirmed peer-reviewed venue are ICRA 2023 (TOTO), ICRA 2026 (CEBench), ICML 2025 (BiAssemble), ICCV 2025 (DexH2R), NeurIPS 2025 (SutureBot), CoRL 2026 (Mem-World) and an IROS 2026 workshop (DexTouch-WM); the rest are arXiv preprints, competitions or non-paper sources. — [2306.00942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00942), [2602.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22663), [2506.06221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06221), [2506.23152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23152), [2510.20965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20965), [2606.18960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18960), [2609.20649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20649)

### Inferences
- Pattern 1, evaluation buried in a paper about something else (about 16 of 43): twelve proxies (OSCAR, WEAVER, Hi-WM, Mem-World, GeniWorld, RoboSnap, Agentic Real2Sim, ViTacWorld, DexTouch-WM, HapticWorld, WorldComposer, ABC-Sim), plus RoboART, CEBench, BiAssemble and TOTO, whose titles carry no "benchmark for real-world evaluation" phrasing a keyword search would hit. Remedy: search by what the experiment reports ("correlation with real-world success", "Pearson", "MMRV", "trials per task") rather than by what the paper is called, and enumerate world-model and real-to-sim method papers explicitly.
- Pattern 2, scope framed as "generalist / VLA manipulation policy" (about 11 of 43): surgical (SutureBot, ICRA surgery competition), food (Food Topping Challenge), construction (RCC), laboratory (LabDex), human-robot collaboration (CT benchmark, DexH2R), sensor comparison (TacO), perception scored by robot picking (BPC), industrial mobile manipulation (RoboCup@Work) and standards (ASTM WK92144) are physical manipulation evaluations outside that frame. This is a scope rule, the same kind that excluded DOMINO, not a search failure.
- Pattern 3, no group for tools (4 entries plus Inspect Robots): the first survey's methodology file covers evaluation statistics in depth, but the catalog's four groups have no place for success detectors, scoring toolkits or statistics packages, so nothing from that file reached the list.
- Pattern 4, hand-off loss between parallel researchers (3 to 5 entries): TOTO, the Sim2Real Challenges, AI for Robotic Surgery and GeniWorld were each seen by a first-survey researcher and dropped as someone else's slice, a roster footnote or a catch-all row.
- Pattern 5, non-academic and non-English sources (3 entries, more unverified): a company site, a blog and a public sporting event; Chinese-language standards and CAICT evaluation services were found only as snippets because the pages could not be fetched, which suggests the first survey faced the same barrier.
- Pattern 6, recency (about 10 of 43): six additions are from the last month and four more from August 2026. A survey dated 1 October cannot be complete for September; a re-run of the arXiv queries monthly would catch these.
- Venue bias toward robotics conferences is real but small: two additions come from vision or ML main conferences with no robotics-venue counterpart (DexH2R at ICCV, BiAssemble at ICML) and one from NeurIPS (SutureBot). Competition coverage was biased by year (ICRA 2025–2026 only) more than by venue type.
- Plain misses also exist: RADAR (February 2026), DexHoldem (May 2026), LabDex (August 2026) and The Imitator Game (August 2026) are titled as benchmarks with real-robot evaluation and fall inside the original scope; nothing structural explains them. A check of their abstracts shows a wording effect: RADAR, LabDex and The Imitator Game do not contain the exact phrase "real-world benchmark" (RADAR says "under realistic conditions"; LabDex says "real-world dataset and benchmark"), and DexHoldem contains the phrase but not the word "robot", so none of the four is returned by the phrase queries that recover the listed benchmarks.

### Gaps
- The pattern counts are my classification of 43 entries and overlap (an entry can be both recent and out-of-frame); they are indicative, not exact.
- Whether the first survey actually used arXiv phrase search is inferred from which papers it found, not documented in its notes.
- Non-English coverage could not be tested properly: Zhihu and Tencent News pages were blocked, so the size of the Chinese-language blind spot is unknown.
- CVPR, ECCV and ICCV proceedings were not scanned systematically; only search-engine and arXiv queries were used, so the vision-venue estimate rests on few cases.
