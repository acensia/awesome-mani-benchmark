# Diagnostic and stress-test simulation benchmarks for VLA / generalist manipulation policies (2024 – Oct 2026)

Scope: simulation benchmarks introduced 2024-01 to 2026-10-02 that probe one specific weakness with a policy executing in closed loop. 81 benchmarks were verified and written to `diagnostic_sim_benchmarks.entries.json`. Each was opened in this session (arXiv HTML, arXiv abstract page, or project/GitHub page). Numbers were read through an automated page-extraction tool, so individual figures should be spot-checked before being quoted as exact; places where the extraction was internally inconsistent are flagged under Gaps.

## 1. Robustness and perturbation: LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, other LIBERO variants, INT-ACT, Eva-VLA, VLA-Arena, VLATest and other perturbation / adversarial / OOD benchmarks

### Takeaway
LIBERO has become the default substrate for stress tests: 15 LIBERO-named variants are catalogued here (14 of them posted between Oct 2025 and Sep 2026) and 26 of the 81 entries run on LIBERO or a LIBERO-style stack. The robustness variants each isolate one axis (static perturbations, compound perturbations, mid-episode change, occlusion, corrupted observation streams, failure recovery, execution error). The consistent finding is that policies scoring 90–98% on standard LIBERO fall to roughly 0–65% under modest shifts, with camera viewpoint and robot initial state the most damaging and language variation often the least (because language is being ignored).

### Cited Findings
- LIBERO-Plus: 10,030 perturbed test tasks, 7 dimensions (object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state, language instruction, lighting, background texture, sensor noise), 21 sub-dimensions, 5 difficulty levels, 20K+ training trajectories; 10 models (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT ×3, π0, π0-FAST, NORA, WorldVLA, UniVLA, RIPT-VLA); success drops from ~95% to below 30%; camera shifts cost 37–90 points, initial-state shifts 44–87; goal-replacement language tests drop to near zero; no real-robot experiments — [arXiv 2510.13626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13626)
- LIBERO-Plus was presented as a CVPR 2026 poster under the title "LIBERO-Plus: A Progressive Robustness Benchmark for Visual-Language-Action Models" (same 13 authors); the arXiv record still carries the title "In-depth Robustness Analysis of Vision-Language-Action Models" and no venue — [CVPR 2026 poster page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/38735); [GitHub README](https://github.com/sylvestf/LIBERO-plus)
- LIBERO-Plus keeps a results table in its GitHub README — [GitHub](https://github.com/sylvestf/LIBERO-plus)
- LIBERO-PRO: perturbs object attributes, initial positions, instructions and environments (4 dimensions, combinable); OpenVLA, π0, π0.5; >90% on standard LIBERO collapses to 0.0% in the generalized setting, π0.5 keeps 0.38 on position changes; simulation only; no venue on arXiv — [arXiv 2510.03827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827)
- LIBERO-X: 600 tasks in 100 scenes, 2,520 VR-teleoperated demos, 5 graded levels (local spatial, extended spatial, scene topology, visual attributes, semantically equivalent instruction); OpenVLA-OFT, π0, X-VLA, GR00T-N1.5, π0.5; π0.5 65.2% at level 1 → 18.0% at level 5; MuJoCo; simulation only — [arXiv 2602.06556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06556)
- LIBERO-VPro: perturbs the observation stream during execution (visual evidence degradation, camera staleness, cross-view inconsistency, task-relevant scene variation); 40 tasks, 12 challenge categories, 96 settings, 3,296 task-condition cases, ~196,000 simulated episodes; OpenVLA-OFT 97.0→56.4%, π0 92.5→60.6%, π0.5 97.2→64.5%, plus three world-action models (FastWAM, LingBot-VA, LaWAM) at 66–67%; 200 real rollouts on Franka Research 3 with π0.5 and LaWAM, trends "broadly consistent", no coefficient — [arXiv 2609.24350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24350)
- LIBERO-Occ: 2,000 occluded instances (500 per suite), 3 occlusion types × 3 severities; OpenVLA-OFT 95.75→47.95%, π0.5 90.00→40.55%, UniVLA 88.25→57.10%; proposed VIM 65.05%; authors state it is a controlled simulation study, not a substitute for real evaluation — [arXiv 2606.10862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10862)
- LIBERO-MAX: world changes after the policy starts acting; 8,000 Base/Dynamic pairs (5,600 from LIBERO-Plus, 2,400 from LIBERO-PRO), 8 event types (target relocation, receptacle relocation, camera shift, illumination switch, sensor-noise onset, visual-theme switch, distractor burst, obstacle insertion); 14 policies (8 VLAs, VLA-JEPA, 5 WAMs); every policy loses 11.0–25.7 points; 20.8–56.1% of episodes solved in Base fail after the event; WAMs are not consistently better or worse than VLAs — [arXiv 2609.36518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36518)
- LIBERO-RECOVER: failure recovery at four levels (action retry, action adaptation, object-state recovery, environmental recovery); 130 subtasks, 2,178 recovery scenarios; OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T-N1.5, π0, π0-FAST, Wan2-Policy, Cosmos-Predict2-Policy; all lose >50% after naturally occurring failures; recovery success ≈40% at L1, ≈3% at L4 — [arXiv 2609.05178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05178)
- LIBERO-CTRL ("Beyond Single-Axis Testing"): matched single-axis vs simultaneous perturbation on the same instance; 40 tasks, 6 axes (camera, lighting, robot pose, sensor, actuation, language), 3 severities, 10,400 rollouts per checkpoint; π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, UniVLA, SmolVLA, VLA-JEPA, PredVLA, MINERVA; up to 29.0% of configurations change outcome while the aggregate barely moves — [arXiv 2609.15940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15940)
- RoboStress: execution errors from the robot itself (Stribeck friction, backlash, joint compliance, gravity-compensation error) in 7 scenarios on 40 LIBERO tasks (MuJoCo 3.7.0, robosuite 1.4.1); π0.5 49.1% and π0 41.7% under stress; real tests on two AgileX Piper arms for the proposed self-compensation method — [arXiv 2609.37334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37334)
- RoboTwin 2.0-Plus: LIBERO-Plus-style 7-type perturbation suite ported to bimanual RoboTwin 2.0; LingBot-VA 74.2%, Fast-WAM 72.7%, MOTUS 71.5%, π0.5 58.6%, X-VLA 53.1%; WAMs robust to visual perturbations, weak on camera and robot-state shifts; simulation only — [arXiv 2603.22078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22078)
- RoboTwin-Phys: 13 physical-parameter dimensions (mass, centre of mass, friction, restitution, damping, table tilt/height, external force, etc.) on 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks; Fast-WAM 91.88→44.24%, Motus 88.66→39.60%, π0.5 82.74→31.60%, Galaxea G0.5 93.70→37.82%; simulation only; arXiv comment calls it a technical report — [arXiv 2609.26292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26292)
- Eva-VLA: black-box search for worst-case physical variations (3D object transformation, illumination, adversarial patch) on 40 LIBERO tasks; OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, UniVLA, π0.5; worst-case failure 68–88% under 3D transformations; OpenVLA >90% failure on LIBERO-Long; three real "put A into B" tasks on an AgileX Piper, qualitative agreement only — [arXiv 2509.18953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18953)
- INT-ACT: 50 SimplerEnv/ManiSkill2 WidowX tasks (expanded from 4) in 3 categories and 10 sub-categories (object diversity, language complexity, vision-language thinking); π0 variants, SpatialVLA, Magma, Octo; intention correct in 80–100% of cases while execution success collapses on OOD variants; simulation only; arXiv comment "Under review" — [arXiv 2506.09930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09930)
- VLA-Arena (ICML 2026): 170 tasks in 11 suites under 4 dimensions (Safety, Distractor, Extrapolation, Long Horizon), difficulty L0–L2, language W0–W4, vision V0–V4; MuJoCo; OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π0, π0-FAST, UniVLA, SmolVLA; steep L0→L2 degradation, success often achieved by violating safety constraints; Franka Research 3 runs showed the same degradation trend, no coefficient; leaderboard on project site — [arXiv 2512.22539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22539); [project](https://vla-arena.github.io)
- VLATest (FSE 2025): fuzzing framework, 4 tasks, 18,604 generated scenes, 10 operators (target/confounding object type, position, orientation, number; lighting; camera pose); RT-1 ×3, RT-1-X, Octo ×2, OpenVLA-7b; average success 12.4/6.0/1.2/0.5% on the four tasks; only 34.0% of passing cases survive camera mutation and 61.3% survive lighting mutation; simulation only — [arXiv 2409.12894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12894)
- LADEV: language-driven scene generation plus instruction paraphrasing on SimplerEnv; 4 tasks, 4,000+ scenes; same seven models as VLATest; 10–30% drop on unseen objects, 5–15% under paraphrase — [arXiv 2410.05191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05191)
- PVEP ("Manipulation Facing Threats"): blur, noise, brightness, typographic prompts, adversarial patches on 14 VIMA and 6 SimplerEnv tasks; LLaRA and OpenVLA; blur adds ~47 points of failure; white-box patch gives 94.5% / 100% failure — [arXiv 2409.13174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13174)
- AttackVLA: 6 adversarial and 3 backdoor attacks on 40 LIBERO tasks; OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, π0-FAST; targeted backdoor success 75.35/51.63/48.23%; attacks reproduced on a real Franka against π0-FAST — [arXiv 2511.12149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12149)
- NEBULA: capability tests (control, perception, language, dynamic adaptation, spatial reasoning, robustness; 3 difficulty levels) plus 4 stress tests (inference frequency, latency, stability, adaptability) on SAPIEN/ManiSkill3; GR00T-N1.5, SpatialVLA, RDT-1B, MT-ACT, Diffusion Policy, ACT; dynamic adaptation and robustness scores near zero for all — [arXiv 2510.16263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16263)
- RoboView-Bias: 2,127 grasping instances varying 141 colours, camera orientation/pose, distance, shape; π0 46.53% success with 53.05% coefficient of variation; VLM-driven agents MOKA and SimpleAgent also tested — [arXiv 2509.22356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22356)
- Adjacent protocols that reuse these suites rather than add tasks: IndustrialVLA-Bench runs six models on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus (10,030 episodes) and LIBERO-Para (4,092) and finds clean averages differ by 1.58 points while robustness and paraphrase summaries span 14.62 and 31.08 points — [arXiv 2609.25562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25562); "Is Success All You Need?" adds trajectory-quality metrics on LIBERO-Plus (π0.5 mean Cartesian jerk +24.1% under camera perturbation among successful episodes) — [arXiv 2610.01351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01351); VLAQuantBench measures post-training quantization in closed loop over 94,574 episodes on LIBERO, SIMPLER, CALVIN and VLABench — [arXiv 2609.25376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25376)

### Inferences
- Perturbation axes by benchmark: camera (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-VPro, LIBERO-MAX, LIBERO-CTRL, VLATest, RoboTwin 2.0-Plus, RoboView-Bias); lighting (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-MAX, LIBERO-CTRL, Eva-VLA, VLATest, PVEP); layout/position (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-X, LIBERO-MAX, VLATest); language (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-X, LIBERO-CTRL, INT-ACT, LADEV, VLATest); object identity/appearance (LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-X, INT-ACT, VLATest, LADEV, Eva-VLA); sensor noise (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-MAX, LIBERO-CTRL, PVEP); physics and actuation (RoboTwin-Phys, RoboStress, LIBERO-CTRL); time-varying events (LIBERO-MAX, LIBERO-VPro, LIBERO-RECOVER).
- The 2024 generation (VLATest, LADEV, PVEP) used SimplerEnv/ManiSkill2 with RT-1, Octo and OpenVLA; from late 2025 the field moved to LIBERO with π0/π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT, and from 2026 to RoboTwin 2.0 and to VLA-versus-WAM comparisons.
- Several 2026 papers argue that aggregate robustness scores still hide structure: compound outcomes (LIBERO-CTRL), behaviour quality among successes ("Is Success All You Need?"), and unsafe successes (see section 6).

### Gaps
- LIBERO-PRO's task count and LIBERO-Plus's physics engine were not stated in the extracted text; LIBERO's robosuite/MuJoCo stack is taken from other LIBERO-derived papers (RoboStress, MEMOBench, LangGap). The LIBERO-Occ extraction named PyBullet, which conflicts; treated as unconfirmed.
- No benchmark named exactly "LIBERO-OOD" exists as a robustness suite; the name belongs to a skill-recombination test (section 2).
- The CVF open-access page for LIBERO-Plus returned HTTP 403; CVPR 2026 status rests on the conference poster page.
- RoboTwin 2.0-Plus task count was not extracted.
- NEBULA's task count was unclear in the extracted text (figures of 216,000 trajectories and 222,000 videos were both returned).

## 2. Generalization levels and compositional generalization: GemBench and others that grade generalization in levels

### Takeaway
Level-graded generalization benchmarks consistently show a cliff between "same task, new placement/object" and "new task or new composition": top policies drop from ~90% at the easiest level to near 0% on unseen long-horizon or compositional tasks. Most of the RLBench-based ones (GemBench, AGNOSTOS, DeCoBench, HiMan-Bench, ArtiBench) evaluate 3D keyframe policies first and VLAs second or not at all.

### Cited Findings
- GemBench (ICRA 2025): RLBench; 16 training tasks (31 variations, 3,100 demos); 44 test tasks (92 variations) in 4 levels — L1 new placements, L2 new rigid objects, L3 new articulated objects, L4 unseen long-horizon tasks; Hiveformer, PolarNet, RVT-2, 3D Diffuser Actor, 3D-LOTUS, 3D-LOTUS++; L1 up to 94.3%, L4 ≤0.3% for all but 3D-LOTUS++ (17.4%); real UR5 experiments test the authors' method on 7 seen and 7 unseen variations — [arXiv 2410.01345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01345); [project](https://www.di.ens.fr/willow/research/gembench/)
- AGNOSTOS: zero-shot cross-task generalization on RLBench; 18 seen tasks, 23 unseen tasks (13 level-1 with partial semantic overlap, 10 level-2 novel); baselines include OpenVLA, RDT, π0, LLARVA, SAM2Act, 3D-LOTUS++, VoxPoser; π0 17.5% on unseen tasks, proposed X-ICM (72B) 30.1%; real xArm7 tests of X-ICM on 5 tasks — [arXiv 2505.15660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15660)
- HiMan-Bench (RoboHiMan): 258 tasks (114 atomic, 144 compositional, with perturbed versions) built from DeCoBench tasks plus 12 Colosseum perturbation factors; training-data levels L1–L4; evaluation modes vanilla / decoupled planner / coupled; RVT-2, 3D Diffuser Actor, π0, π0.5; near zero on compositional tasks without a planner, RVT-2 with rule-based planner 39.5%; π0.5 on Mobile ALOHA: 17.5%→10% (vanilla) and 47.5%→27.5% (rule-based planner) under perturbation — [arXiv 2510.13149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13149)
- DeCoBench (RA-L 2026 per arXiv comment): 10 atomic and 12 compositional RLBench tasks; RVT-2, 3DDA, ARP near 0% on compositions without DeCo; real Franka test of DeCo on 9 novel long-horizon tasks (53.33% vs 0%) — [arXiv 2505.00527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00527)
- ClevrSkills (NeurIPS 2024 D&B): ManiSkill2; 33 tasks in 3 levels (12 simple, 15 intermediate, 6 complex); ~330K trajectories; JAT, RoboFlamingo, Octo, StreamRoboLM; L0 24–63%, L1/L2 ≤ ~6% even after fine-tuning; simulation only — [arXiv 2411.09052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09052)
- LIBERO-OOD: 20 tasks (10 goal-ood, 10 spatial-ood) requiring recombination of sub-trajectories seen in training; π0 9%, π0-FAST 15%, OpenVLA-OFT 1%, UniVLA 21%; text-latent interpolation lifts π0 to 83%; simulation only — [arXiv 2505.03500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03500)
- ArtiBench: 132 articulated scenes, 449 variations, levels L0–L4 (random placement, novel parts, novel instances, novel categories, long-horizon composition); 3D-LOTUS++ 35.8% average; only the proposed ArtiBrain completes L4; real Franka Research 3 tests of the method — [arXiv 2511.20330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20330)
- GenManip-Bench: 200 curated Isaac Sim scenarios testing appearance, spatial, common-sense and long-horizon instructions; CoPA 23.0%, MOKA 14.0%; GR-1 95.0% single-task but 0.0% on unseen instructions/objects — [arXiv 2506.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10966)
- RoboCAS: SAPIEN; picking/selecting/searching in scattered, orderly, stacked arrangements; 7,300 episodes; RT-1 34.2/23.3/10.2%, RoboFlamingo 15.6/13.3/0% — [arXiv 2407.06951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06951)
- EBench: Isaac Sim mobile dual-arm; 26 tasks; 5 capability axes and 4 generalization dimensions (background, object, instruction, mix); π0.5 41.0%, π0 34.4%, InternVLA-A1 27.6%, X-VLA 24.7%; hosted leaderboard; explicitly simulation-only "screening" — [arXiv 2606.18239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18239); [leaderboard](https://internrobotics.shlab.org.cn/eval/evaluation-list)
- Behavior-Skill: 50 BEHAVIOR-1K tasks annotated with 235,492 skill instances in 34 categories; π0.5 42.4% and GR00T N1.7 36.9%; contact-rich skills (open lid, close door, pour) are the persistent bottlenecks — [arXiv 2608.30536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30536)
- RoboEval: 8 bimanual tasks with 3–5 variations, 3,000+ demos, 14 behavioural metrics; the top-success model leads on only 34 of 104 other metric comparisons; ACT, Diffusion Policy, GR00T N1.6, X-VLA, π0.5 — [arXiv 2507.00435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00435)
- An unnamed multi-object picking study on ManiSkill shows SmolVLA at 90% under small jitter and 2% under full randomization, and 44%→0% on held-out object-region pairings, with up to 100,000 demos — [arXiv 2602.24143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24143)

### Inferences
- Level definitions fall into three families: (a) distance from training distribution in objects/placement (GemBench L1–L3, ArtiBench L0–L3, LIBERO-X); (b) task novelty (AGNOSTOS L1–L2, GemBench L4, LIBERO-OOD); (c) composition depth (ClevrSkills L0–L2, DeCoBench, HiMan-Bench).
- GemBench is commonly cited as a VLA generalization benchmark, but its own paper evaluates no VLA; VLA numbers on RLBench-style level benchmarks come from AGNOSTOS and HiMan-Bench.

### Gaps
- AGNOSTOS: the extracted improvement margins over π0 were not consistent with the absolute numbers; only absolutes are reported. No venue on the arXiv record.
- GemBench: the extraction called RLBench "PyBullet-based", contradicting other RLBench papers (CoppeliaSim/PyRep). The simulated arm was not confirmed.
- GenManip and RoboEval: venue not on the arXiv record; RoboEval's simulator was not named in the extracted text.
- RADAR (arXiv 2602.10980), which grades VLA generalization under dynamics, is a real-robot benchmark and was excluded from this slice; CEBench (arXiv 2602.22663, ICRA 2026) is a broad cross-embodiment suite and was left to the broad-suites researcher.

## 3. Long horizon and memory: RoboCerebra, MemoryBench, MIKASA-Robo and others

### Takeaway
Memory became a benchmark category of its own in 2025–2026: at least 12 simulation suites now isolate non-Markovian manipulation, and all report that reactive VLAs (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT) score between ~0 and ~30% on memory-dependent tasks, with explicit memory modules roughly doubling that but staying under ~65%.

### Cited Findings
- RoboCerebra (NeurIPS 2025): LIBERO-based; 1,000 human-annotated trajectories over 100 task variants; mean 2,972 steps (about 6× earlier long-horizon sets); sub-task types ideal, memory exploration, memory execution, random disturbance, observation mismatching, mix; OpenVLA controller with GPT-4o / Qwen2.5-VL / LLaVA-Next-Video planners; best VLM planner 16.04%, ground-truth plan 25.16%; simulation only — [arXiv 2506.06677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06677); [project](https://robocerebra.github.io/)
- MemoryBench (in SAM2Act): 3 RLBench tasks (reopen_drawer, put_block_back, rearrange_block) built to break the Markov assumption; RVT-2 54.0%, SAM2Act 55.0%, SAM2Act+ 94.3%; one real memory task on a Franka (SAM2Act+ 70%, RVT-2 40%) — [arXiv 2501.18564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18564)
- MIKASA-Robo (ICLR 2026 per project page): ManiSkill3; 32 tasks in 12 groups; object, spatial, sequential and capacity memory; RL baselines plus Octo, OpenVLA, π0, SpatialVLA; success drops to near zero as memory demand grows; three tasks on an SO-101 arm mirror the simulation ordering — [arXiv 2502.10550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10550); [project](https://sites.google.com/view/memorybenchrobots/)
- MIKASA-Robo-VLA (submitted 2026-09-30): 90 language-conditioned tasks, 26 families, 10 memory types, 22,500 oracle episodes; π0.5 0.211 mean success on a 14-task subset and 0.017 on long-split tasks; simulation only — [arXiv 2610.00604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00604)
- RoboMME (ICML 2026): ManiSkill; 16 tasks in 4 suites (Counting, Permanence, Reference, Imitation), 1,600 demos; 14 memory variants on π0.5 plus π0.5, SAM2Act+, MemER; best learned variant 44.51%, oracle symbolic 84.08%; four real tasks show "similar patterns" (25/40 and 19/40) — [arXiv 2603.04639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04639)
- RoboMME-Interference: 9 task families with 0/1/3/7 distractor sessions between demonstration and query; 12 memory systems; retrieval-based variant stays at 44.7–44.9% while others decay — [arXiv 2606.22338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22338)
- RMBench: RoboTwin 2.0 / SAPIEN; 9 bimanual tasks split by memory complexity M(1) (5 tasks) and M(n) (4 tasks); Diffusion Policy, ACT, π0.5, X-VLA, Mem-0; best baseline X-VLA 11.8% / 7.3%; Mem-0 42.0% overall; three real tasks on an X-One dual-arm robot (Mem-0 22.5%, π0.5 5.83%) — [arXiv 2603.01229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01229)
- RoboMemArena: 26 simulated and 5 real tasks; transferring, occlusion, counting, sequence; mean 1,076 steps; 68.9% of subtasks memory-dependent; π0.5 21.5%, HiF-VLA 16.9%, MemoryVLA 15.0%, MemER 27.3%, PrediMem 38.5%; real: π0.5 20%, MemER 40%, PrediMem 52% — [arXiv 2605.10921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10921)
- MEMOBench: 30 LIBERO-stack tasks with 4,200 executable checkpoint instances; separate storage/update/compression rates; π0.5 30.4% success with 54.9% storage, 13.5% update, 4.2% compression rate; simulation only — [arXiv 2609.07047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07047)
- HIDE: 15 RLBench tasks (repetition counting, historical-state recall, execution-progress tracking); OpenVLA 1.6%, π0 22.1%, GR00T-N1.7 26.4%, RVT-2 42.9%, SAM2Act+ 51.2%, SEEK 62.9%; four real Franka tasks (SEEK 89%, π0.5 13%) — [arXiv 2609.38886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38886)
- LIBERO-Mem (AAAI 2026): 10 tasks extending LIBERO-Goal with object-level partial observability; baseline VLAs average 5.0% subgoal completion — [arXiv 2511.11478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11478)
- RuleSafe: SAPIEN, Unitree H1-2 with Inspire hands; 20 lock rules on 10 safes; non-Markovian articulated manipulation; π0 80.0% on the simplest rule, CogACT 20.0% — [arXiv 2603.09513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09513)
- COIN: ManiSkill3; 90 tasks (50 interactive); hierarchical VLM+VLA 1.5–2.2%, ReKep 3.26%, human teleoperation 40% — [arXiv 2604.16886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16886)
- BiCoord: 18 long-horizon tightly coupled bimanual tasks on RoboTwin 2.0; π0 46.4% single-task and 27.2% multi-task — [arXiv 2604.05831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05831)
- ActiveArena: 35 active-perception tasks on an Astribot S1 simulation extending RoboTwin 2.0; π0.5, MemER, SaPaVe, HiF-VLA, MemoryVLA, FAST-WAM; best in-house variant 62.97% ID / 47.60% OOD; eight real tasks with consistent ordering — [arXiv 2609.24124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24124)
- BAVO-Bench: 5 bimanual active-vision tasks under unexpected occlusion; π0.5 falls from 42% to 4.0% — [arXiv 2609.37292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37292)

### Inferences
- Benchmarks differ in what they call memory: spatial recall (MemoryBench), typed taxonomies (MIKASA 4 types → 10 types; RoboMME 4 types; RoboMemArena 4 types), amount of history (RMBench M(1)/M(n)), cross-session interference (RoboMME-Interference), process-level operations (MEMOBench), and memory combined with dynamics (DOMINO-Long, section 4) or active perception (ActiveArena).
- π0.5 without memory is the common reference point and lands at 21.1% (MIKASA-Robo-VLA subset), 21.5% (RoboMemArena), 30.4% (MEMOBench), 35.4% (DOMINO-Long); the numbers are not comparable across suites but all sit far below its LIBERO scores.
- RMBench's stated motivation (MemoryBench hard to reproduce, MIKASA RL-oriented, LIBERO-Long not memory-dependent) explains why several memory suites appeared in parallel in early 2026.

### Gaps
- RoboMemArena's simulator was not named in the extracted text. MEMOBench's embodiment description ("bimanual") looked inconsistent with LIBERO and was omitted.
- RoboSPA (EMNLP 2026) covers long-horizon procedural planning but its arXiv HTML returned 404; only the abstract and GitHub README were read, so the four evaluated VLAs are not named.
- FindingDory (arXiv 2506.15635, Habitat) tests memory in embodied agents but was not opened and is not catalogued.

## 4. Dynamic environments and reactivity: DOMINO and other moving-target, conveyor, disturbance or latency benchmarks

### Takeaway
Dynamic manipulation went from no VLA benchmark to seven within 2026. DOMINO (ECCV 2026) and DOM (in DynamicVLA, NeurIPS 2026 per arXiv comment) are the two anchors; follow-ups add long horizons (DOMINO-Long, MotionForge), reaction-critical tasks (ReflexBench, DynBench) and speed variants (PhysMani-Bench). Standard VLAs score roughly 5–37% on these suites; every paper pairs the benchmark with its own method.

### Cited Findings
- DOMINO, full detail: paper "Towards Generalizable Robotic Manipulation in Dynamic Environments", arXiv comment "Accepted to ECCV 2026"; built on RoboTwin 2.0 in SAPIEN; 35 dynamic tasks; 110K+ expert trajectories; five embodiments (project page lists Aloha-AgileX, Franka Panda, Piper, UR5-WSG, ARX-X5); three dynamics levels — L1 constant velocity, L2 polynomial trajectories (degree 2–5), L3 multi-segment with abrupt transitions; speed set by a coefficient α (α = 0.1 means 0.1 m/s maximum); metrics are success rate and a manipulation score (route completion, ×0.5 if the target leaves the workspace or view, ×0.8 on collision) — [arXiv 2603.15620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15620); [project](https://h-embodvis.github.io/DOMINO/)
- DOMINO results: static-trained policies evaluated zero-shot on dynamic tasks — ACT 27.7%→6.5%, OpenVLA-OFT 17.5%→6.7%, π0.5 44.8%→7.5%; fine-tuning on dynamic data adds under 3 points (9.4%, 9.1%, 9.6%); OpenVLA 1.54% SR / 6.10 MS, RDT-1B 5.34 / 17.71, π0 8.17 / 23.96, InternVLA-M1 5.40 / 27.57, PUMA 17.20 / 34.97; PUMA 10.5% at level 2 and 4.6% at level 3 vs π0.5 7.9% and 1.6% — [arXiv 2603.15620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15620)
- DOMINO real-robot section: AgileX Piper; five DOMINO tasks (Click Bell, Grab Roller, Lift Bottle, Move Playing Card, Press Stapler); 50 teleoperated demos per task; simulation-pretrained models LoRA-fine-tuned on the real demos; 20 trials per task; averages ACT 7%, RDT-1B 8%, π0.5 24%, PUMA 42%; no sim–real correlation is computed — [arXiv 2603.15620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15620)
- DOMINO releases: code, dataset on Hugging Face and PUMA weights; no leaderboard on the project page — [project](https://h-embodvis.github.io/DOMINO/); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/H-EmbodVis/DOMINO)
- DOM (Dynamic Object Manipulation benchmark, in DynamicVLA): Isaac Sim; 200K synthetic episodes, 2.8K scenes, 206 objects, plus 2K real episodes; Franka and AgileX PiPER; nine sub-dimensions under interaction, perception and generalization; DynamicVLA 47.06% vs 12.33% for the strongest baseline (VLASH); baselines Diffusion Policy, ACT, OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5, SmolVLA, GR00T-N1.5, RTC, VLA-Adapter-Pro; real evaluation of 16 tasks × 60 trials (960 trials per method); arXiv comment "NeurIPS 2026" — [arXiv 2601.22153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22153)
- DOMINO-Long (in D2-VLA): 10 multi-stage tasks, 50 demos each, 201–911 steps; cues seen early are needed later while objects move; PUMA 20.6%, π0.5 35.4%, D2-VLA 60.0%; eight real tasks on a Unitree G1D — [arXiv 2609.34792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34792)
- MotionForge: Isaac Sim; 40 tasks (17 long-horizon), 11 motion patterns, ~20,000 episodes, factorized shifts (object, background, lighting, motion speed); ACT 22.20% best in-domain, GR00T N1.7 11.55%, X-VLA 13.10%, π0.5 4.85%, FastWAM 1.15%; best under joint shift 5.85%; freezing the scene adds 21.7–27.5 points; simulation only — [arXiv 2609.25689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25689)
- ReflexBench (in Reflex): 6 tasks (conveyor pick-and-place, ball catching, whack-a-mole, rolling-ball interception, ball throwing, rotating-peg insertion), 200 demos each; ReflexVLA 50.4%, PUMA 50.2%, SmolVLA 38.3%, DynamicVLA 37.3%, π0.5 36.9%, OpenVLA-OFT 36.0%; three real tasks on an AgileX Piper — [arXiv 2608.14379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14379)
- DynBench (in DSDyn-VLA): MuJoCo, Franka; 9 tasks ported and expanded from four real dynamic tasks; π0.5 9.7%, RTC 19.4%, VLASH 27.1%, DynamicVLA 34.3%, DSDyn-VLA 48.4%; four real tasks on an AgileX Piper, 50 trials each — [arXiv 2609.39198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39198)
- PhysMani-Bench (ECCV 2026 per arXiv comment): extended from RLBench; 16 tasks (8 groups × normal/high speed), 2,560 trajectories; PhysMani 45.9%, 3DFA 37.8%, π0.5 8.3%; four real tasks on an Astribot S1 — [arXiv 2607.01938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01938)
- Mid-episode change on static tasks: LIBERO-MAX (8 event types, 8,000 pairs; see section 1) — [arXiv 2609.36518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36518); VLA-Arena includes DynamicObstacles and DynamicDistractors suites — [arXiv 2512.22539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22539); NEBULA includes latency and inference-frequency stress tests — [arXiv 2510.16263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16263)

### Inferences
- DOMINO differs from DOM in substrate (RoboTwin 2.0/SAPIEN bimanual vs Isaac Sim single-arm), in having explicit motion-complexity levels, and in real-robot role: DOMINO's real section tests method transfer on 5 tasks, DOM includes a 2K-episode real set as part of the benchmark.
- Dynamic suites already form a small citation graph: DOMINO → DOMINO-Long; PUMA and DynamicVLA serve as baselines in ReflexBench and DynBench; MotionForge positions itself against DOM, DOMINO and PhysMani-Bench.
- Every dynamic benchmark found was released together with a proposed method that tops its own table; none has yet been evaluated by an independent group in the sources read.
- The Kinetix latency benchmark is 2D, state-based and not manipulation-specific, so it was kept out of the JSON catalog.

### Gaps
- DOMINO: the paper's "6.3% absolute improvement" claim could not be reconciled with the extracted table (PUMA 17.20% vs listed baselines up to 9.6%); the full baseline table should be checked in the PDF. The GitHub README returned HTTP 504 and was not read.
- ReflexBench's simulator and DOMINO-Long's physics engine were not named in the extracted text.
- No venue was found on arXiv for Reflex, DSDyn-VLA, D2-VLA or MotionForge.
- "Toward Real-Time VLAs" (arXiv 2609.39822) turned out to be a physical-robot study, not a simulation benchmark.

## 5. Language, spatial and embodied reasoning in closed-loop manipulation: VLABench and others

### Takeaway
A cluster of benchmarks shows that VLAs frequently execute a well-learned motion without reading the instruction: grasp or reach success stays high (85–99%) while instruction-conditioned success sits near chance or collapses by 20–70 points under paraphrase, counterfactual or implicit instructions.

### Cited Findings
- VLABench (ICCV 2025 per its GitHub README): MuJoCo/dm_control; 100 task categories (60 primitive, 40 composite); 2,164 objects; dimensions mesh & texture, spatial, common sense & world knowledge, semantic instruction, physical laws, reasoning; OpenVLA 11.74% seen / 7.93% unseen, Octo 1.34 / 0.77%, RDT-1B 15.37 / 9.08%; simulation only — [arXiv 2412.18194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18194); [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/VLABench)
- LIBERO-Para (EMNLP 2026 main, oral): 4,092 paraphrases of 10 LIBERO-Goal instructions over 43 action×object paraphrase types; PRIDE metric; seven configurations (OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5, Xiaomi-Robotics-0, X-VLA, VLA-Adapter); drops of 22.8–51.9 points; 79.5–95.5% of failures are planning-level — [arXiv 2603.28301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28301)
- LangGap: 99 tasks (40 LIBERO + 59 with changed object, target, spatial description or drawer action in the same layout); π0.5 93.8%→21.4%, 0% on changed target location — [arXiv 2603.00592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00592)
- LIBERO-CF: 50 counterfactual tasks in 4 suites; OpenVLA-OFT 0.4%, π0 9.6%, π0.5 13.2%, X-VLA 13.8% vs 65–97% in-domain; Franka Research 3 tests of the proposed guidance — [arXiv 2602.17659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659)
- SimplerEnv-Instruct (in InstructVLA): 80 zero-shot tasks (50 task aggregation, 30 situated reasoning), 1.1K trials; InstructVLA 46.2%, OpenVLA (FT & GPT) 35.6%, Magma 23.8%, SpatialVLA 16.5%, π0 12.0% — [arXiv 2507.17520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17520)
- InstructMove: Isaac Lab; language-indispensable picking with semantic distractors; π0.5 0.82 reach / 0.57 lift on category picking; with an absent target π0.5 still reaches some object 99% of the time; 200-demo real PiperX study — [arXiv 2608.22990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22990)
- ConflictVLA-Bench: 2,826 LIBERO tasks with invalid premises (instruction-internal, object-grounding, spatial-relation, physical-feasibility), 14,530 rollouts per model; 8 VLAs; target-approach ratios stay at 0.586–0.741 even when the premise is invalid — [arXiv 2609.31792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31792)
- RoboIRGBench: 40 variants of 11 RoboMME-based tasks for implicit referential grounding; 45.82% (explicit) → 16.85% (reasoning-mediated) for one memory variant; real Franka: 70–85% → 5–10% — [arXiv 2609.34384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34384)
- RoboSemanticBench: robot answers multiple-choice maths/knowledge questions by picking a block; nine VLAs; grasp success 85–99%, task success near chance; best π0.5 at 21.8% — [arXiv 2606.02277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02277)
- BeTTER: 10 base tasks → 60 variations under causal interventions; GR00T-N1.6 follows 100% of spatial but 5% of semantic instructions; 52.5% drop on unseen subgoal compositions; SO-101 real checks — [arXiv 2604.18000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18000)
- LIBERO-VIFO: 8 visual-cue families, 1,347 cue instances on 40 LIBERO tasks; full-chain accuracy ≤17.6%; unauthorized cues induce actions in 32.2–60.1% of cases without language — [arXiv 2608.17600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17600)
- RoboSPA (EMNLP 2026): 56 base tasks × 5 levels on RoboTwin 2.0 (SAPIEN + CuRobo), 527K trajectories, five embodiments; every evaluated VLA stays below 25% at the highest level — [arXiv 2609.05324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05324); [GitHub](https://github.com/fanzhenxuan/RoboSPA)
- KinDER (RSS 2026): 25 procedurally generated environments isolating physical reasoning; bilevel planning 0.57, fine-tuned π0.5 0.32, Diffusion Policy 0.24 — [arXiv 2604.25788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25788)
- IMBench (RSS 2026 SemRob workshop): 35 robosuite tasks in 7 physical-intuition categories; after fine-tuning Diffusion Policy 0.24, π0.5 0.15, GR00T N1.5 0.02 — [arXiv 2607.15641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15641)
- INT-ACT's language-complexity and vision-language-thinking categories (section 1) and COIN's interactive reasoning (section 3) also belong here — [arXiv 2506.09930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09930); [arXiv 2604.16886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16886)

### Inferences
- Three test designs recur: (a) same scene, different instruction (LangGap, LIBERO-CF, InstructMove, ConflictVLA-Bench); (b) same task, reworded instruction (LIBERO-Para, LADEV, LIBERO-X level 5); (c) instruction requiring knowledge or inference (VLABench, SimplerEnv-Instruct, RoboSemanticBench, RoboIRGBench, RoboSPA).
- Designs of type (a) expose the largest gaps because standard LIBERO pairs each layout with one task, as LangGap states explicitly.

### Gaps
- VLABench has no venue on its arXiv record; ICCV 2025 comes from the GitHub README. No public leaderboard URL was found.
- InstructVLA (source of SimplerEnv-Instruct) and LIBERO-CF list no venue on arXiv.
- Excluded for not rolling out a low-level manipulation policy: ESI-Bench (VLM agents with high-level action primitives, arXiv 2605.18746). Excluded for evaluating general-purpose LLM agents rather than VLAs: LIBERO-Agent (arXiv 2609.39507), which nonetheless runs closed-loop low-level control and may suit a different category.
- A multilingual-instruction study (arXiv 2606.11906) was found in search but not opened.

## 6. Safety, physical plausibility, contact/force

### Takeaway
Twelve safety-oriented simulation benchmarks for VLAs appeared between Mar 2025 and Sep 2026, and they agree on one result: a large share of "successful" rollouts are unsafe (18–28% on LIBERO, 38–56% on RoboCasa-365, ~71% in SafeStage). ManiGuard-Bench is the only benchmark in this whole slice that reports a numeric sim-to-real correlation.

### Cited Findings
- SafeVLA-Bench: post-hoc safety evaluation wrapping LIBERO and RoboCasa-365 (MuJoCo); 58 tasks, 8 constraint families, ~18,000 episodes; 18 policies on LIBERO and 9 on RoboCasa-365; 15 LIBERO policies above 90% success still have 18–28% unsafe episodes; 38–56% of RoboCasa-365 successes violate a constraint; success rank weakly predicts safety rank (Kendall τ = +0.34); SO-101 pilot found 4 unsafe among 26 successes — [arXiv 2606.00773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00773)
- LIBERO-Safety (ECCV 2026): extends LIBERO-Plus; 75 tasks (5 suites × 5 tasks × 3 levels: affordance-aware grasping, human-robot interaction, tabletop avoidance, free-space hand/object avoidance, semantic safety reasoning); 19,664 collision-free demos; 8 VLAs plus 2 embodied foundation models; π0.5 best at 35–88%; simulation only — [arXiv 2606.23686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23686)
- SafeLIBERO (in VLSA/AEGIS, IROS 2026): 16 tasks, 32 obstacle scenarios, 1,600 episodes; π0.5 18.7% collision-avoidance rate, OpenVLA-OFT 15.1%, AEGIS 77.9%; two real Franka tasks — [arXiv 2512.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11891)
- Safety-CHORES (in SafeVLA, NeurIPS 2025 spotlight): AI2-THOR/ProcTHOR mobile manipulation; Safety-ObjNav, Safety-PickUp, Safety-Fetch; five safety-critical component types; 83.58% safety improvement for the proposed ISA; one real deployment on dual Realman arms — [arXiv 2503.03480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480)
- HazardArena: MuJoCo/VLABench; safe/unsafe twin scenarios in 7 hazard categories; safe-only fine-tuning raises unsafe execution with safe success (π0: 0.47 safe, 0.44 unsafe); real Franka, 100 trials per condition: 46.0% safe and 39.6% unsafe success, cut to 0.4% unsafe with refusal-augmented training — [arXiv 2604.12447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12447)
- ForesightSafety-VLA: RoboTwin-based; 66 scenarios, 13 categories including force/torque and thermal; OpenVLA-OFT safe success 0.42, RDT 0.30, DP 0.24, ACT 0.20; no baseline fully safe; simulation only — [arXiv 2606.27079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27079)
- SafeManip: temporal safety properties on 50 RoboCasa365 and 40 LIBERO tasks, 8 categories (collision/contact, grasp stability, release stability, cross-contamination, action onset, mechanism, containment, enclosure); 7 policies; capability and violations correlate positively on RoboCasa365 (r = +0.66) and negatively on LIBERO (r = −0.99) — [arXiv 2605.12386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12386)
- SafeStage: Isaac Sim, DROID embodiment; 97 scenarios over initial-state, execution-time and final-state hazards; π0.5, GR00T N1.7, DreamZero, Cosmos3 Nano Policy; 33.74% success vs 9.84% safe success — [arXiv 2609.21223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21223)
- OopsieVerse: DamageSim plugin for OmniGibson and RoboCasa; 32 tasks; mechanical, thermal and fluid damage; GR00T 8–92% completion but 0–8% safe completion on OmniGibson tasks; π0 17.5% / 0%; real Franka shelving and pouring (65% vs 5% safe completion) — [arXiv 2606.31993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31993)
- ManiGuard-Bench: Isaac Sim/OmniGibson; 200 base tasks → 1,000 scenarios; LTLf specifications; 8,000 demos; zero-shot safe success 0.0–1.8%, fine-tuned 7.5–29.8%; physical Franka replication with sim–real correlation r = 0.91 (success), 0.88 (safe success), 0.09 (violation rate) — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17386)
- SoftVTBench (ECCV 2026 workshop per arXiv comment): Isaac Sim FEM soft bodies with GelSight tactile sensing; 40 tasks; π0.5 vision-only 70.4% goal success but 21.4% safety success; visuo-tactile 71.8% / 35.6% — [arXiv 2607.04234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04234)
- BeSafe-Bench (BSB-EmbodiedVLA track): LIBERO via VLA-Arena; OpenVLA 49.81% success / 30% success-and-safe, OpenVLA-OFT 56.85 / 35.19%, π0.5 23.89 / 13.89% — [arXiv 2603.25747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25747)
- Related: VLA-Arena has four safety suites (StaticObstacles, CautiousGrasp, HazardAvoidance, StatePreservation) plus DynamicObstacles — [arXiv 2512.22539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22539); physical-parameter and contact stress in RoboTwin-Phys and RoboStress (section 1); security-style attacks in AttackVLA and PVEP (section 1)

### Inferences
- Safety benchmarks split into physical (collision, contact force, deformation, damage: SafeLIBERO, SafeVLA-Bench, SafeManip, OopsieVerse, SoftVTBench), semantic/contextual (HazardArena, LIBERO-Safety SSR, ConflictVLA-Bench, LIBERO-VIFO) and specification-based (SafeVLA-Bench STL, ManiGuard LTLf, SafeManip temporal properties).
- Contact and force are probed indirectly (deformation thresholds, contact events, damage models); no benchmark found evaluates VLAs on force-controlled assembly in simulation as a diagnostic.
- ManiGuard's near-zero correlation for violation rate (r = 0.09) alongside high correlation for success (r = 0.91) suggests simulated safety-violation frequencies should not be read as real-world frequencies.

### Gaps
- SafeVLA-Bench's use of Signal Temporal Logic comes from a search-result summary of the abstract rather than the extracted HTML.
- BeSafe-Bench: the size of the VLA track was not extracted; the nine listed risk categories apply to the whole agent benchmark.
- HazardArena and ForesightSafety-VLA list no code or project URL.
- Whether SoftVTBench's "ECCVW" acceptance refers to ECCV 2026 was inferred from the submission date (July 2026).

## 8. Real-robot experiments: sim-to-real method transfer, paired sim-vs-real with correlation, or none

### Takeaway
Of 81 benchmarks, 44 are simulation-only, 36 include some real-robot experiment without a correlation statistic, and exactly one (ManiGuard-Bench) reports a numeric sim–real correlation. The real experiments are mostly either the authors' method transferred to hardware, or a small replication of the stress condition on a few tasks with qualitative agreement.

### Cited Findings
- (ii) Paired sim-vs-real with a reported correlation: ManiGuard-Bench only — r = 0.91 for task success, 0.88 for safe success, 0.09 for violation rate, from 30 rollouts per cell on two task families with three fine-tuned policies; verdicts scored by a human operator — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17386)

### Inferences
- For the catalog's purposes, almost none of these benchmarks qualifies as a real-validated proxy in the sense of SIMPLER or REALM; they belong in a simulation-only category, with ManiGuard-Bench the one borderline case.
- Where real experiments exist, they usually cover 2–8 tasks and 10–60 trials per condition, enough to show a failure mode exists on hardware but not to validate ranking fidelity.
- DOMINO's real-robot check is of type (i): it shows the proposed method and dynamic pretraining transfer to a Piper, not that DOMINO scores predict real dynamic performance.

### Gaps
- The classification rests on extracted summaries of experiment sections; a paper could contain a correlation analysis in an appendix that the extraction did not surface.
- Counts (44 / 36 / 1) follow the `real_validation` field of the JSON, where SimplerEnv-Instruct is counted as having real experiments although those test the InstructVLA method rather than the benchmark.
